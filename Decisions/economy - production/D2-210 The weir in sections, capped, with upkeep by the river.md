# The weir in sections, capped, with upkeep by the river (D2-210)

*live · 16 Nov · spec: [[economy - production|economy/production]], [[environment - wildlife|environment/wildlife]]*

## The rule
(proposals/river_and_water.md §3b): one weir per water parcel; weirs on a stretch take in turn from upstream; built and upgraded in sections (a wattle first section as built, up to three timber sections, stone footing a section at a time; upgrading costs the difference); a night's catch is the share passing capped by what the weir holds (about 10 food a night for one section, 30 for a full weir); a year's take about 13% of a village of 15's food for one section and 40% for a full weir; wear per section by material x water type x the flow (from the catchment's rain until a river level exists) x the gap shut in high water x ice, so a full weir costs a quarter to a third of its building a year to keep; keeping the catch (smokehouses, salt, fuel) is the intended bottleneck. All values calibrate

## Where it came from
conversation (16 Nov: "yes that seems fair"; "a weir seems like quite high upkeep ... a lot more than a house"; "if you think the numbers above match that then it is fine")

## What it replaced
the weir of [[D2-209 Brief 5 (river and water) is built|D2-209]] as one work with no cap and a building's wear
