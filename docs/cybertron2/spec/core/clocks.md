# Clocks: which systems run on which time

Owner: 37.2 (the rule, D2-273); each system's owner keeps its system on its clock. Read this before deciding how long anything takes,
lasts, grows or keeps.

## The three clocks (D2-273)

| Clock | One step | Real against engine | Systems on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day** | a night; about 6 units of work a day, a unit 2 hours | real rates | work done in an hour (a craft's rate, a catch per hour), food eaten a night, walking and travel |
| **Year** | 32 days, seasons of 8 (D2-209) | real durations compressed about 1/11 | land and its yields, the seasons, harvests, the wild's production and regrowth, the runs, how long goods keep, process times measured in real days or weeks (smoking, retting, tanning, drying), the years a work takes to come into use (a withy bed, a coppice) |
| **Life** | a world's lifespan (D2-153; 64 days in current worlds) | a human life compressed to the lifespan | ageing, learning and practice (D2-267: set at a 64-day lifespan, scaled), raising children, births |

## The rules (D2-273)

- Every system runs on one clock, named in its spec.
- A duration of more than a few real days compresses with its clock; a duration of a day or two keeps its real nights as a floor
  (fresh fish or meat about 1 to 2 nights, milk the same day).
- All food runs on the year clock (what land and water yield, how long food keeps, how long it takes to keep it), so food sources and
  keeping are calibrated against each other.
- Rates per hour of work and food per night are never compressed.

## Open against this rule

- The building scale: works are built at about 1/30 to 1/70 of real hours while crafts take real hours (OPEN Q-84).
- Goods' shelf lives to be set on the year clock (proposals/hunt_fish_materials.md §7.3; a first table there).
