# Clocks: which systems run on which time

Owner: 37.2 (the rule, D2-273); each system's owner keeps its system on its clock. Read this before deciding how long anything takes,
lasts, grows or keeps, or what it costs to make.

## The three clocks (D2-273)

| Clock | One step | Real against engine | Systems on it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Day** | a night; a unit of work is 2 hours, 7 units a day in summer, 6 in autumn, 5 in winter (D2-275) | real rates | work done in an hour (a craft's rate, a catch per hour), food eaten a night, walking and travel, making what is used up in one go or a handful of uses |
| **Year** | 32 days, seasons of 8 (D2-209) | real durations compressed about 1/11; real work for durable things about 1/7 (D2-276) | land and its yields, the seasons, harvests, the wild's production and regrowth, the runs, how long goods keep, process times measured in real days or weeks (smoking, retting, tanning, drying), the years a work takes to come into use (a withy bed, a coppice), making durable things and their lives |
| **Life** | a world's lifespan (D2-153; 64 days in current worlds) | a human life compressed to the lifespan | ageing, learning and practice (D2-267: set at a 64-day lifespan, scaled), raising children, births |

## The rules

- Every system runs on one clock, named in its spec (D2-273).
- A duration of more than a few real days compresses with its clock; a duration of a day or two keeps its real nights as a floor
  (fresh fish or meat about 1 to 2 nights, milk the same day) (D2-273).
- All food runs on the year clock (what land and water yield, how long food keeps, how long it takes to keep it), so food sources and
  keeping are calibrated against each other (D2-273).
- Rates per hour of work and food per night are never compressed (D2-273).
- **Making things (D2-276):** what ends a thing's life decides its clock. What is used up in one go or a handful of uses (food, fuel,
  salt, bait, lights, seed, a snare line) costs real hours. Everything else is capital (buildings, works, gear, tools): its making
  costs real hours times about 1/7, a share of a year's work (about 384 engine hours a year against about 2,700 real), worked out from
  each world's own numbers as one named lever; its life is its real years counted as world years, or its real uses times the same
  factor. Materials stay real in quantity and rate; upkeep is priced with the thing. `calibrate`: the factor.
