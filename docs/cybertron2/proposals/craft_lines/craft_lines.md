## work (34)

### break a dam
- **does**: pull open a dam that floods a meadow
- **aware**: pulling open a beaver dam to drain a meadow
- **rough**: go where a beaver dam floods the meadow; anywhere else the work finds nothing to break, and the day's work is spent.
- **capable**: The meadow's grass comes back as the water runs off, but the pond's extra fish go with it.
- **known**: While the beavers live they build it again in time; trap them out at their lodge and the meadow stays dry, but they never come back.

### burn
- **does** (new): burn a dead beast on a pyre of firewood: everything on it burns, nothing comes to feed on it after, and the smoke is seen
- **aware**: burning a dead beast so nothing comes to it
- **rough**: name the dead beast; the wood comes from your hands or a store you may take from.
- **capable**: Meat, hide and tallow all burn: take off it what is worth keeping first. Burned, it no longer draws the beasts that eat the dead.
- **known**: Burying does the same work with digging in place of wood; a shallow grave can still be dug up by beasts that dig.

### burn the stubble
- **does** (new): burn off the stubble on a field: for some days after, slugs do less harm to a crop sown there, and some are killed
- **aware**: firing the stubble after harvest against slugs
- **rough**: it is done on a field, with nothing more needed; it does nothing against other pests.
- **capable**: Slugs eat young grain and beans in the first days after sowing: burn shortly before you sow, so the cover lasts into them.
- **known**: It is the field's one guard: picking slugs or other pest work there afterwards replaces it. It thins the village's slugs a little too.

### bury
- **does** (new): bury the dead in a grave you dig, or under a cairn of stones; the digging keeps between days, and others can dig with you
- **aware**: burying the dead in a grave
- **rough**: name the body; one day's digging may not finish the grave: come back, or others dig with you. Frozen ground takes longer.
- **capable**: Dug shallow it is quicker, but beasts may dig it up; a cairn of stones covers the body at once, or goes over a grave later.
- **known**: The dead left open draw beasts that feed on them; a deep grave or a cairn draws none. A dead beast is buried as a body is.

### care
- **does** (new): look after a child here for the day, feeding, minding and keeping it warm; you may give it food from your hands tonight, and a sentence to keep
- **aware**: minding, feeding and keeping a child warm
- **rough**: name the child, who must be here with you; food you mean to give must be in your hands, not in a store.
- **capable**: An infant not looked after each day may die of neglect, and a child unminded is at more risk; cold without a fire, or sleeping out, adds to it.
- **known**: At nightfall the carer offering most food feeds the child, so stay and keep it in hand. Your sentence becomes one of its kept lines.

### clear
- **does** (new): clear rough ground for a field: the work keeps between days and adds up across workers; when it is done the ground is a field, fallow and unbroken, and yours if nobody held it
- **aware**: turning meadow, pasture or scrub into a new field
- **rough**: Done on meadow, pasture or scrub with a tool in hand; bare hands go slowly. It takes many days before the ground is a field.
- **capable**: A better tool and practice clear more a day, and the tool wears. Others' work on the same ground adds to yours. Scrub takes far longer than grass.
- **known**: Cleared meadow starts richer than cleared scrub. Hoe or plough the new field before sowing. Clearing ground someone already holds makes it their field, not yours.

### clear for building
- **does** (new): clear open ground or scrub for building: the work keeps and adds up across workers; when it is done it is built ground, farmland no longer, and yours if nobody held it
- **aware**: making ground ready to build houses on
- **rough**: Done with a tool in hand on field, pasture, meadow or scrub; refused while a crop stands there. Bare hands go slowly.
- **capable**: A better tool and practice clear more a day, and the tool wears. Others' work adds to yours. Scrub, with its roots, takes far longer than grass.
- **known**: Built ground takes houses but never a crop or grazing again: its grass, crop and holdings end with the clearing. Clear only what you mean to build on.

### clear the leat
- **does** (new): clear the mill leat of silt and weed: a choked leat leaves the water mill grinding less from the same work until it is cleared
- **aware**: keeping a water mill's channel clear so it runs
- **rough**: Done at a water wheel; it needs no materials. The leat silts with time, not with use, so even an idle mill needs it.
- **capable**: Clearing it before it is choked is not wasted: it starts the reckoning afresh. Either a choked leat or worn millstones slows the grinding, so see to both.
- **known**: The leat silts by the year and the stones wear by the grain put through, so they fall due at different times: keep a reckoning of each.

### dig a strip
- **does** (new): break the turf of a strip of meadow or pasture you hold, so flax sown there takes as on a field; the meadow stays a meadow
- **aware**: breaking turf for a small flax patch
- **rough**: Only on meadow or pasture you or yours hold. Sow flax seed on it after; seed thrown on unbroken turf mostly comes to nothing.
- **capable**: Practice breaks more a day, up to the largest strip a holding may have; past that it is refused. Sow no more than a strip's worth of flax seed there.
- **known**: The dug turf waits for the seed and is used up by the sowing. Flax sown thicker than a strip becomes the main crop, and takes the turf's loss.

### ditch
- **does**: ditch and bank wet ground: crew work, anyone may dig, led by one who knows how
- **aware**: draining wet ground so a field bears fully
- **rough**: Only on wet ground. A day's digging holds only if someone who knows how works on the ditches that same day; otherwise it is lost.
- **capable**: A tool and practice dig more, and the tool wears. The more of the field ditched, the nearer it bears to good ground. Finished ditches want mending each season.
- **known**: Unmended through a season, finished ditches fall in by a share and must be dug again. Once whole and kept for the season, more work is refused.

### dress millstones
- **does** (new): recut the furrows of worn millstones: worn stones grind less from the same work until they are dressed
- **aware**: recutting millstones so they grind well again
- **rough**: Done at the millstones; it needs no materials. They wear by the grain put through them, so a busy mill needs it sooner.
- **capable**: Dressing stones not yet worn is not wasted: it starts their wear afresh. At a water mill a choked leat slows the grinding too, whatever the stones.
- **known**: Worn stones take the full work for a lesser grist, so every day ground on them is part lost: dress them before a heavy grinding, not after.

### fence
- **does** (new): put a fence round a parcel, timber going in with the work; when it is finished, with its gate shut, it keeps beasts in and out
- **aware**: a fence round a field holding beasts in or out
- **rough**: Needs timber to hand as you work; a tool helps. It holds nothing until finished, and where a wall stands it is refused.
- **capable**: Anyone's work counts and adds up over days. Once standing, work on it is mending: unmended through a year it grows gappy, then falls, and gappy lets beasts through.
- **known**: A fallen fence is rebuilt from what still stands, so mend it before it goes. It keeps out large raiders by night, but small beasts slip through any fence.

### guard the field
- **does**: sit up by a field all night to scare beasts off, with a fire, clappers, a horn or the dog; you lose the night's sleep
- **aware**: sitting up all night to keep beasts off crops
- **rough**: Needs something to scare with: wood for a fire, clappers or a horn to hand, or a dog at home. You will be tired tomorrow.
- **capable**: Name the scare, else the first you have is used, and a fire burns its wood. A watch lasts one night; hungry or bold beasts turn less.
- **known**: Some beasts grow used to a scare used night after night, so change it. Watching the gap they come in by turns them sooner. Herded beasts count as watched.

### herd
- **does** (new): watch a herd on its grazing for the day: herded beasts do not wander, and a herder holds far more head together than beasts left alone
- **aware**: watching the beasts so they keep together
- **rough**: Done where the beasts are. Unnamed, it is yours or your household's; name another's herd to watch it. Once watched enough today, it is refused.
- **capable**: Left alone a big herd breaks into groups that stray into crops and onto the roads; each herder holds a good number together, so a large herd wants more.
- **known**: A herd watched by day also counts as watched when raiders come for it that night. A ripe crop near pulls even fed beasts far: herd them then.

### hoe
- **does** (new): break a field's soil with a hoe, turning up stones: broken ground sown bears more; over a standing crop it turns the crop under
- **aware**: breaking up field soil by hand for sowing
- **rough**: Done on a field, best with a tool in hand. Hoeing where a crop stands turns it under, ripe or green, and it is gone.
- **capable**: A better tool and practice break more, and the tool wears. Break unsown ground and sow it: broken soil bears more. The stones turned up are yours.
- **known**: Turning under a failed or unwanted crop frees the ground for seed and feeds the soil a little, the more the further it grew. A plough breaks far more.

### manure
- **does** (new): spread dung on a field's plots: the soil comes back toward its best, the more the poorer it is, never past what that ground can be
- **aware**: spreading dung to feed tired soil
- **rough**: Done on a field, with dung to hand; gather it where beasts stand. Soil already good gains little from it.
- **capable**: Practice covers more ground each go. On a large holding each go lifts the whole soil less, so spread often. Beasts in stalls keep more dung for you.
- **known**: Poor land stays poor: dung lifts a soil only toward what that ground can be. Beans after grain, or a rest, restore it too; dung serves ground you cannot rest.

### mend the brine pan
- **does** (new): patch and reset a burnt-through brine pan: a worn pan boils less salt from the same work until it is mended
- **aware**: patching the salt pan when it burns through
- **rough**: Done at the brine pan, with a little copper in your own hands; copper in a store is not reached. It wears with each boiling.
- **capable**: Mending a pan not yet burnt through is not wasted: it starts its wear afresh. Count your boilings and mend it before it fails.
- **known**: Keep copper by you for it: a worn pan takes the full work for less salt, so every boiling on it is part lost.

### milk
- **does** (new): milk the cows in milk of a herd: the milk is yours, and those one milker leaves, another may milk the same day
- **aware**: drawing milk from cows that have calved
- **rough**: Only where a cow in milk is; unnamed, only your own or your household's herd. Milk does not keep long.
- **capable**: Practice draws more from each cow. A cow comes into milk when she calves, which needs a bull with fed cows; she dries off with time, faster when hungry.
- **known**: Milk spoils fast: drink it, or make cheese with salt. Kill a cow in milk and her milk goes with her; a hungry winter dries the herd.

### open the gate
- **does** (new): open a gate in a fence and leave it standing open: beasts pass freely in and out until it is shut, and it is noticed
- **aware**: letting beasts through a fence
- **rough**: Only where a fence stands whole enough to have a gate, and the gate is shut. It takes no work.
- **capable**: An open gate lets a herd wander through as if no fence stood, in or out, until someone shuts it.
- **known**: Who left it open is marked with the gate, and the land's holders are concerned in it: open another's gate only with cause.

### pest work
- **does**: go over a crop and take off what is eating it; name the pest with "looking for", else the worst there
- **aware**: picking pests off a growing crop
- **rough**: Done on a growing crop, or on meadow grass for moles. Name the pest, else the worst there is taken.
- **capable**: Slugs come out at dusk: a tallow light in hand lets you work on into the dark and take more, and burns down. The good lasts only some days.
- **known**: The grey spiny grubs on beans are ladybird young that eat the fly: name them and you kill your helpers. Beasts are no pests: watch the field for those.

### plough
- **does** (new): plough a field with a yoked pair of oxen, a plough and harness: it breaks far more ground than a hoe, turns up stones, and turns under any standing crop
- **aware**: breaking a field with oxen and a plough
- **rough**: Needs a yoked pair of oxen you may use here, a wooden plough and harness to hand. Ploughing a sown field turns its crop under.
- **capable**: Practice breaks more a day. The plough wears by the ground broken, the harness by each day's work; others' oxen may be used, but it is seen.
- **known**: Plough unsown ground before sowing: broken soil bears more. A spare harness and plough keep the team working when one wears out; harness is made from timber and hide.

### pull flax
- **does** (new): pull a ripe strip of flax by the root: you get its straw, with seed rippled from it, and the strip is done when all is pulled
- **aware**: harvesting flax by pulling it up whole
- **rough**: Only a ripe strip of flax of yours or your household's; an unripe one is refused.
- **capable**: Practice pulls more a day. Flax left standing past ripeness rots. Pulling the whole strip lets its ground's soil take its step.
- **known**: Ret the straw before it is spun. Keep back the rippled seed for next year's strip; sown thicker than a strip, flax becomes a field's main crop.

### reap
- **does** (new): reap a standing crop: ripe, it gives its grain or beans, with straw for fodder from grain and rye; cut green, it gives green fodder
- **aware**: cutting a ripe crop to bring in the harvest
- **rough**: Done where a crop stands, best with a tool in hand. Cut before it is ripe and you get green fodder, little food.
- **capable**: A better tool and practice cut more a day, and the tool wears. Weeding beforehand raises what it gives. Left past its time it rots night by night.
- **known**: A crop is nobody's until reaped: whoever reaps another's plots takes the grain, and is seen. A blighted crop gives nothing. Keep grain from vermin.

### reline oven
- **does** (new): reline a cracked stone oven: a cracked oven bakes less from the same work until it is relined
- **aware**: mending a cracked oven so it bakes well
- **rough**: Done at the oven, with stone in your own hands; stone in the store is not reached. It cracks with use, a little each baking.
- **capable**: Relining an oven not yet cracked is not wasted: it starts its wear afresh. Count the bakings and reline it before it fails.
- **known**: A cracked oven takes the full work for less bread, so reline it first. A hoe or plough turns up stone from the fields.

### repair
- **does** (new): mend a building's damage: its stuff goes in when the mending starts, then the work, until it is a step sounder; a sound building is refused
- **aware**: mending a damaged house or building
- **rough**: Needs its stuff, timber or stone as it is built, to hand or in its store. Mending stone is walling, which you must know.
- **capable**: The work keeps between days until done; practice does more each time. A worn house lets in damp, a holed one lets foxes and rats in, shut or not.
- **known**: Storms, frost and neglect wear buildings, and unmended damage worsens: mend early. A weir is mended a section at a time.

### select seed
- **does** (new): go through a ripe crop marking the best ears for seed: once you know it fully, the crops you sow ripen sooner
- **aware**: choosing the best ears for next year's seed
- **rough**: Needs a ripe crop in a field; once you have been through a crop, going through it again is refused.
- **capable**: It is the knowing that pays, not the marking: practise on every ripe crop you can until you know it fully.
- **known**: A sooner crop can beat the frost or let another sowing into the year. The marked ears change nothing; it is your own sowings that gain.

### shut
- **does**: shut up your house, store and hens for the night
- **aware**: shutting up house, store and hens against night raiders
- **rough**: Done on built ground, for tonight only; it shuts every house of yours that has a store. Do it each evening.
- **capable**: A shut door keeps out most of what comes for hens or grain; left open, much gets in. A dog at home turns raiders too.
- **known**: Shutting is no help to a holed house: beasts small enough come through its gaps anyway, so mend the walls. A cat in the store keeps vermin down.

### shut the gate
- **does** (new): shut a gate in a fence: the fence holds beasts in, and keeps them out, as well as it stands
- **aware**: closing a field gate to keep beasts in
- **rough**: Only where a fence stands and its gate is open. It takes no work.
- **capable**: A shut gate holds as well as its fence: a gappy fence still lets some through, shut or not.
- **known**: Beasts that strayed come home at dusk only at the fence's odds while the gate round home is shut; a hungry herd stays out after food.

### slaughter
- **does** (new): kill a beast of a herd: its meat, hide and tallow come to you, in your hands or the store you work for, and the herd is one fewer
- **aware**: killing a beast for meat, hide and tallow
- **rough**: Say which beast, cow, ox, bull or calf, or which herd; unnamed it is refused. Name another's herd and you kill theirs, seen.
- **capable**: Named by herd alone, a calf goes first. Fresh meat spoils fast: salt, smoke or dry it soon, or eat it. A cow in milk takes her milk with her.
- **known**: Hide makes harness, packs and bedding; tallow makes lights for night work. Keep the cows and a bull: without them the herd bears no calves.

### sow
- **does** (new): sow seed broadcast on your plots of a field, then the unclaimed plots: it grows into a crop to weed and reap
- **aware**: throwing seed on ground to grow a crop
- **rough**: Say which seed. Ground with another crop standing must be reaped or turned under first. Seed thrown on unbroken turf mostly comes to nothing.
- **capable**: Sow on hoed or ploughed ground for more; tired soil gives less. The same crop twice running fails more often. Seed in cold ground lies, a little lost each night.
- **known**: A master sower's crops bear more. Beans after grain rest the soil. A small sowing of flax seed goes into a strip beside the main crop.

### tend the smoke
- **does** (new): feed and bank a smokehouse fire for the night: only a tended night counts toward what hangs there; untended, the fire dies and what hangs spoils
- **aware**: keeping a smokehouse fire going overnight
- **rough**: Only where something hangs smoking, once a day. Firewood must be there: with too little for the night the fire dies anyway.
- **capable**: Firewood in your hands goes on if the store is short. Tend it every day until the lot is done, then take it down.
- **known**: Each night it burns, the fire may catch the smokehouse and what hangs in it, a wattle one most: build it of timber or stone, apart from the houses.

### terrace
- **does**: terrace hill ground: crew work, anyone may dig, led by one who knows how
- **aware**: building terraces so hill ground bears fully
- **rough**: Only on hill ground, with stone to hand. A day's work holds only if someone who knows how works on it that day.
- **capable**: A tool and practice do more, and the tool wears. The more terraced, the nearer the field bears to good ground. Finished terraces want mending each season, and stone.
- **known**: Unmended through a season, finished terraces fall in by a share. Stones a hoe or plough turns up serve. Once whole and kept for the season, more work is refused.

### wall
- **does**: wall a parcel round in dry stone: crew work, anyone may work on it, led by one who knows how
- **aware**: a dry-stone wall round a field, holding beasts best
- **rough**: Needs stone to hand, going in with the work. A day's work holds only if someone who knows walling works on it that day.
- **capable**: A tool and practice lay more, and the tool wears. A finished wall holds beasts far better than a fence, keeps most raiders out, and does not rot.
- **known**: Walling is the mason's craft too: building or mending in stone needs it. Where a fence stands the wall is refused. A finished wall wants no yearly mending.

### weed
- **does** (new): weed a growing crop: a cleaner crop bears more when reaped, up to as clean as weeding makes it
- **aware**: pulling weeds so a crop bears more
- **rough**: Done on a growing crop; it needs no tool. Weeding a ripe crop does it no good, and the day is wasted.
- **capable**: Practice weeds more ground each go. Each weeding gains less than the last, and a large crop takes many goes to come clean.
- **known**: Past clean it gains nothing: stop and spend the day elsewhere. A crop withered by blight is refused; a strip beside the main crop is never weeded.

## build (25)

### anvil
- **does** (new): fit an anvil and bellows in a building with a hearth: a smithy, where copper is smelted and worked into tools, hooks and coin
- **aware**: a smith's anvil and bellows, for working copper
- **rough**: it goes on the ground floor of a building that already has a hearth; a try that goes wrong spoils the stone and hide
- **capable**: the hearth is a mason's work, so a smithy wants a mason before a smith; once begun, carry the work on over days with no more materials
- **known**: it never wears out, unlike a brine pan or an oven; those who cannot get into the building cannot smith there, so its holder decides who works it

### beds
- **does** (new): put beds in a house: whoever sleeps in a bed is housed; one without a bed lies on the floor, as in a damaged house
- **aware**: beds, so people sleep housed, not on the floor
- **rough**: in a finished building with free floor, each bed taking floor of its own; a try gone wrong spoils the timber and straw
- **capable**: ask for several at once and as many go in as the free floor takes; two children share a bed, and a baby sleeps in its mother's
- **known**: household members take the beds at home first, eldest first; guests need a bed free; a building with beds counts as a house, and none go in a smokehouse

### bins
- **does** (new): fit bins in a building: grain, meal, salt, nuts and cured goods kept off the damp floor, and less of them taken by rats and mice
- **aware**: bins to keep dry goods off the damp floor
- **rough**: dry and cured goods only, not fresh meat, fish or roots; in a finished building with free floor; a bad try spoils the timber
- **capable**: goods are put where they keep best without your sorting; salted fish and meat do not go in bins, but in a salting tub or barrel
- **known**: bins keep off the damp, less well the mice: a chest guards better; in a gappy or holed building the store spoils faster, and failing, as the open

### brine pan
- **does** (new): fit a brine pan over a furnace in a building at or beside a brine spring: salt is boiled there from the brine; it burns through with use and is mended
- **aware**: a salter's pan for boiling brine into salt
- **rough**: it goes only in a building at or beside a brine spring, on the ground floor; a try gone wrong spoils its copper and stone
- **capable**: each boiling wears it; worn, it boils half as much for the same work until mended, with a little copper, by anyone who can get in
- **known**: boiling is seen and smelt from about, so others know when salt is made; a worn pan wastes the same work on half the salt, so mend it first

### cairn
- **does** (new): raise a cairn of stones: a monument with no room, on which lines can be painted or cut, read by those who attend to it
- **aware**: a heap of stones raised to mark a place
- **rough**: it stands anywhere but in water, taking a little building ground where there is some; a cairn over a grave is burying, not this
- **capable**: begun, it is raised over days with no more stone; built finely, richly or grandly it costs more stone and work and is seen from further off
- **known**: lines are painted, or cut with a tool in hand, and cutting into its stone needs a mason's walling; describe it in your words as you raise it

### camp
- **does** (new): put up a camp anywhere but on water: a lean-to of branches round a fire ring, with beds, shelter and a fire away from a house; it keeps little and does not last
- **aware**: a lean-to and fire, to sleep sheltered away from home
- **rough**: anywhere but on water, with room if on building ground; it takes no store or hearth, and keeps only a little food or fuel
- **capable**: its fire burns fuel kept in it or piled at its side; a downpour or gale can put it out, less often for the ring, and the night turns cold
- **known**: only partly housed, worse than a house for a birth; no room is added and nothing written on it; it sags before it falls: build again then

### carved post
- **does** (new): set up a carved post: a timber monument with no room, on which lines are painted or carved, read by those who attend to it
- **aware**: a carved wooden post set up to mark a place
- **rough**: anywhere but in water; on building ground it needs a free space; the timber comes from your hands, then a store you may draw on
- **capable**: begun, it goes up over days with no more timber; built finely, richly or grandly, it takes more timber and work and is noticed further off
- **known**: carving a line into it needs a tool in your hands, and wears it; painting needs none; the post weathers and breaks like any timber building

### cellar
- **does** (new): dig a cellar under a building: food kept cool below the floor, out of the summer's heat and the frost; grain, meal, salt and cured goods take its damp
- **aware**: a cool store dug under the floor for food
- **rough**: a mason's work under a finished building's ground floor; dry goods spoil in its damp, so keep them in bins or on a granary floor
- **capable**: it takes no floor and grows with the ground-floor rooms above it; one who knows stone walling can make it without having learned cellars
- **known**: the summer does not hasten fish, meat, milk, cheese or eggs there, nor frost the roots; cheese is made at one; vermin reach it as the open floor

### fire ring
- **does** (new): set a ring of stones for a fire: warmth and cooking where people sleep, burning fuel from the store or the woodpile at the door, and a rack to dry meat and fish
- **aware**: a ring of stones for an open fire
- **rough**: in a finished building with free floor; its fire is lit only when people sleep there, with fuel in the store or at the door
- **capable**: on cold nights it half-warms a house at most; a lit fire cooks the food; with no hearth its drying rack hangs outside and dries only on dry nights
- **known**: in a camp it keeps the fire in better through downpour and gale; unlike a hearth it needs no mason, but the larger the room, the less it warms

### granary
- **does** (new): lay a granary floor: grain, rye, oats and beans kept raised and dry, off the damp, with less taken by rats and mice than on the open floor
- **aware**: a raised floor to keep grain dry
- **rough**: grain, rye, oats and beans only, in a finished building with floor enough free; a try gone wrong spoils the timber
- **capable**: goods go where they keep best, so grain fills any bins first, as vermin reach them less, and the granary floor takes the rest
- **known**: an owl hole in a store building nobody sleeps in may draw a barn owl against the mice; grain in a cellar takes its damp, so keep it out

### hall
- **does** (new): build a hall: the greatest room, floor for many beds, a hearth, stores and fittings, and the most places for lines on its walls
- **aware**: the great room of a house, for many people
- **rough**: Timber on a stone footing, or stone, never wattle; stone is a mason's walling. All the materials go in first: short of one, nothing begins.
- **capable**: It needs free building ground, and a hall wants much of it: clear more first. The work runs over many days, more an act with skill; hungry, you do less.
- **known**: Added to a standing house it costs a quarter less. Finished, it is bare floor until fitted. Stone wears least in weather and holds a break-in off longest.

### hand mill
- **does** (new): build a hand mill: a quern in a house, where grain is ground by hand into meal for porridge, not bread
- **aware**: a quern for grinding grain by hand
- **rough**: Fitted inside a finished building, taking a little floor; name it, else it goes in your home here. With no free floor it is refused.
- **capable**: Grinding at it takes little grain an act, and its meal makes porridge only: bread wants flour from millstones or a water mill.
- **known**: It never wears, as millstones do, so it serves for small grindings even where a mill stands. A spoilt start loses its stone.

### hearth
- **does** (new): build a hearth: a fire in the house that warms it at night, cooks the household's raw food, bakes bread and dries meat or fish on its rack
- **aware**: a fire in a house, for warmth and cooking
- **rough**: A mason's work in stone, fitted inside a building, one to a building; never in a smokehouse or a camp.
- **capable**: It warms only so much floor, so a large house is part cold. It burns the house's store, then the woodpile at the door; with neither, it stays cold.
- **known**: A cold night burns several times a mild one's wood: keep a woodshed full. A smoke loft hangs above it, and an anvil needs one beside it.

### medium room
- **does** (new): build a medium room: floor for a household's beds, hearth and stores, and places for a few lines on its walls
- **aware**: a middling room for a household's house
- **rough**: Timber on a stone footing, or stone; too large for wattle. In timber it is a carpenter's work, in stone a mason's walling.
- **capable**: Built onto a house here it costs a quarter less and needs ground beside it; a new house needs free building ground. More skill, more work done an act.
- **known**: In a stone house it can go on an upper storey, taking no ground, never larger than the floor below. A wattle house must be rebuilt in timber first.

### millstones
- **does** (new): build dressed millstones: an ox mill, where an ox turns the stones and grain is ground to flour for bread, in large batches
- **aware**: great stones turned by an ox to grind flour
- **rough**: A mason's work and heavy: it goes on the ground floor of a finished building with floor to spare. Milling there needs an ox.
- **capable**: Grinding wears the stones: past their life they grind half as much until dressed again. A water wheel can turn them in place of the ox.
- **known**: Dress them as soon as they grind slow. Beside a river, add a water wheel: it grinds far more an act and needs no ox.

### oven
- **does** (new): build a stone oven: bread baked in great batches, a firing at a time, far more than a hearth bakes
- **aware**: a stone oven for baking bread in batches
- **rough**: A mason's work, fitted only in a stone building, taking some floor; in wattle or timber it is refused.
- **capable**: Each firing takes a full batch of flour, not meal, and firewood. After some firings it cracks and bakes half a batch until relined.
- **known**: A firing wants its whole batch of flour to hand, so it pays only where a mill keeps flour coming. Keep stone in hand to reline it.

### owl hole
- **does** (new): build an owl hole: a gap high in the gable where a barn owl may come to nest; nesting there, it keeps down the mice and rats in that building's store
- **aware**: a nesting gap for a barn owl, against mice
- **rough**: Cut into a finished building, taking no floor. No owl comes to a building where people sleep.
- **capable**: An owl takes the hole only at the turn of spring, and not every year. Once in, it stops more mice than rats in that one store.
- **known**: Best in a barn or granary nobody sleeps in, and it guards that building only. A dog or a cat does more against rats.

### small room
- **does** (new): build a small room: the least house, floor for a few beds, a hearth and bins, in wattle, timber or stone
- **aware**: the smallest room: a hut, or a house's first room
- **rough**: Wattle wants only timber and withies; stone is a mason's walling. It goes on built ground or scrub, never on field, pasture or wood.
- **capable**: The materials go in at the start, the work over days, more an act with skill. Finished, it is bare floor: beds, a hearth and bins make it a house.
- **known**: Wattle wears fastest in weather but mends for work alone; timber and stone wear slower and mend with their own stuff. Stone holds off a break-in longest.

### smoke racks
- **does** (new): build smoke racks: hurdles over a smouldering fire in a building of their own, where fish and meat are hung to smoke and keep
- **aware**: racks for smoking fish and meat
- **rough**: They go in a building with no beds or hearth, leaving floor free to work the fire; in a house they are refused.
- **capable**: More sets hang more, as the floor allows. Tend the fire daily, fed from the store or the woodpile at the door, or the hanging waits, going off as fresh.
- **known**: A hard cure wants a timber or stone smokehouse. Its fire can catch and spread to buildings on the same ground: keep it apart from houses, and not wattle.

### stalls
- **does** (new): build stalls: beasts housed through the winter, one a stall, eating less fodder, their dung kept on the ground, and not driven off past a barred door
- **aware**: stalls to house beasts through the winter
- **rough**: Built in a finished building on the ground where the herd stands, one held by the herd's people; each stall takes floor, ground floor only.
- **capable**: Only the share of the herd with a stall is housed, so build one a head. They count in winter alone, and not in a ruined building.
- **known**: Stalls on other ground than the herd do nothing: bring the herd to the byre's ground for winter. The dung kept there can be gathered for the fields.

### standing stone
- **does** (new): raise a standing stone: a monument with no room, on whose faces lines can be painted, carved or cut for those who come to it
- **aware**: a great stone set up as a monument
- **rough**: A mason's work, raised anywhere but in water; on building ground it takes a little of it. Its stone is all taken first.
- **capable**: Raised fine, rich or grand it costs far more stone and work, and is noticed further off. Cutting a line into it needs a mason and a tool.
- **known**: Its lines are read by those who work or speak at it. A line cut in stone costs more work to change or erase than paint.

### vault
- **does** (new): build a vault: a stone room of its own on the ground floor, where what is kept is safe from fire
- **aware**: a fireproof stone room for what must not burn
- **rough**: A mason's work: it needs a finished building and free building ground for its own room. All its stone is taken at the start.
- **capable**: It holds little for its cost, so keep there what a fire would ruin; the household's book can say what is kept in it.
- **known**: A fire takes the rest of the store but not the vault. It does not keep out rats and mice, nor the damp that spoils grain and cured goods.

### water wheel
- **does** (new): build a water wheel and leat: a water mill, where the river turns the millstones and grain is ground to flour in the largest batches
- **aware**: a mill wheel turned by the river
- **rough**: A carpenter's work, set in a building by water that already has dressed millstones; without the stones, or away from water, it is refused.
- **capable**: It wants copper and much timber and stone, and many days' work. The leat silts up within the year: then it grinds half until the leat is cleared.
- **known**: Where fish run, shut hatches grind at full but hold a run back below the dam; open, they grind half. Held fish are easier taken by hand.

### weir
- **does** (new): build a fish weir across a river with a crew: stakes and wattle that turn the fish runs into wicker traps in its gap, which fill by night
- **aware**: stakes across a river that trap fish as they run
- **rough**: Crew work across a river: name who builds it with you. One weir to a place; its first section is wattle unless you say timber.
- **capable**: Lift the catch often: full traps take no more. Shut, its gap takes the runs; open, very little. Floods and ice wear it, a shut gap in high water most.
- **known**: Add sections and stone footings to take more and wear less; timber outlasts wattle. Mend it a section at a time. Taking from another crew's weir is theft, and seen.

### woodshed
- **does** (new): build a woodshed: a lean-to against a building where a great stack of timber, firewood and charcoal is kept off the house's floor
- **aware**: a lean-to for stacking fuel and timber
- **rough**: Put against a finished building, taking ground beside it, not floor; with no free building ground there it is refused.
- **capable**: It holds only timber, firewood and charcoal. It is part of the house's store, so the hearth burns from it first.
- **known**: Without one, a winter's fuel crowds the floor and spills outside the door, where it still burns but lies open to anyone.
