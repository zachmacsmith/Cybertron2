# Fishing, hunting and their materials, developed together

**Status:** discussion (21 Nov 2026). Nothing decided, nothing built.
**Owner:** 37.2 (the wild, the water, the materials, the worlds); 37.4 for the core of senses, act results, wear and clocks.
**Thread:** fishing, hunting and their materials developed together (W-73), under the nutrition worlds (W-69).

## Summary

One design for how people take fish, game and fowl, what their gear is made of, what it costs to make and keep, what they can
sense of the water and the wood, and what each method yields, calibrated together on the shared basis (D2-258, D2-259). It gathers
the signs of the river (W-72, proposals/river_signs.md), the fishing bugs the live runs exposed (Q-79), the hunting and resources
build that was benched (L-111), the shallows stock Zach asked for on 16 Nov, and fowling split from fish nets. The research is in
`docs/review/37_growth/02zzzh_hunt_fish/` (five notes: fishing_ladder, hunting_ladder, fibre_and_cord, withies_wood_materials,
engine_audit).

The shape is **many short ladders, not one ladder of rungs** (Zach, 15 Nov: "having 4 ladder rungs is a terrible idea"): every
method works with nothing at a low take; each piece of gear multiplies one factor of the take by about 2 to 5; a few switches of
paradigm (the crew's net, the fixed work, the managed bed, keeping beasts) change what a village is.

## The proposal

### 1. The compressed year, first

Both ladders found the same fault. Breeding and regrowth run on the 32-day year (D2-209), but each person eats every day. So a
wood's game feeds about 365/32 ≈ 11 times as many mouths a year as a real one: a good temperate wood gives 50 to 120 food a km² a
year from its big game, which in the engine feeds 30 to 75 people's whole diet off a village's ring of about 20 km², against 3 to 7
in life (hunting_ladder §numbers; it explains brief 4c's master holding 2 to 3 food a unit for 16 days on one wood). Resident fish
are the same (fishing_ladder, question 2). The runs were already put on the real year by a named factor of 32/365 (D2-258).

**Proposed:** one rule for every wild stock that renews by a year's production (game, resident fish, the shallows, wild plants):
the production per day of the compressed year is the real day's (the real year's ÷ 365), not the real year's ÷ 32, as the runs
are; a departure is named and kept as a lever (D2-259). 37.4 to confirm the reading of the code. This is the largest single
change to what wild living yields, and it should be decided before anything is calibrated.

### 2. Materials: two goods and a crop

- **withies** (one good, willow and hazel rods; a bundle of about 10 kg green, weight 1): cut at a managed wood or a withy bed
  (about 4 bundles a unit of work) or from wild willow on wet parcels (about 1.5), best in winter. The **withy bed** is a work on
  a parcel (about 200 m², the cuttings and about 10 units), giving nothing the first world year, half the second, about 30 bundles
  a year from the third (withies_wood_materials).
- **cord** (one good: cord, twine, line, light rope): **twist cord** from wild nettle or lime and willow bast, basic, slow and
  seasonal (about 0.04 cord a unit; 35 to 80 hours a kg in life); or from **flax**, a crop on the farming verbs (about 8 kg of
  straw to a kg of fibre), retted in water (1 to 2 nights, fouling the parcel for fish and beasts) or on grass (3 to 4 nights,
  no fouling), then **dressed and spun** (about 3 times cheaper than wild cord) (fibre_and_cord).
- **Folded in:** poles into timber (a managed wood's young growth yields timber as poles, which also mends the "cut poles"
  alias); gorse into firewood.
- **From the kill:** a **butchering** act with skill (a novice loses 10 to 20%), giving meat, a hide (rawhide for gear, tanning
  later), tallow from fat beasts, and **bone and antler** as one good for hooks and points. Fish keep arriving gutted until the
  salting rate (Q-73) decides whether gutting is its own work.
- **Left out for now:** bark and tanning, pitch and tar (until boats), glue, wax, reeds and sedge, sinew and horsehair, feathers
  (until the bow), bird lime unless fowling needs it.

### 3. Gear as things that are made, set, lifted and worn

Today snares, wicker traps and nets cost nothing and leave nothing (engine_audit, seam 1). Proposed:

| Gear | Made from (engine units) | Life | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|
| wicker trap | 0.5 withies, 3 units | about two world years; spates still wreck it | set gear |
| snare line (5 nooses) | 0.02 cord, 1 unit | gear with a life | snare |
| fishing line and rod | 0.1 withies, 0.01 cord | uses that run down | line fishing |
| fish net (gill or dip) | 1 cord, about 25 to 30 units | 60 to 80 uses, renewed by barking later | edge fishing, drawing a net |
| fowling net | 0.8 cord, about 30 units | as the fish net | fowling |
| seine | about 10 kg cord, about 200 units, a crew | later, with lake worlds and boats | drawing a net |
| weir section, wattle | 12 withies, 1 timber, 16 units (first section's base 4 timber, 2 withies, 8 units) | D2-210's wear | the works |
| smoke racks | 2 withies, 1 timber | | keeping |

- **Set and lift are two acts.** "Set traps" places traps one holds (a line of several per act, as a snare line lays 5: real work
  sets 6 to 10 a unit); "lift traps" takes what is in all of one's own traps at a place. This mends the set-for-lift confusion in
  about 40% of trap acts (Q-79; 02zzy2).
- **Wear reaches use.** Weapons and rods wear when used (D2-46; today only ploughs and hoes wear, engine_audit seam 2).
- **Existing worlds:** households in fishing and trapping worlds are seeded with the gear their arm or archetype holds.

### 4. Methods: general, with gear deciding what they reach (D2-204)

**Water, three stocks and the runs:** the **shallows** (small fish, crayfish, eels at the edge; small, stripped in days), the
**open water** (the body's resident stock, D2-229), the **runs** (as built), kept fish later.

| Method | Reaches | Gear that raises it |
|---|---|---|
| fishing at the edge (today's fish by hand) | the shallows; a run where it passes a shallows or a pinch point | spear, dip net |
| line fishing (today's angle) | the open water | rod, hand line, better hooks; its rate lowered to about real (0.1 to 0.3 kg an hour) or the factor named |
| set gear (today's trap in water, plus set lines) | runs and the edge (traps), the open water (set lines) | the trap, the line |
| drawing a net | open water and runs, by crew | fish net, seine |
| the works | runs (weir, race) | sections, the gap |

**Land and air:** keep hunt the wild, stalk, drive, snare, den work and gather young (widened to eggs); gear carries the ladder
(spear now; the bow, dogs and ferrets later). **Fowling** becomes its own method (nets and decoys at the water, best in winter
and at dusk), and the bird net stops meeting salmon: birds get a `flies` trait and fish nets stay with the water's methods.
Wildfowl numbers on wet ground checked against real winter counts (refuges 400 to 1,500 a km²; a wet district 50 to 300).

### 5. Signs, results and lore

- The river: proposals/river_signs.md (W-72) as written.
- The wood and the fowl (engine_audit, hunting signs): presence signs for the hare, roe, woodpigeon, wildfowl, fox and otter at
  the village; the fowl arriving, leaving and flighting; the deer gone from a wood; "hunted too often here" recalibrated (it fires
  on the first deer, names kinds not met, and its expert line reads short-lived pressure); "came on nothing" saying whether the
  game is thin or shy, the choice D2-255's longer trips turn on.
- Results report the catch separately from what the water or wood held, and name losses.
- Lore and the trade line by livelihood (L-141, with the archetypes, W-70): a fisher's year, a fowler's winter, a hunter's
  seasons, the scale of each method, at the length farmers get; the farmers' own lore line mended (it is cut at the 25-word cap).

### 6. The order the yields should keep (calibration targets)

Against plough farming at 2.4 to 2.8 food a unit: hands and rod well below it (0.1 to 0.5); gear fishing and snares at or below
it (0.4 to 2); run nights at traps or a weir far above it (5 to 18) for a few nights; the capable hunter 0.4 to 1.2, the master
1.2 to 3.2 near home and 3 to 6 on fresh ground far away, capped by the carry (D2-255); fowling 0.24 to 2.2, rising in a freeze.
The stocks and the keeping, not the hourly rates, stop wild food feeding everyone (fishing_ladder §10, hunting_ladder §numbers).

### 7. Phases

1. **Mend what misleads** (small, first): hand fishing refused in a run, the net meeting salmon, "the Brook" read as Brook
   House, the antler found at the river, wear reaching use, the stale records (engine_audit finding 8); the river's results and
   the worst signs (W-72's "never empty when it is not").
2. **The compressed year** (§1), decided and applied to the wild stocks, then the bots rerun.
3. **Materials and gear** (§2, §3): withies, cord, flax, butchering; traps, snares and nets as gear; set and lift; the weir and
   racks recosted.
4. **Methods and stocks** (§4): the shallows, line fishing lowered, set lines, fish nets, fowling split.
5. **Signs and lore** (§5), the river's and the wood's.
6. **Later:** the bow and its chain, dogs and ferrets as kept beasts, keeping (park, warren, dovecote), the seine with boats,
   tanning, the fish pond, the coast.

Each phase ends with the bots on the nutrition worlds and a short live run of the arm it changes.

## Why

Calibrating fishing or hunting alone does not hold: the take depends on the gear, the gear on its materials, the materials on land
and time, and what people choose on what they can sense (Zach, 21 Nov: "I don't think calibrating really makes sense without
this"). The live runs showed all four failing at once (02zzz, 02zzy, 02zzy2).

## Alternatives considered

- **Calibrate the take rates alone and keep gear free:** quick, but gear stays no choice and capital differs only by racks and a
  weir; rejected by Zach's ask.
- **A deep ladder of rungs per livelihood:** rejected (15 Nov).
- **Keep the 32-day production as a generous named lever:** possible (§1); it would keep wild living about 11 times too rich per
  mouth and hide every other calibration under it.
- **Cord and twine as two goods, rope as a third; sinew and horsehair:** more real, more goods; the notes recommend one cord.

## Open questions

1. The compressed year (§1): production on the real day, as the runs, or the 32-day year kept as a named lever?
2. Gear life: real lives (a net hundreds of uses, an heirloom) or a middle course (60 to 80 uses, renewed)?
3. The withy bed's two to three years: kept, or compressed as the salmon's return was?
4. Flax: a plot crop or a garden close, and how far retting fouls the water?
5. One trap set per act, or a line of several?
6. The shallows as a stock of their own (recommended)?
7. Fowling split with a `flies` trait (recommended), and wildfowl raised to real winter numbers?
8. Butchering yield and by-products by skill now, or later with tanning?
9. Gutting as its own work, settled with the salting rate (Q-73)?
10. The bow, dogs and keeping (park, warren, dovecote): later (recommended)?
