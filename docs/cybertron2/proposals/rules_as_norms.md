# Rules as the grammar of norms: claims, access and laws in the same book

**Status:** speculation (23 Nov 2026). Nothing decided.
**Owner:** 37.2. **Thread:** households generalised into institutions and laws (W-86, benched); the household book (D2-274, D2-337 to
D2-339; proposals/store_rules.md; proposals/w73_design/u6_book.md).

## Summary

The household book's grammar (D2-339: set, keep, first, share, do, drop, with structured conditions) is a small language for "who
does what, with what, when". Claims (who holds a thing), access (who may use or take from it), and later a village's laws could be
written in the same grammar, so that one mechanism carries a household's routine, a lord's dues and a village's by-laws, and
institutions grow out of households (D2-299) by widening who a book belongs to and whom it binds.

## What it would add

- **Permission kinds:** `may` and `may not` {who: a set, act: an act or a kind of act, on: a thing, place or set, when?, unless?}:
  who may take from a store, fish a water, cut a wood, use a weir's gap, graze a common. Claims become the standing `may` rules of a
  holder; access lists become sets.
- **Books above the household:** a book that belongs to a set wider than a household (a village, a manor, a guild), with an
  author (the head of that set, or a body that agrees: the "agree" shape between many) and a scope (the people it binds).
- **Duties owed outward:** `do` rules owed to another (a tenant's days of work, a share of the catch as rent), which are today's
  agreements made standing.
- **Breach and enforcement:** going against a rule is seen and recorded (as D2-274 already has for the household); what follows
  is people's choice (shame, a fine agreed, a refusal of trade, force), not the engine's. The engine never stops a breach: a law
  is a norm, and enforcement needs people (scenario_design.md: chokepoints that need people to enforce them).

## Why it fits

- **One grammar:** the routine of a house, the rights in a wood and the rules of a village are the same shape: who, what act, on
  what, when, unless. Agents learn one form.
- **Growth from households (D2-299):** a household's book with sets of people is already a small polity; widening the book's
  owner and scope is the step to institutions, rather than a separate law system.
- **Leverage:** who holds the weir, the salt pan or the wood becomes legible as rules others live under, which is where
  dependence and resentment come from.

## Open questions

1. Are claims re-expressed as `may` rules, or do claims stay and permissions sit beside them?
2. Who may write a book wider than a household, and how is it agreed?
3. What does the engine do with a breach beyond recording it and letting people see it?
4. How much of this a prompt can carry: a household's book is about 90 words of grammar; laws would add authors, scope and
   permissions.
