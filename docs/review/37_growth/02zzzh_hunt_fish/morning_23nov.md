## The questions, ranked by what they block

**A. The basis everything is priced on** (blocks every number in the build)

1. **What a unit of the bulk goods weighs** (wave1 q1; audit 1). Recommend handling units of a stated weight, every recipe rewritten
   in real kg: timber and stone a 25 kg load, firewood a 10 kg bundle, straw a 16 kg truss, hay 10 kg, charcoal 20 kg, withies and
   bark 10 kg bundles. The other way keeps today's units at their real weights, and timber can then never be carried on foot.
2. **How "keeps for d days" becomes a nightly loss** (wave1 q3; W80-Q1; audit 2). Half gone by then (part 1), a quarter, or a tenth.
   A household reaches spring with about 12, 29 or 41 food. Recommend a quarter now, a curve by age later with dated lots in stores.
3. **Fuel burned a night at its real weight** (wave1 q2): about 10 kg on a normal night, 30 on a cold one; today's heat needs give 20
   and 80 even at the new bundle. Recommend yes.
4. **The racks' load** (Q-83 and D2-284; audit 4): your two yeses differ by two (500 fish on 2 spaces; 250 kg a set). Recommend 250
   kg a set on 1 space, D2-284 standing and Q-83's record corrected.
5. **The food's energy basis** (Q-69; audit S1): "1 food = 2,500 kcal, children 0.6, elders 0.8" is only a default taken, yet every
   food number rests on it. Recommend recording it as a decision, or saying what it should be.

**B. The mechanisms** (each blocks a part of the build)

6. **Processing containers as one engine unit** (W80-Q2; audit 6): racks, a salting tub, drying racks, tan pits and retting ponds
   as one kind of container whose lots change state over nights. Recommend yes; it is what "move smoked fish to the cellar once
   cured" (D2-282) needs.
7. **How conditional rules fire** (W80-Q3): on lasting states held until the chore is done (so a missed chore is not lost), reading
   only what the household knows, in a chore phase before the fallback. Recommend yes. 37.4's standing-rules build.
8. **Acts on held things default to one's own, recorded** (Q-80; audit 9): your 21 Nov answer is only in Q-80's notes. Recommend
   recording it with one change: a tend takes all one's own of that kind at the place (six traps are one line), an act that uses
   a thing up asks; household things count as every member's own.
9. **Set and tend as two acts on gear** (W81 q1): setting needs the gear in hand; coming back is "tend"; a technique named alone
   chooses by what stands (tend if one's own gear is there, set if not). This also fixes a flaw in part 4's ten shapes, which would
   bring the set-for-lift bug back. Recommend yes, with the ten shapes as the verb list (Q-27; W78 q3).
10. **The building factor's staging** (bots q1; audit 7): houses, fittings and the weir to 1/15 first; fences and scrub clearing
    held until cheaper crop protection (a fence by its length, a dead hedge) and clearing a plot at a time (L-153) exist; gear on
    the same factor as the works at each stage. Recommend yes. The short live run then on Brookend's newcomers at 1/15.
11. **Lives counted in nights against lives counted in acts** (wave1 q4; audit 5): set gear, bedrolls and tents by the calendar's
    1/11.4, work done by 1/7; a net's life about 40 to 60 sets, so the flax close holds. Recommend yes.
12. **Materials at real hours while making is at 1/7** (W2-1): the cord in a net costs about eight times its knotting. Recommend
    keeping it, named as a departure: it makes the material trades worth having.

**C. Scope of this build**

13. **A flax strip smaller than a plot** (W2-2): recommend a strip for a household and whole plots for a supplier.
14. **A salting tub now, barrels and the cooper later** (W80-Q9; audit 12): recommend yes; salt fish keeps its year only in brine.
15. **Tanning's scope** (audit 13; W2-7): lime from wood ash, lighter leathers at 16 to 24 nights, frame, harness and bellows in
    leather; shoes now or later (shoes are three quarters of a village's leather). Recommend shoes now, worn by days walked.
16. **What a freeze stops** (D2-265; audit 14): set gear idle, edge and bank lines refused, the weir icing and wearing, a run's
    night held over to the next open night, fowling at its best. Recommend this, checking that a big river's open stream still
    lets a run pass.

**D. The people**

17. **The learning lifespan's base** (audit 8): the engine's base is 32, D2-267 says 64, a proposal's tables 96. Recommend 64 as the
    default, with every craft's practice scaled by lifespan.
18. **Kept lines over a life** (audit 10): founders' seeded lines on top of the places opened each stretch would reach about 40 lines
    in old age. Recommend additive with a cap of about 30, measured for cost in the first runs.
19. **Should the core prompt say knowledge dies unless passed on?** (W78 q1): recommend not, so it stays a cultural strand that can
    be run against its absence.
