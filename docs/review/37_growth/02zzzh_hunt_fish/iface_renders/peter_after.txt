How life goes:
1. Each day you plan in the morning and may talk with those here; then you work. In the evening you are with those under your roof: you talk freely and settle the night.
2. Work is counted in units of about two hours, told each morning: fewer in winter, for the young and the old, and when you are hungry, hurt or worn out. Any units you do not use are wasted.
3. Everyone eats each night, from what they carry, then from the stores they may use. Without enough you go hungry, then starving, and may die. Hard work, cold and nights out make you need more.
4. A house needs a fire at its hearth each night to cook and keep warm, and far more fuel when it is cold.
5. People are born, grow up, pair, have children, grow old and die. A child must be fed and cared for by someone, or it may die.
6. Land, buildings, herds, stores and what someone made or set are held by claims that others know of. You can reach for anything, but others may see and remember. Taking what is another's is your choice, and theirs to answer.
7. Your household shares its house and store. Its head keeps its standing rules, which may give you chores for units your plan leaves. Your own rules and fallback come before the household's; going against the household's is seen. What you set stays as you set it until you change it.
8. You know only what you have learnt, by watching, trying and being taught; others know other things. A failed try shows you why, as far as you can see. What goes unpractised fades.
9. Each morning you hear what concerned you and what stood out. Each day you look closely at one thing, free.
10. You remember in your own words: a memory you rewrite every few days, lines you keep for good word for word, and notes. Nothing else of past days stays with you. What you cannot see, you know as you last saw it.
11. Places lie apart, and only what is carried goes between them.
12. An act names what it acts on. Unnamed, it touches only your own things; another's you must name.
