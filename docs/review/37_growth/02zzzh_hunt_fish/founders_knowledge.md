## The short answer

- **What they keep is mostly world knowledge, learnt late and at a cost.** Of 1,364 kept lines, about half are how the world works
  (seasons and timing 285, crafts and yields 180, keeping 63, places 54, dangers 51, prices 37), 176 (13%) are how the acts work
  (set against lift, teach and learn the same day, the gap after a birth), 180 (13%) are the scoring goal and its arithmetic of
  children (171 of them in the river run, whose seeded goal said "on which you will be graded"), and the rest state, events and
  people. The best of them were learnt by loss: "Barley on barley ruins the ground" (c3, day 29), "wolves take beasts from a
  grazed Leaze in winter" (lake, day 18), "fresh meat rots in the hand ... dry or salt every scrap the same day" (river, day 25).
- **Seasonal knowledge is what living cannot teach in time and what rolling memory drops.** 27 river agents wrote of the eels;
  their later memories kept it a third of the time (34%), against 72% for wolves and 79% for the salt price. In the runs world
  the summer's empty river taught "fished out" before the autumn run came (02zzy). The one seasonal fact that was seeded, the
  sowing lore, changed what people did: sowing acts by those who held it fell in a good window 197 times in 218 (90%), by those
  without it 107 in 146 (73%); in pw1, where half the founders held it, 21 of 21 against 9 of 18.
- **Passing on works when the parent's own livelihood is real.** In the river run 134 of 487 care acts passed a line; about 25 of
  the 122 distinct sentences are practical, and nearly all name the household's own living: the Brooks' salt ("one firewood a
  salt, and Middleham pays 2 grain a salt"), the Wrights' wicker ("never let a fish lie in the wicker past dawn"). Lucy's line went
  to Olive, who passed "walk the snare line and the wicker at first light" to Absolon: a practical line that crossed two
  generations within the 30 days. Elsewhere almost nothing practical passed (pw1: 11 passes, all identity).
- **The base set** every founder shares: eight lines, about 310 words, on the year, winter, keeping, the land renewing, danger, the
  household and its neighbours, and how knowledge is got and lost (Part 2.1). **Practice sets** of 6 to 10 lines each, every line
  read from engine state (Part 2.3; the fisher in full).
- **Cultural strands that matter most,** by the evidence: passing knowledge on (or not), taboos that carry a real rule (the
  spawning run, seed corn, the plough ox), exploring against keeping to the known, and sharing crafts against guarding them
  (Part 3).
- **Leave out:** the scoring goal (it took 36% of the river run's kept lines and half its passes); the mechanics of acts (they
  belong to the core text and the engine's lessons, not a person's memory); exact yields, prices and the best number of traps (the
  decisions themselves); lists of every craft, food and place; anything that changes (stocks, holdings, "fished out"). Budget: a
  founder starts with about 14 to 18 kept lines (550 to 700 words), against about 20 of their own over a life (D2-266) (Part 4).

### 1.6 What passed to children, and whether it was practical

- **River (134 passes, 122 distinct):** about 70 are the goal ("take a partner young under a Wright roof, and bear many"); about
  25 practical, and those almost all the household's own living: Avice and Warin the sowing calendar on day 0 ("beans in early
  summer, barley in spring, rye in late autumn"); Mabel "Net the duck and geese on the Brook when frost packs them" (true: a freeze
  crowds fowl); Mabel "Keep out of Hazel Copse when a bear lies on its kill"; Lucy "eat nothing raw that a fire can double"; Lucy
  "SEED IN THE GROUND, every spring, before any bin or bargain" (a taboo she made herself after the blight). None mentions the
  eel run, though the Wrights and Brooks knew it, and none where stone lies.
- **Did it reach behaviour?** In the few children who came of age within the run: Dionisia, told "walk your wicker every morning"
  (Aldric, day 18), learnt wicker traps and set them (day 27); Matilda, told the salt lines, boiled salt after her lesson (day 23).
  Small numbers, but the direction is right.
- **c3:** Margery (Opus 5.5) passed 4 practical of 7: "Keep the best ears of each harvest for seed", "Keep timber back for mending
  the buildings", "Barley is not hardy: sow it in spring or early summer", "Lay up plenty of firewood and timber in autumn".
- **pw1, wild_s7, f1, lake:** identity or nothing ("We are the Ashford folk: keep the kin together, the field sown and the store
  full"). When the system prompt tells everyone everything, a parent has no reason to pass the world on (Q-81).
- **Teaching** (river 22 teach, 17 lessons met): boil salt 7, milking 5, wicker traps 3, nets 2, smelting 2, ploughing 2, mining
  1: the household's specialism each time, to its own youths.

### 1.7 Noise

- **Told to all, used by none.** Of 74 basic crafts on the "Everyone knows" line, 16 were never planned in nine runs (the weir,
  smoking fish and meat, tending the smoke, salt pans, the hand mill, oven bread, the tent, the bedroll among them) and 19 fewer
  than 10 times. The place lines' "its people know ..." lists (the river run's Middleham line names nine crafts) were not where
  anyone looked for a teacher.
- **The scoring goal.** "YOUR GOAL, on which you will be graded ..." (river, all 45) became 171 kept lines and 70 passes of "bear
  many": the largest single use of memory in the run, against scenario_design §3.
- **Awareness from speech** (crafts_evaluation): of 226 river records, 45 the old `hunt` row, 35 `hidden: under brush`, 31 `wall`.
- **Kept lines that never mattered:** ages and countdowns ("Hamo (56 days old) turns elder day 20"), one-off events ("Christian
  died day 16"), and the order of a single morning. About a fifth of kept lines are state that is stale within days.
- **A seeded custom refused.** The Gorm were seeded "we take what others have, and drive out or kill every other family"; Ketil
  (Haiku 5.5) kept instead "We meet strangers in peace and trade, not by raid or force". A seeded hostile value can be overridden
  by the model, which matters for Part 3.

## Part 2: the livelihoods and what each should know

### 2.1 The base set every founder shares

Eight lines, about 310 words. Each is the companion of the purposeful core (Q-81 §1): the core says how a day, eating and replying
work; the base set says how this world's year and living go, as a person of it would put it. Each line is a template filled from
the world's own state at the start, so it is true where it is seeded.

### 2.3 Each practice: crafts by level, its starting lines, what a neighbour knows

Craft names are the engine's rows (`engine/data.py` TECHNIQUES) or, marked ★, the network's new ones (proposals/hunt_fish_materials.md
§4 to §7). The difficulty class (trying_and_learning.md: s simple, p practised, k skilled) follows each where it is set. "Known" is
the household's core, "capable" its common crafts, "rough" the specialism one person in a district holds at capable or above, and
"aware" what they have watched others do. A founder of the practice gets the full set of lines; a household member who helps but
does not live by it gets the first three; a household with a second livelihood (a farmer who fishes) gets its first four.
