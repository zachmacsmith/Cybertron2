# 02zt: dependences, and things that help other things (9 Nov)

> **Superseded in part (9 Nov):** consolidated in [02zv_wild_consolidation.md](02zv_wild_consolidation.md), whose ledger places every item here in a pack, a deferral or a proposed cut; where they differ, 02zv holds, and the build brief for the first stage is [proposals/methods_and_encounters.md](../../cybertron2/proposals/methods_and_encounters.md). Withdrawn readings are listed in 02zv §3.9 (among them: real-day conversions of acts, the attention budget, institutions written into the wildlife).

The third outside note on the wild layer, after 02zr (the behaviour critique) and 02zs (the wild in a society's economy and
development). Zach asked: from the new goods, tools, buildings and ladders of 02zs, what new dependences could emerge, and how can
each add layers of things that help other things: things that make hunting better, that help predict, store, guard, find, carry or
breed. It is a session note: it proposes, it decides nothing.

**The two principles hold throughout** (02zs §1): nothing social is forced (a dependence here is physical: who holds or makes what
others need; any institution that forms is the agents'), and no bespoke one-kind checklists (improvements are preferred that serve
many kinds and many systems). Perception is never filtered.

**Time.** Y = 32: an engine day stands for about 11 real days, a year 32 engine days, a unit of work about two real days. Each
improvement below carries a rough build or growth time in engine days, because whether it can matter within a run, or only across
continued runs, decides how it can be tested.

## 0. Findings in brief

1. **The wild ladders are mostly not separate ladders: they are new uses for the engine's existing ones.** Transport makes far
   grounds and light, dear goods (furs, wax, smoked eels, feathers) worth carrying; preservation makes a run, a drive or a freeze's
   fowl worth taking in bulk; buildings make stores and kept animals possible; tools and recipes make the gear; husbandry makes
   dogs, ferrets and pigs; knowledge and documents make spots and counts last. Building the wild layer on these, instead of beside
   them, multiplies what each already does (§1).
2. **Every useful improvement creates at least two dependence edges, usually upstream ones the user does not see.** A better bow
   needs the yew's holder, the bowyer, the fletcher, the goose-keeper or fowler for feathers, the glue-boiler, and the smith for
   heads. A net needs bast or hemp, the rope-maker and the netter. A kennel needs meat and offal from the hunt and the butcher, and
   bread from the miller. These are the edges scenario_design asks for, and they arise from physics, not rules (§2).
3. **Improvements come in four layers:** first-order (the tool or structure in use: a better bow, a raised granary); second-order
   (its makers and inputs: the fletcher, feathers, glue, the yew); third-order (the suppliers' suppliers: the coppice for bark and
   poles, the quarry for lime and stone, the salt route, the fuel); and **instruments and infrastructure that help many things at
   once** (rides cut through a wood, a high seat, a lookout, a cart road into the wood, a boat, a smokehouse, a salt supply, a
   kennel, a mews, tallies at the weir, a year-book of mast and runs, a map of spots, marked trees, teaching). The fourth layer is
   where development compounds (§3).
4. **Prediction and knowledge aids are improvements in their own right.** A count kept at the weir each night, a year-book of mast
   and runs, a map of spots, marked bee trees, the elder's sayings tested against a tally: each raises the value of every take,
   watch and store it informs. They need only documents (decided), spots as knowledge items, and teaching (built) (§3.4).
5. **A few hubs sit on so many chains that they become chokepoints and specialisms:** the smith (heads, traps, knives, nails,
   chains), the rope-maker (snares, nets, leashes, bowstrings, harness lashing), the salter (every preserved catch), the cooper
   (barrels for salted fish and meat, tallow, honey), the tanner (leather for harness, falconry gear, leashes, buckets), the
   coppice and its woodward (withies for traps and hurdles, poles, bark for tanning, charcoal), the kennel, and the smokehouse.
   The coppice is the least obvious and the most connected (§4.1).
6. **Several loops compound:** the hunt feeds the hounds that improve the hunt; the warren's ferrets eat its offal; kept beasts' dung
   feeds the fields that feed the beasts; the dovecote's pigeons eat grain and give the best manure; geese give feathers that fletch
   the arrows that take fowl; the coppice gives withies for the eel traps whose catch is smoked over its wood; salt lets a bulk
   catch keep, which pays for carrying salt. These loops are what make an improvement repay its cost, and some run away (the
   catch rises until the stock falls) (§4.2).
7. **Development changes the problem rather than ending it.** Stone stores end rats as a store problem but not as a field one;
   enclosure ends the commons and makes new edge habitat for foxes and rabbits; clearing ends the boar and the mast; draining ends the
   ague, the eels and the fowl; kennels answer wolves and bring rabies risk and the dogs' food bill; better takes end stocks faster.
   Each trade-off is a reason for a society to argue, and none needs a rule (§5).
8. **The engine needs few new mechanisms for all of this,** mostly from 02zs §8: traits against properties (so improvements answer
   kinds without lists), one take with gear, kept populations in structures, trained beasts with bonds, the butcher step and
   by-product goods, delayed processes, spots and records as knowledge, and physical externalities. New here: **infrastructure as
   parcel and edge features with physical effects** (rides, high seats, a cart track into the wood, a boat landing), **goods with
   condition and quality** (a hide, a hawk, a net), and **capacities on structures** that make scale matter (§6).

**Three interlocks worth stating plainly** (as Zach suggested them):
- **Transport makes far grounds and the fur trade worth it.** Fresh meat at about 0.3 spoilage a day cannot travel an edge; smoked
  or salted it can; furs and wax travel any distance. Without carriage and preservation, wild takes are local and small; with them,
  a wood or fen village has something to sell.
- **Preservation makes the run worth catching in bulk.** The eel run's best night gives more than a household can eat before it
  rots; a smokehouse, salt and barrels turn it into the winter's food and a trade good. The bottleneck is the smoking capacity and
  the salt, not the catch.
- **A cart makes bulk takes movable.** At the engine's scaling one person carries one deer (about 2.6 meat by weight against a
  carrying capacity of 10), so a single kill needs no cart. Bulk does: a weir's catch on the best eel night, a drive that kills
  several beasts far out, a slaughter, salt brought in for them, timber for a pale. A pack ox (60) or an ox cart (200 to 400, later)
  turns a collective take into a collective harvest, and the distance a take can come from grows with it.

| **a calendar or almanac of markers** (warmth signs against farm work) | predict | records; markers (built) | | timing sowing and the year's work by warmth, not date |
| **teaching** (built) and written methods | all | a master; documents | the learner needs the master; a book outlives him | methods spread; crafts survive deaths |
| **a lookout or watch-place** (a high place with a view of the fields' edge) | guard, find | a parcel feature (a tower, a tree platform, a high bank) | | one watcher sees more parcels; the watch's reach by sight (the noticing rule) |
| **a high seat or a hide** (in a wood or at a field edge) | hunt, guard | timber; a known place | | the watcher unseen and above the scent (M1: animals' noticing of people falls) |
| **rides cut through a wood** | hunt, find, carry | felling and clearing along a line; upkeep | | sight lines (visibility from forest state, forests.md), standers' places for a drive, a way to haul timber and carcasses, firebreaks |
| **a cart track into the wood** | carry | clearing; upkeep | | handcarts and carts reach the wood (a handcart needs a track: transport.md) |
| **a boat landing; a ford made good** | carry | stone, timber | | crossings, fowling and fishing from boats |
| **a crew's organisation** (who beats and who stands; who smokes and who salts; who sets and who checks) | hunt, store | agreements (built) if people make them | the organiser and the crew | the drive, the weir night, the smokehouse batch |

## 3. The layers of things that help other things

### 3.1 The four layers
| Layer | What it is | Examples | Who it makes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **first-order** | the thing in use, at the moment of the take, the watch or the store | a better bow; a net; a scent hound; a raised granary; a smokehouse; a hedge | the user who owns it |
| **second-order** | its makers and inputs | the bowyer, the fletcher, feathers, glue, the yew; the rope-maker and the bast; the dog-breeder and the meat for the kennel; the mason and the staddles | specialists with skill and tools |
| **third-order** | the suppliers' suppliers | the coppice (bark, withies, poles, charcoal); the quarry and the lime kiln; the salt pan and the salt road; fuel; the fields (straw, hemp, grain for the kennel and the dovecote); the herds (hides, horn, offal) | the woodward, the quarryman, the salter, the carter, the farmer and herder |
| **instruments and infrastructure** | things that help many takes, stores, watches and journeys at once | rides, high seats, lookouts, cart tracks into the wood, boats and landings, a smokehouse, a salt supply, a kennel, a mews, tallies, a year-book, a map of spots, marked trees, teaching | holders of fixed works; keepers of records; masters |

**The point of layers:** a first-order improvement repays its user; second- and third-order ones are where other households come to
need each other; the fourth layer compounds, because one ride, one smokehouse or one tally helps many people and many kinds. That is
also where control can concentrate, and so where people may argue: the agents' business.

### 3.2 Worked chains (first to fourth layer)

**A better hunt for deer.**
- First: the bow; a scent hound; a stalk into the wind.
- Second: the bowyer (a yew stave), the fletcher (ash shafts, goose feathers, glue), the smith (heads), the dog-breeder and
  trainer, meat and offal for the kennel.
- Third: a yew tree's holder; geese (kept) or wildfowl (taken) for feathers; hides and bone for glue (the herds, the butcher); the
  coppice's charcoal for the smith.
- Fourth: rides through the wood (standers' places, sight, hauling), a high seat, spots known and told, a cart track to bring a
  drive's take home, a smokehouse and salt so a big take keeps.

**Keeping the grain from rats.**
- First: a raised granary on staddles; a stone floor; a cat; a terrier.
- Second: the mason and the quarry (staddles, flags), the carpenter, a kitten from a good mouser, the terrier's breeder.
- Third: the quarry's tools from the smith; the cat's milk and scraps; the terrier's meat.
- Fourth: a common store if people make one (the village's granary is an existing layer only a society can afford: economy_plan.md).

**Taking the eel run.**
- First: wicker traps in a weir; a crew on the night.
- Second: the basket-maker (withies), the weir's builders (timber, stones), the smoker and the salter.
- Third: the coppice (withies, fuel), the salt pan or spring, the cooper's barrels.
- Fourth: a tally of catches by night at the weir (when it runs, how big it is this year); a year-book of the dry spells and the first
  rains; a cart track to the weir; a boat to set traps in the deeper water.

**Guarding the flock.**
- First: a fold; a mastiff; a herder with a horn and a light.
- Second: the waller (stone), the dog-breeder, the horner, the chandler.
- Third: the quarry; meat for the dog; fat for tallow.
- Fourth: a lookout on the pasture's edge; news from the next village of the pack's direction (the inn, travellers).

**Keeping bees.**
- First: skeps; a bee bole in a wall; driving rather than killing the bees.
- Second: straw (the harvest), the waller, the beekeeper's knowledge.
- Third: the fields (straw, beans and orchards as forage; the lime and heather).
- Fourth: wax to the scribe (seals) and the chandler (candles): documents and light now depend on bees.

### 3.3 Prediction aids
Prediction is the "know" family made into improvements:
- **Instruments that turn signs into numbers:** a tally at the weir; a count of a herd; a warren's take by season; nests counted in a
  heronry. They make trends visible inside a run and let people see a stock fall before it collapses (the hyperstable trap,
  living_populations.md §0).
- **Records across years:** a year-book of mast, runs, freezes, vole years and grub years carries slow arms (years) into a run, where
  02zr found them otherwise invisible. Elders' sayings can be checked against it.
- **Places that see:** a lookout over the field's edge, a high seat, rides cut for sight lines: each raises what the noticing rule lets
  a watcher see.
- **Sentinels kept or encouraged:** geese at the house, a dog in the yard, jays read in the wood (02zr M7).
- **Teaching and written methods:** the expert's readings (by skill) passed on, so the next generation reads signs at a higher level.

None of these needs the engine to do more than hold documents, spots and counts that people choose to write, and to let the noticing
rule see further from a high place.

## 4. Compounding hubs and loops

### 4.1 Hubs: improvements on many chains at once

| Hub | Chains it sits on | Why it becomes a chokepoint or a specialism | What makes it scarce |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the coppice and its woodward** | withies (traps, hurdles, baskets, wattle), poles (fences, pales, hop poles), bark (tanning), bast (cord), charcoal (the smith), firewood (smoking, rendering), browse (deer) | almost every wild improvement draws on it; a coppice is a cycle of years and can be ruined by browsing and grazing | its age, its protection from browsing, the woodward's skill, who holds it |
| **the smith** | heads, knives, traps (iron later), chains, nails, hoops, bells, the billhook and felling axe (D2-48) | specialised tools repay a specialist in weeks (recipes.md §15); every hunt and structure needs metal somewhere | copper or iron, charcoal, skill |
| **the rope-maker** | snares, nets, bowstrings, leashes, harness lashing, climbing rope, well ropes | cord is the hidden spine of the take family (02zs §2.4) | bast or hemp, time, skill |
| **the salter and the salt route** | every salted catch and slaughter, cheese (needs salt now), hides (salting before tanning) | salt has one source in a region (02g) and no world has one yet | the shore or the spring, fuel for boiling, carriage |
| **the cooper** | barrels for salted fish and meat, tallow, honey, ale | bulk preservation and its carriage need barrels | oak staves, hoops, skill |
| **the tanner** | harness (transport, ploughing), falconry gear, leashes and collars, buckets, shoes, bellows (the smith) | months-long process; needs bark and lime as well as hides | hides, bark, lime, pits, time |
| **the butcher and the renderer** | meat, fat (tallow for light), hides (the tanner), bone (glue, tools, bone meal), sinew (bowstrings), offal (the kennel, the ferrets, bait) | every carcass, wild or kept, passes through | skill, a place |
| **the kennel** | drives, finding, guarding folds and houses, herding, ratting, rabbiting | a pack is capital that took seasons; the keeper's skill and the lines bred | meat and offal, a trainer, years |
| **the smokehouse** | eels, salmon, a drive's meat, the slaughter, fowl | capacity over days decides how much of a glut keeps | fuel, a tender, a building |
| **the miller** (indirect) | bread for the kennel; acorn and bean meal; bait | the mill is already a chokepoint (economy_plan.md) | the mill |
| **the boatwright** | fowling and eeling in deep water, seines, crossings, river transport | water's riches need boats | timber, pitch or hides, skill |

### 4.2 Loops

**Reinforcing loops (an improvement in one system feeds another back):**
- **Hunt, hounds, hunt:** the hunt's offal and meat feed the kennel; the hounds find and hold game; better takes feed more hounds.
  Bounded by the stock (a better hunt depletes) and the meat the kennel eats.
- **Warren, ferrets, warren:** the warren's rabbits and their offal feed the ferrets that bolt the rabbits to the nets.
- **Dung, fields, beasts:** kept beasts' dung feeds the fields; the fields feed the beasts (straw, hay, grain). The dovecote's pigeons
  eat grain (often the neighbours') and return the best dung.
- **Geese, feathers, arrows, fowl:** kept geese give feathers that fletch arrows that take fowl; the fowl's feathers fletch more.
- **Coppice, withies, traps, smoke:** the coppice's withies make eel traps; the catch is smoked over the coppice's wood; the eeler wants
  the coppice kept.
- **Salt, preservation, trade, salt:** salt lets a bulk catch keep; the kept catch is traded; the trade pays for carrying salt.
- **Knowledge, takes, knowledge:** each foray finds or confirms spots; better spots give better takes; the takes pay for the forays.
- **Hedges, habitat, pests, hedges:** hedges keep stock and deer out and also shelter rabbits, birds and foxes, which raises the case
  for more hedges and for gap-tight coops (a loop that turns benefit into new problems).

**Runaway and balancing loops:**
- **Better takes deplete the stock** (a tool never adds to a stock, recipes.md §8): the bow, the net and the kennel raise catches
  until the stock falls; catch per effort then falls too, or holds while the stock collapses (the hyperstable case).
- **Better defence displaces:** a hedge round one field sends the boar to the next (02zr M3); defence by one household raises the
  value of defence for its neighbours.
- **Keeping attracts:** a warren, a dovecote, a fishpond and a coop each draw predators (foxes, stoats, otters, herons, hawks) and
  thieves.

## 5. Trade-offs: where development changes the problem

| Improvement | What it ends | What it creates or leaves |
|---|---|---|
| stone stores | rats and mice as a store problem; damp in grain | rats in the stacks, the field edges and the midden; the mason's and the quarry's importance |
| enclosure with hedges and banks | straying, deer and boar at that field, open grazing of that land | new edge habitat (rabbits, foxes, birds); the commoners' takes on that land gone; displacement to unhedged neighbours |
| clearing a wood | boar, wolves, deer cover near the fields; the bear | no mast, no pannage, no game, no firewood or bark from it; wind and erosion (if modelled) |
| draining fen and wet meadow | the ague, slugs on wet land, fluke, floods on that meadow | eels, fowl, reeds, cranes, peat gone; fishers and fowlers lose their ground |
| a weir | the run passing freely; a crossing problem | a chokepoint on the river; less for those below; the mill and the weir compete |
| a kennel | wolves at the fold, finding as luck | the dogs' food bill; rabies risk (when illness exists); dogs that chase deer or sheep |
| a warren | wild rabbits as a free take | the warren's edge grazing neighbours' crops; feral rabbits if it decays; foxes and stoats drawn; poaching |
| a dovecote | squabs and dung to its holder | pigeons on the neighbours' seed |
| the bow and the net | small takes | faster depletion of every stock they reach |
| a smokehouse | gluts rotting | fuel demand on the wood; a fire risk; a concentrated store worth stealing |
| a deer park | deer as a wild take | timber for the pale, upkeep, gaps that let deer into crops; the forest's deer drawn in by a leap |
| breeding dogs or pigs | wildness and variability | dependence on the breeder's line; disease spread in dense keeping |

Each row is a reason for a society to argue about land, water and stocks. None needs a rule; the physics gives the stakes.

## 6. What the engine needs

Mostly 02zs §8 (traits against properties; one take with gear; kept populations; trained beasts with bonds; the butcher step and
by-product goods; delayed processes; spots and records as knowledge; physical externalities and evidence). New from this pass:

1. **Infrastructure as parcel and edge features with physical effects.** A ride (raises visibility in that forest parcel and gives a
   drive's standers' places), a high seat or hide (raises what a watcher notices and lowers animals' noticing of the watcher, M1), a
   lookout (sight reaches the next parcels), a cart track into a wood (lets a handcart in: transport.md's track), a boat landing or a
   ford made good. Each is a work on a parcel or an edge like field works (D2-66) and fences, with upkeep.
2. **Goods with condition and quality.** A hide spoiled by a careless take, a net that frays, a hawk whose bond fades, a coppice past
   its cutting age: the existing wear scale (recipes.md §9) and decay (D2-87) extended to quality where it changes value.
3. **Capacities on structures** (a smokehouse's batch, a kennel's pack, a dovecote's nests, a pond's stock, a warren's mounds, a weir's
   traps), so scale is physical and a larger structure is a larger investment.
4. **Inputs that are other systems' outputs**, written as recipes and structure costs: withies, poles and bark from the coppice
   (forests.md Q4 already asks for poles from young growth), lime from the quarry, straw from the harvest, offal from the butcher, salt
   from a pan or spring (D2-56, not built), barrels from a cooper (a recipe), cord from bast.
5. **Kept-animal food as a need someone meets** (husbandry.md §4): a kennel eats meat and bread; a dovecote grain in winter; a mews meat;
   ferrets meat. That is what ties the kennel to the hunt and the butcher, and the dovecote to the fields.
6. **Visibility from position** in the noticing rule (a high place sees further), which the rule's presence term can carry.
7. **Documents that people write about wild things** (tallies, year-books, maps of spots), using the decided documents and the spots as
   knowledge items of 02zs §8.6, with nothing automatic: the engine never writes them.
8. **Diagnostics only:** the share of improvements of each layer built; dependence edges created by them (who supplies whom); hubs'
   concentration (how many households depend on one smith, one rope-maker, one salter); loops observed; and what emerged socially
   around them, coded after the run.
