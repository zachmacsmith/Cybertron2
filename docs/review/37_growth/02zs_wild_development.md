# 02zs: the wild in a society's economy and development (9 Nov)

> **Superseded in part (9 Nov):** consolidated in [02zv_wild_consolidation.md](02zv_wild_consolidation.md), whose ledger places every item here in a pack, a deferral or a proposed cut; where they differ, 02zv holds, and the build brief for the first stage is [proposals/methods_and_encounters.md](../../cybertron2/proposals/methods_and_encounters.md). Withdrawn readings are listed in 02zv §3.9 (among them: real-day conversions of acts, the attention budget, institutions written into the wildlife).

A second outside deep dive, at Zach's request after reading 02zr (`02zr_wild_behaviour_critique.md`). It asks how every wild kind
fits a society's economy and its development: what each supplies and what needs it creates; the ladders a society climbs in dealing
with wild things; the tools and techniques on those ladders; how pests and predators are handled at scale; where each kind sits;
and what the engine needs so that progress is possible without being scripted. It also goes back through 02zr and
`proposals/wild_behaviour.md` under Zach's two new principles. It is a session note: it proposes, it decides nothing.

**Zach's principles for this pass (requirements):**
1. **No forced social mechanisms.** The engine gives physical substrate: stocks, structures, goods, evidence (which can be faked),
   externalities that exist physically. Whether people make rules about any of it is the experiment. Forest law is at most a
   separate experimental arm (a world option), not part of the wildlife.
2. **No bespoke checklist solutions.** A kind must not be answered only by its own ritual ("do X at time T against Y"). Responses
   must be general, transferable and improvable, so a more sophisticated society can find better and broader ways: better hunting
   methods, husbandry, farming, structures, tools, organisation at scale.

**Also decided since 02zr:** perception is never withheld or capped to save agents' attention. If too much happens, the world's
rates are fixed. Lines per agent-day may be measured as a diagnostic only.

**Time.** Y = 32: an engine day stands for about 11 real days, a season 8 engine days, a unit of work about two real days. Runs last
6 to 48 engine days; continued runs carry the long run. Anything on the ladders below that takes real years (a warren filling, a
hedge growing, a breed changing) is a continued-run matter or the world's seeded history.

## 0. Findings in brief

1. **The two principles reinforce each other.** A forced institution and a bespoke ritual are the same mistake at two scales: each
   decides for the society what the answer is. The engine's job is to make a few physical capabilities that many kinds respond to,
   each improvable along a ladder, and to let the costs of wild things fall where they physically fall. Then a richer society
   answers more kinds better with the same few capabilities, and any institution is its own.
2. **The built ecology branch already shows the checklist problem.** It has about twenty kind-named techniques: `take the young
   herons`, `take the young rooks`, `dig out the fox earth`, `shut the hens in`, `trap beavers`, `break the beaver dam`, `stalk deer`,
   `boar hunt`, `snare hares`, `set eel traps`, `fowling`, `pick slugs at dusk`, `clear the grubs off the beans`, `top the beans`,
   four `watch the field (...)` variants, `make clappers`, `make a horn`. Each answers one kind in one way. Most of them should
   become cases of about eight general families with gear, place and method as parameters (§7).
3. **Eight response families answer almost every kind** (§7): **take** (by method and gear), **exclude** (barriers and secure
   stores), **guard** (presence, light, noise, dogs), **reduce at source** (lines of traps, den and nest removal, habitat change),
   **keep and breed** (hold a population in a structure), **manage** (rotation, rest, leaving breeders, coppice), **process and
   store** (butcher, render, preserve, tan), and **know** (read signs, remember spots, count, teach, record). Each family has a
   ladder of tools and structures whose inputs come from other chains (cord, timber, stone, metal, hides, salt). One improvement on a
   family (a better net, a raised stone granary, a trained dog) answers many kinds at once.
4. **The question "which kinds does this answer?" should be answered by the kind's constitution, not by a list.** A net takes
   whatever flocks or runs into it; a snare takes whatever uses a run at its height; a scent hound finds whatever leaves scent; a
   raised floor stops whatever cannot climb a smooth stone; a mesh stops whatever cannot pass its gap. If methods and barriers carry
   physical properties and kinds carry body and behaviour traits (size, climbing, burrowing, flocking, scent), new kinds are
   answered by old capabilities automatically, and a better capability answers more of them. This is the single most important
   engine change (§8.1).
5. **Five ladders carry development** (§3): the **animal ladder** (take, tend, keep, breed, intensify), the **land ladder** (forage,
   manage, enclose, convert), the **defence ladder** (presence, barriers, structures, removal at source, landscape change), the
   **processing ladder** (eat fresh, preserve, use every part, specialised crafts, trade goods) and the **reach ladder** (near home,
   the ring, the wild blocks; a day out, a camp, a path, a new node). Knowledge (reading, remembering, teaching, recording) runs along
   all of them.
6. **Some kinds are rungs or bridges between ladders.** Boar to pig, wolf to dog, greylag to goose, mallard to duck, polecat to
   ferret, rock dove to dovecote, wild bees to skep, wild rabbits to warren, red and fallow deer to park, river fish to fishpond,
   hawks to the mews (trained, not bred). A society that keeps a bridge kind changes its relation to the wild one (pannage pigs
   compete with boar for mast; dogs guard against wolves; ferrets answer rabbits).
7. **The carcass is the economy's biggest untapped multiplier.** Every beast taken or slaughtered yields meat, fat, hide, bone,
   horn or antler, sinew and gut; birds yield feathers and down. The engine has meat, hide, antler and horn. Adding **fat (tallow),
   bone, sinew and glue, feathers and down, wax**, with a general `butcher` step, would let each take feed light, adhesives, cord,
   tools, arrows, bedding and writing. The tanner, horner, chandler and fletcher then depend on hunters and herders alike, and the
   wild and kept sides of the economy join (§2).
8. **General tools beat narrow ones.** Cord and nets, traps, dogs, the bow, fences and hedges, secure stores, fire and light, salt
   and smoke work across many kinds and situations. Narrow tools (a decoy pipe, an eel grig, a skep, falconry gear) should be
   specialisations of general ones (a structure, a trap, a hive as a held structure, a trained beast) so that knowing the general
   craft gives most of the special one (§4).
9. **Pests at scale are answered by structures and landscape, not rituals.** A household watches; a richer one fences; a village
   with stone and a mason builds raised stone granaries and walls; a society with cord and dogs keeps lines of traps, kennels and
   terriers; a large one clears, drains and encloses, which removes kinds altogether. Each step unlocks buildings, trades and supply
   chains; institutions (rotas, by-laws, bounties, wardens) are things a society might invent around them, never seeded (§5).
10. **Under the two principles, about a third of 02zr's social items survive as substrate, a third must be recast, and a third go**
    (§9). What survives: physical externalities (displacement, refill), evidence (traces, marks that can be cut or faked, crows that
    mob a trapper), structures as capital, possession of things. What goes: forest law (except as a world option), bounties as an
    office, close times, the nutting day, renders, standing from gifts, seeded customs, the attention budget.
11. **Forays fit the take, know and reach ladders at once** (§10): better hunting is better planning (spots known, gear, dogs,
    companions, range), and a regular foray becomes a path and a camp, then a frontier node.

## 1. What the two principles mean for design

### 1.1 Substrate, not institution
**The engine may hold:**
- stocks and populations, and their physical dynamics;
- structures (fences, walls, pales, granaries, pens, warrens, ponds, hives, weirs) with physical properties, condition and capacity;
- goods and their processing;
- techniques with gear, place, method and skill;
- physical externalities: a scared flock lands elsewhere; a vacated territory refills from outside; a weir takes what would have
  passed downstream; retting fouls water downstream;
- evidence: traces, carcasses, marks cut on an animal or a thing (which can be cut by anyone, cut over, or forged), crows that mob
  the person who trapped them, tracks in snow, and the readings people make of them by skill;
- possession (D4-23): who holds a thing or a place, as the engine already enforces it;
- world options for experiments (a forest-law arm: certain kinds and their cover held by a named person; dogs within it lawed).

**The engine must not hold:** offices, customs, closed seasons, bounties, renders, tithes, rights of common, standing or prestige
effects, or any rule about who may take what. People can make all of those with the engine's agreements, records, sayings and
force; whether they do is what experiments measure.

**The test:** if the engine's code names a social role, a rule or a payment, it is forcing. If it names a physical state that
people may make rules about, it is substrate.

### 1.2 General capabilities, not rituals
**The test for every kind and mechanism:** does the society have general, transferable, improvable ways to respond, or only a
kind-specific ritual? Three sub-tests:
1. **Transfer:** the same technique, gear, structure or knowledge answers several kinds (a mesh against weasels, rats and
   sparrows; a scent hound against deer, boar, foxes, hares; a raised stone floor against rats, mice and damp).
2. **Improvement:** there is a better version reachable through other chains (wattle hurdle, then hedge, then stone wall; snare,
   then purse net and ferret, then a warren; spear, then bow, then crossbow), and the better version answers more or answers better.
3. **Scale:** a larger or richer group can do something a household cannot (a drive, a long hedge, a stone barn, a kennel, a decoy
   pond, a fishpond), and the scale is physical (many hands, much material, a structure), not a rule.

A kind that fails all three is a ritual and should be redesigned or cut.

### 1.3 What this changes in 02zr's framing
02zr proposed a three-layer framing (constitution, encounter, social footprint). The constitution layer survives and becomes more
important: it is what lets general capabilities answer kinds without lists (finding 4). The encounter layer survives. The social
footprint layer is **recast**: it is no longer a design requirement that each kind create an institution, but a **diagnostic
prediction**: what physical externality, evidence, structure or good the kind adds, and what a null arm would therefore lack in the
state if people respond to it. Nothing in the engine acts on it. The attention budget is withdrawn (lines per agent-day remain a
diagnostic).

### 2.2 Needs, and which are real now
A good matters only if something consumes it (02j; resources_and_goods.md's tests). Under that test:
- **Real now:** food (meat, fat, eggs, honey, fish); **light** for night work and the watch (tallow, rushlights, horn lanterns; the
  audit already requires a watch's light to be a thing the state holds); hides for the built uses; dung for fields; guarding and
  herding by dogs; draught.
- **Real once decided items are built:** parchment and **wax seals** for documents (D2-24 decided documents); **feathers and sinew**
  for bows once bows exist (weapons are decided; their grades are open); glue for arrows and joinery.
- **Waiting on a parked or future need:** furs and wool for clothing (D2-10 parked); castoreum and other remedies for illness;
  oil for light beyond tallow.
- **Never forced:** "status goods". Venison, furs, swans or a hawk become status only if people treat them so. The engine can make
  them scarce, durable, visible and hard to get (substrate); it must not attach standing to them (§9).

### 2.4 What this says
- **The carcass is the hub.** One beast feeds four or five chains. A `butcher` step that splits a carcass into meat, fat, hide, bone,
  sinew and (for deer) antler would serve wild and kept animals alike and raise the value of every take without any kind-specific
  code.
- **Light and documents are the two existing needs most dependent on animals.** Light needs fat or wax or horn; documents need hide
  and wax. Both are already required by decided or audited rules.
- **The bow is the main improvable hunting tool** and the most dependent: on yew, goose feathers, sinew or hemp, glue and metal.
- **Cord is the hidden spine of the take family** (snares, purse nets, clap nets, flight nets, fishing nets, seines, bowstrings).
  Nets are deferred in economy_later.md waiting for fibre; bast cord from lime trees (a coppice product) is the cheaper first rung
  and does not need flax.

## 3. Layer b: ladders of interaction

Each ladder is a sequence of general capabilities, each rung costing more (labour, materials, a structure, a skill) and giving more
(yield, predictability, control, less risk). No rung is scripted; each is reachable by building, making, keeping or learning.
Climbing usually needs other chains (cord, timber, stone, metal, salt, fuel) and so needs other people.

### 3.1 The animal ladder: take, tend, keep, breed, intensify

| Rung | What it is | Needs | Gains | Real sequence | Engine form |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. take** | hunt, trap, net, gather eggs and young from the wild | a method, gear, skill; reach | food and goods, irregular | the oldest; the broad-spectrum shift added small game, fowl and fish where large game ran short [1] | `take` by method and gear on populations (§8.1) |
| **2. tend** | improve a wild stock where it lives: leave breeders, rest a ground, feed in a hard winter, mark a bee tree, protect a heronry, put up an owl hole | knowledge; restraint; small works | steadier yields; a stock that recovers | everywhere: traditional territories rested and rotated (the Cree, 02zr [25]) | no new mechanism: the population's dynamics answer what people leave or add |
| **3. keep** | hold a population in a place made for it and meet its food: pen, coop, sty, warren, dovecote, pond, hive, park, mews, kennel | a structure with capacity; food; labour; a source of stock (captured young, a swarm, bought animals) | predictable yields; possession of the animals; secondary products | warrens (from the 12th century), dovecotes (Garway 1326), deer parks (most made 1200 to 1350), fishponds (carp spreading from the 12th to 13th century) [2][3][4] | a kept population: habitat is a held structure, food a need someone meets (husbandry.md §4, §7) |
| **4. breed** | choose which animals breed, for traits | kept animals that breed within runs (dogs, pigs, geese, ducks, rabbits, pigeons); generations | animals better at a job (scent, speed, docility, size) | dogs bred into many types long before; the secondary products of milk, wool and traction followed keeping by long stretches [5] | traits on a kept lineage that drift by which parents breed (husbandry.md §7b) |
| **5. intensify** | more output from the same land: feed in winter, stock densely, drain and refill ponds, house beasts, fold sheep on arable, combine (pigs on mast, dung to fields) | capital, fodder, labour, many structures | output per area rises, labour per output may rise first (Boserup's point about intensification under pressure) [6] | the high medieval manorial economy | composition of the rungs above; nothing new |

**Bridges** (a wild kind and its kept relative on one lineage): boar and pig; wolf and dog; greylag and goose; mallard and duck;
polecat and ferret; rock dove and the dovecote pigeon; wild and hived bees; wild and warren rabbits; red, roe and park fallow deer;
river and pond fish. A society that climbs one bridge changes its relation to the wild side: pannage pigs eat the boar's mast; dogs
guard against wolves and find deer; ferrets turn rabbits into a crop; a hive's swarm founds a wild colony and a wild swarm fills a
hive.

### 3.2 The land ladder: forage, manage, enclose, convert

| Rung | What it is | Effect on wild kinds | Real sequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **forage** | take what the land offers | none beyond the take | |
| **manage** | coppice in rotation, pollard, cut hay in a set season, rest a ground, burn heath in strips, cut bracken | coppice regrowth feeds deer and roe (and is eaten by them); hay timing decides corncrake and hare leverets; burning makes young heather for grouse and grazing | coppice central for about two millennia; Hayley Wood on a seven-year cycle; underwood worth more than timber in many woods [7] |
| **enclose** | hedge, ditch and bank a wood against grazing; pale a park; wall a warren; dam a pond | excludes deer and stock from coppice; holds rabbits, deer, fish | woods enclosed from wood-pasture between about 1000 and 1300 [7] |
| **convert** | clear wood to field, drain fen and meadow, plough old grass | removes cover (boar, wolves, deer, bear go), removes wet ground (cranes, fowl, eels, the ague go), starts wireworm in old grass | the long medieval and early modern clearing and draining |

The land ladder is where development has its sharpest trade-offs (§5.4): converting removes threats and the goods that came with
them.

### 3.3 The defence ladder: presence, barriers, structures, removal, landscape

| Rung | Against crops | Against stores | Against beasts | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **presence** | a watcher, a child, a dog; light and noise | a person sleeping in the barn | a herder, a dog | labour every night |
| **barriers** | hurdles, wattle, a ditch | a shut door, a lid | a fold of hurdles, a coop | timber, poles from the coppice |
| **structures** | a laid hedge, a bank and ditch, a stone wall | a raised granary on staddles, a stone floor, sealed bins | a stone fold, a mesh-tight coop, a kennel of guard dogs | stone, a mason, a smith's nails, time for a hedge to grow |
| **removal at source** | lines of snares, a drive, nets | cats, terriers, a rat-catcher's round | traps at the den, a drive, poisoned bait (later) | cord, traps, dogs, many hands |
| **landscape** | clear the cover by the fields; drain | | clear the wolves' wood | very much labour; the trade-offs of §5.4 |

Each rung moves cost from **recurring labour to capital** (fences.md already makes this choice for straying herds: herding every day
or a fence and its upkeep). Capital is where specialists (the mason, the hedger, the smith) and holders (who owns the wall) enter.

### 3.4 The processing ladder: eat, preserve, use every part, craft, trade
1. **Eat fresh:** meat in a day or two.
2. **Preserve:** dry (built), salt (built for fish), smoke (a smokehouse: a fitting at a hearth or a hut with fuel), pot in fat.
   Preservation turns pulses (the eel run, a drive's carcasses, the autumn slaughter, the winter fowl) into stores, which makes
   catching in bulk worth it.
3. **Use every part:** a `butcher` step (§2.4) gives fat, hide, bone, sinew, horn or antler; render fat to tallow.
4. **Craft:** tanning, rendering, horn-working, glue-boiling, fletching, parchment, chandlery. Each a known method at a workplace
   (tanning pits, a rendering pot, a horner's bench), each slow or skilled enough that a specialist does it better.
5. **Trade:** light, durable, valuable goods travel (furs, wax, smoked eels, feathers, leather), linking villages that differ by land.

### 3.5 The knowledge ladder
Notice, read signs by skill, remember spots and seasons, keep counts and tallies, teach, write methods down. The engine has
noticing, readings by skill (two levels, 02zq), skill by practice, teaching, and documents (decided). What it lacks is **knowledge of
places as a thing that can be told, sold, kept secret and recorded** (a spot where the hinds lie in an east wind; the pool where the
salmon wait; the bee tree), and **records of counts** (a weir's tally by night; a warren's take by season) as documents that let a
society see a trend (catch per effort falling). Both are substrate: whether anyone keeps a tally is theirs.

### 3.6 The reach ladder (forays)
Near home, the ring (2.5 km), the wild blocks (8 km); a few units, a day out, a night at a camp, several nights; alone, with a dog,
with a crew; on foot, with a pack ox to bring meat home. A regular foray wears a path; a camp used often becomes a shelter; a
shelter with stores and people becomes a node (37.5's frontier). See §10.

### 3.8 What games and simulations show about progression
- **Anno** ties progression to needs: each population tier asks for new goods, which open longer production chains, and neglecting
  a lower tier starves the higher [9]. Lesson: a new rung matters only if something consumes it (light for the watch, seals for
  documents); demand, not unlocks, drives climbing.
- **Factorio-style chains and The Settlers** show that intermediate goods (cord, leather, tallow, glue) are what make a chain deep
  and a specialist necessary; a chain with no intermediate goods is one step and no one depends on anyone.
- **Civilization's tech tree** (animal husbandry revealing horses and pastures) shows a known capability changing what land is
  worth. In Cybertron a capability should be know-how held by people and taught, not a global unlock: one household's dogs or nets
  do not make the village's.
- **Dwarf Fortress and RimWorld** let animals be tamed, trained and bred, with training that decays without a trainer (RimWorld)
  and hunting parties, war animals and pastures (Dwarf Fortress). Lesson: trained beasts and breeding are a natural rung that
  makes a specialist (02zr §2.1).
- **Banished** hid the hunting stock behind a building and players saw unexplained falls in output (02zr §2.1): a rung that hides
  the stock teaches nothing.
- **Kenshi** lets players buy and raise animals that grow and carry, so kept animals are investments. Lesson: a kept beast should
  be capital that grows and can be lost.

### 5.3 What makes these choices real in the engine
- **Labour against capital:** every defence rung saves recurring work for an upfront build plus upkeep (fences.md's numbers). The
  watch's cost (D2-21: the night's sleep, the guard's food, units off tomorrow) is already built; barriers already exist for
  straying herds. Extending barriers and stores with physical properties (§8.1) lets the same rungs answer wild kinds.
- **Physical externalities:** a hedge round one field sends the boar to the next; a cat at one barn does nothing for the next; a
  fox culled is replaced from the ring. These are what make the larger-scale responses worth more than the sum of private ones
  (02zr M3), without any rule.
- **Specialists by skill and tools:** a rat-catcher with terriers, a hedger with a billhook, a fowler with nets, a mason with
  chisels each does a job far better than a household (D2-48's specialised tools, D2-52's skill by practice).

### 5.4 Development changes the problem, with trade-offs
- **Clearing ends the boar and the wolf near the fields, and the mast and fuel with them.** A cleared wood gives no pannage, no
  game, no firewood (forests.md: the near wood is a commons that can collapse).
- **Draining ends the ague and the flooded meadow, and the eels, fowl, reeds and cranes with them.**
- **Stone stores end rats as a store problem, not as a field one** (rats and mice in the stacks and the field edges remain).
- **Enclosure ends the open commons, and with it the commoners' wild takes** (a hedge or a pale makes a stock someone's); it also
  creates new habitat (the hedge feeds birds and hares and harbours foxes).
- **Keeping ends the hunt for a kind, and makes the kept kind a target** (a warren draws foxes, stoats and poachers; a dovecote's
  pigeons eat the neighbours' seed; a fishpond draws otters and herons).
- **Better weapons and nets end the stock faster** (a tool never adds to a stock: recipes.md §8), so better takes make restraint
  matter more.
- **Keeping dogs answers wolves and deer and brings rabies risk and the dogs' food bill.**

### 6.4 What the per-kind view shows
- **Every kind is answered by at least two families, and every family answers ten or more kinds.** The table above would be the
  same with a third of the techniques the branch has, if techniques were general.
- **The kinds that reward the most development** are those on bridges (boar, rabbit, deer, fish, fowl, bees, hawks, dogs) and those
  answered by stores and barriers (rats, mice, sparrows, foxes, stoats, boar and deer at crops). Their developed answers are
  structures and kept animals, which are capital, held by someone, built by specialists, and fed by other chains.

- **The kinds whose main value is knowledge** (ravens, jays, markers, crows, the black bane field, plants) need nothing beyond signs

- each rung of a ladder is a good or a structure made from another chain (cord, timber, stone, metal, leather, feathers, salt), so

- skill belongs to the method and carries to every kind the method takes (a good netter nets pigeons, fowl, partridge and fish);
- knowledge spreads by teaching (built) and documents (decided), so a better method can spread through a society;
- structures persist and accumulate across continued runs (wild capital, 02zr M12).

## 8. What the engine needs

Code under `cybertron2/` is 37.4's; this lists needs, not a build plan. Ordered by how much each unlocks.

### 8.1 Methods, barriers and stores with properties; kinds with traits
The core change. Instead of per-kind take tables (`takes: {"red deer": 2.5, ...}`) and per-kind targets:
- **Each kind carries body and behaviour traits** (from 02zr's constitution layer): size class, whether it climbs, leaps, burrows,
  gnaws, flies; whether it uses fixed runs; whether it flocks and follows its kind; its senses (scent, sight, hearing); when it is
  active; whether it lives in colonies, burrows, dens, roosts.
- **Each method carries properties:** what it needs from the quarry (on a run at loop height for a snare; flocking or social
  following for a net and decoy; scent left for a hound; in a burrow for a ferret or terrier; within range and seen for a bow),
  what it is selective for (size), how its gear raises yield, its crew and its danger.
- **Each barrier carries properties:** height, gap size, strength, burrow-proof; each store carries raised, sealed, stone, dry.
- **The interaction is a rule over traits against properties**, written once: a snare on a run takes whatever uses runs at its
  size; a raised stone floor stops whatever cannot climb smooth stone; a mesh stops whatever cannot pass its gap; a net over a
  feeding flock takes the social feeders. A new kind is answered by existing methods without new code; a better method answers more.
- **Guard test:** no technique, target or barrier names a kind.

### 8.2 One `take` (and the foray)
A single take verb with method, gear, crew, place and range as its object (knowledge.md's "few verbs, many objects"), resolved
against the populations there by §8.1's rule, with the search inside the act (02zr §3.5 F1). Forays (§10) are its extended form.
Skill per method (trapping, netting, archery, hound-work, falconry, stalking), carrying across kinds.

### 8.3 Kept populations in held structures
husbandry.md §4 and §7 already propose it: a population is kept when its habitat is a held structure and its food a need someone
meets; wild and kept as two rows of one lineage; wild, kept and feral as the population's state. Needed: a structure type with a
capacity for a class of kinds (pen, coop, sty, hutch, warren, dovecote, pond, hive, park, mews, kennel); stocking from a capture, a
swarm or a purchase; a harvest by technique; escape to feral when the structure decays. Dogs and hens should become kept beasts,
not fittings and store goods (02zq one-offs 3 and the dog fitting).

### 8.4 Trained beasts with a bond, and breeding
A led beast that multiplies a method (D4-58's led beasts; husbandry.md's "beast as a tool"), with a **bond** to its trainer that
decays without handling (02zr F11), and types by breeding (dogs first: scent, speed, guarding, herding, burrowing; traits on the
lineage that drift with which parents breed). A kennel is a structure; a pack is capital.

### 8.5 Goods and processing
- Add **fat** (rendered to **tallow**), **bone**, **sinew** (and gut), **glue**, **feathers and down**, **wax** and **honey**, a
  general **pelt** (with quality), **leather** (or keep leather inside recipes, as resources_and_goods.md folds it, but then the
  tanning time must be a delayed process), **cord** (bast first), **nets**, **bow** and **arrows** as grades of the weapon, **traps**
  (decided, not built).
- A general **butcher** step for any carcass (wild or kept), splitting it into parts by the kind's body; the hide's quality by how
  it was taken (a spear thrust, an arrow, a snare: RDR2's lesson, 02zr §2.1).
- Delayed processes (D4-103 part 4): tanning, retting, curing, smoking, rendering, training a beast.
- **Light** as a use of tallow, rushlights, horn panes and wax, at least for the night watch (the audit's one-off 1 already needs a
  light from a thing the state holds).

### 8.6 Knowledge
- **Spots as knowledge items**: a place, what was found there, in what conditions, when; held by a person, told, taught, sold,
  written; and fading if unused. This carries the hunter's lie map, the fowler's flight lines, the bee tree, the eel pool.
- **Records as documents**: counts and tallies people choose to keep (a weir's catch by night; a warren's take). The engine records
  nothing for them; it only lets a document hold what a person writes.
- **Readings by skill** with more than two levels (02zq's open item), so the expert's reading is a real advantage to teach.
- **Methods as teachable know-how** (built teaching; decided documents), so a better method can spread.

### 8.7 Physical externalities and evidence
- **Displacement and refill** (02zr M3): turned raiders go to the next target that stretch; vacated territories refill from the
  ring; scared flocks land on the next field.
- **Downstream loads**: a weir's take, retting's fouling, a dam's flood.
- **Evidence**: traces of people as well as animals (tracks in snow, a robbed snare, a cut fence), carcasses (D4-52), marks on
  animals and things that anyone can cut, cut over or forge, crows and geese that react to particular people. All read by the noticing
  rule and skill.
- **Possession only** (D4-23): who holds a structure, a beast, a trap's catch; the engine enforces possession and nothing more.

### 8.8 World options (for experimental arms, never default)
- A forest-law arm: certain kinds and their cover held by a named person; dogs within it lawed (they chase but do not catch);
  fences within it limited in height. The world option sets physical facts and a holder; whether anyone enforces anything is still
  people's (a holder can only act through the same verbs).
- Seeded structures from history (old hedges, a warren, a fishpond, a weir) where the land has long been settled.

### 8.9 Not needed, and not to build
- Offices, customs, closed seasons, bounties, renders, standing effects, rights of common as engine rules.
- Per-kind techniques, per-kind targets, per-kind scare lists.
- Sign caps or attention filters.

## 10. Forays and the ladders

Zach's foray (an act with a plan: where, how, with whom, how many units; resolved as a route over home parcels, the ring and the
wild blocks of `wild/areas.py`; returning a short story; with named spots people remember, tell and sell) is the take family's and
the know family's main ladder, and the reach ladder itself:

| Ladder | What a foray adds |
|---|---|
| **better taking** | method and gear chosen per trip; dogs and companions; the take resolved against what lives along the route |
| **better knowing** | spots found and named; their conditions remembered; told, sold, kept secret; a scouting trip as a foray with no take |
| **better carrying and keeping** | a pack ox or a handcart to bring meat home; preservation at a camp (drying, smoking) so a far take keeps |
| **reach** | more units reach further; nights out become camps (D2-159: the camp and the fire); a regular way becomes a path; a camp with stores becomes a seasonal station and, with people, a node (37.5's frontier) |
| **land change** | felling and charcoal on a far foray; clearing for a new field at a frontier camp |

Forays let development show without rules: a richer household sends a crew with dogs and a pack ox further and brings back more,
better kept; a knowing one goes straight to the spot. Whether a society organises regular forays, shares spots or guards them is
its own.

### 11.2 Diagnostics (measured, never acted on by the engine)
Against a null arm (the wild pack off) and between arms that differ in what capabilities a world starts with:
- **method diversity**: distinct take methods and gear used per household and per village over a run;
- **capital share of defence**: the share of losses averted by structures and kept allies against nightly labour;
- **kept share**: meat and goods from kept populations against wild ones;
- **by-product use**: the share of carcass parts used;
- **structures built** that answer wild kinds (hedges, granaries, coops, kennels, warrens, ponds);
- **spread of methods**: teaching events for wild methods, and methods adopted after being told;
- **dependence edges through wild things**: goods and services traded or given that are wild or kept animal products, and their
  makers' specialisation;
- **institutions that emerged**: agreements, sayings or rules about wild things, coded after the run, never seeded;
- **lines per agent-day** (diagnostic only), and the acted-on rate by kind.

**The developmental test:** do worlds started with more general capabilities (cord, dogs, stone, a mason) answer more kinds with
fewer distinct acts, lose less, and build more capital than worlds without, and do societies in long continued runs climb rungs
(a first kennel, a first raised granary, a first warren) on their own?
