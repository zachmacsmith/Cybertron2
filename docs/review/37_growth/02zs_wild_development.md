# 02zs: the wild in a society's economy and development (10 Nov)

A second outside deep dive, at Zach's request after reading 02zr (`02zr_wild_behaviour_critique.md`). It asks how every wild kind
fits a society's economy and its development: what each supplies and what needs it creates; the ladders a society climbs in dealing
with wild things; the tools and techniques on those ladders; how pests and predators are handled at scale; where each kind sits;
and what the engine needs so that progress is possible without being scripted. It also goes back through 02zr and
`proposals/wild_behaviour.md` under Zach's two new principles. It is a session note: it proposes, it decides nothing.

**Zach's principles for this pass (requirements):**
1. **No forced social mechanisms.** The engine gives physical substrate: stocks, structures, goods, evidence (which can be faked),
   externalities that exist physically. Whether people make rules about any of it is the experiment. Forest law is at most a
   separate experimental arm (a world option), not part of the wildlife.
2. **No bespoke checklist solutions.** A kind must not be answered only by its own ritual ("do X at time T against Y"). Responses
   must be general, transferable and improvable, so a more sophisticated society can find better and broader ways: better hunting
   methods, husbandry, farming, structures, tools, organisation at scale.

**Also decided since 02zr:** perception is never withheld or capped to save agents' attention. If too much happens, the world's
rates are fixed. Lines per agent-day may be measured as a diagnostic only.

**Time.** Y = 32: an engine day stands for about 11 real days, a season 8 engine days, a unit of work about two real days. Runs last
6 to 48 engine days; continued runs carry the long run. Anything on the ladders below that takes real years (a warren filling, a
hedge growing, a breed changing) is a continued-run matter or the world's seeded history.

## 0. Findings in brief

1. **The two principles reinforce each other.** A forced institution and a bespoke ritual are the same mistake at two scales: each
   decides for the society what the answer is. The engine's job is to make a few physical capabilities that many kinds respond to,
   each improvable along a ladder, and to let the costs of wild things fall where they physically fall. Then a richer society
   answers more kinds better with the same few capabilities, and any institution is its own.
2. **The built ecology branch already shows the checklist problem.** It has about twenty kind-named techniques: `take the young
   herons`, `take the young rooks`, `dig out the fox earth`, `shut the hens in`, `trap beavers`, `break the beaver dam`, `stalk deer`,
   `boar hunt`, `snare hares`, `set eel traps`, `fowling`, `pick slugs at dusk`, `clear the grubs off the beans`, `top the beans`,
   four `watch the field (...)` variants, `make clappers`, `make a horn`. Each answers one kind in one way. Most of them should
   become cases of about eight general families with gear, place and method as parameters (§7).
3. **Eight response families answer almost every kind** (§7): **take** (by method and gear), **exclude** (barriers and secure
   stores), **guard** (presence, light, noise, dogs), **reduce at source** (lines of traps, den and nest removal, habitat change),
   **keep and breed** (hold a population in a structure), **manage** (rotation, rest, leaving breeders, coppice), **process and
   store** (butcher, render, preserve, tan), and **know** (read signs, remember spots, count, teach, record). Each family has a
   ladder of tools and structures whose inputs come from other chains (cord, timber, stone, metal, hides, salt). One improvement on a
   family (a better net, a raised stone granary, a trained dog) answers many kinds at once.
4. **The question "which kinds does this answer?" should be answered by the kind's constitution, not by a list.** A net takes
   whatever flocks or runs into it; a snare takes whatever uses a run at its height; a scent hound finds whatever leaves scent; a
   raised floor stops whatever cannot climb a smooth stone; a mesh stops whatever cannot pass its gap. If methods and barriers carry
   physical properties and kinds carry body and behaviour traits (size, climbing, burrowing, flocking, scent), new kinds are
   answered by old capabilities automatically, and a better capability answers more of them. This is the single most important
   engine change (§8.1).
5. **Five ladders carry development** (§3): the **animal ladder** (take, tend, keep, breed, intensify), the **land ladder** (forage,
   manage, enclose, convert), the **defence ladder** (presence, barriers, structures, removal at source, landscape change), the
   **processing ladder** (eat fresh, preserve, use every part, specialised crafts, trade goods) and the **reach ladder** (near home,
   the ring, the wild blocks; a day out, a camp, a path, a new node). Knowledge (reading, remembering, teaching, recording) runs along
   all of them.
6. **Some kinds are rungs or bridges between ladders.** Boar to pig, wolf to dog, greylag to goose, mallard to duck, polecat to
   ferret, rock dove to dovecote, wild bees to skep, wild rabbits to warren, red and fallow deer to park, river fish to fishpond,
   hawks to the mews (trained, not bred). A society that keeps a bridge kind changes its relation to the wild one (pannage pigs
   compete with boar for mast; dogs guard against wolves; ferrets answer rabbits).
7. **The carcass is the economy's biggest untapped multiplier.** Every beast taken or slaughtered yields meat, fat, hide, bone,
   horn or antler, sinew and gut; birds yield feathers and down. The engine has meat, hide, antler and horn. Adding **fat (tallow),
   bone, sinew and glue, feathers and down, wax**, with a general `butcher` step, would let each take feed light, adhesives, cord,
   tools, arrows, bedding and writing. The tanner, horner, chandler and fletcher then depend on hunters and herders alike, and the
   wild and kept sides of the economy join (§2).
8. **General tools beat narrow ones.** Cord and nets, traps, dogs, the bow, fences and hedges, secure stores, fire and light, salt
   and smoke work across many kinds and situations. Narrow tools (a decoy pipe, an eel grig, a skep, falconry gear) should be
   specialisations of general ones (a structure, a trap, a hive as a held structure, a trained beast) so that knowing the general
   craft gives most of the special one (§4).
9. **Pests at scale are answered by structures and landscape, not rituals.** A household watches; a richer one fences; a village
   with stone and a mason builds raised stone granaries and walls; a society with cord and dogs keeps lines of traps, kennels and
   terriers; a large one clears, drains and encloses, which removes kinds altogether. Each step unlocks buildings, trades and supply
   chains; institutions (rotas, by-laws, bounties, wardens) are things a society might invent around them, never seeded (§5).
10. **Under the two principles, about a third of 02zr's social items survive as substrate, a third must be recast, and a third go**
    (§9). What survives: physical externalities (displacement, refill), evidence (traces, marks that can be cut or faked, crows that
    mob a trapper), structures as capital, possession of things. What goes: forest law (except as a world option), bounties as an
    office, close times, the nutting day, renders, standing from gifts, seeded customs, the attention budget.
11. **Forays fit the take, know and reach ladders at once** (§10): better hunting is better planning (spots known, gear, dogs,
    companions, range), and a regular foray becomes a path and a camp, then a frontier node.

## 1. What the two principles mean for design

### 1.1 Substrate, not institution
**The engine may hold:**
- stocks and populations, and their physical dynamics;
- structures (fences, walls, pales, granaries, pens, warrens, ponds, hives, weirs) with physical properties, condition and capacity;
- goods and their processing;
- techniques with gear, place, method and skill;
- physical externalities: a scared flock lands elsewhere; a vacated territory refills from outside; a weir takes what would have
  passed downstream; retting fouls water downstream;
- evidence: traces, carcasses, marks cut on an animal or a thing (which can be cut by anyone, cut over, or forged), crows that mob
  the person who trapped them, tracks in snow, and the readings people make of them by skill;
- possession (D4-23): who holds a thing or a place, as the engine already enforces it;
- world options for experiments (a forest-law arm: certain kinds and their cover held by a named person; dogs within it lawed).

**The engine must not hold:** offices, customs, closed seasons, bounties, renders, tithes, rights of common, standing or prestige
effects, or any rule about who may take what. People can make all of those with the engine's agreements, records, sayings and
force; whether they do is what experiments measure.

**The test:** if the engine's code names a social role, a rule or a payment, it is forcing. If it names a physical state that
people may make rules about, it is substrate.

### 1.2 General capabilities, not rituals
**The test for every kind and mechanism:** does the society have general, transferable, improvable ways to respond, or only a
kind-specific ritual? Three sub-tests:
1. **Transfer:** the same technique, gear, structure or knowledge answers several kinds (a mesh against weasels, rats and
   sparrows; a scent hound against deer, boar, foxes, hares; a raised stone floor against rats, mice and damp).
2. **Improvement:** there is a better version reachable through other chains (wattle hurdle, then hedge, then stone wall; snare,
   then purse net and ferret, then a warren; spear, then bow, then crossbow), and the better version answers more or answers better.
3. **Scale:** a larger or richer group can do something a household cannot (a drive, a long hedge, a stone barn, a kennel, a decoy
   pond, a fishpond), and the scale is physical (many hands, much material, a structure), not a rule.

A kind that fails all three is a ritual and should be redesigned or cut.

### 1.3 What this changes in 02zr's framing
02zr proposed a three-layer framing (constitution, encounter, social footprint). The constitution layer survives and becomes more
important: it is what lets general capabilities answer kinds without lists (finding 4). The encounter layer survives. The social
footprint layer is **recast**: it is no longer a design requirement that each kind create an institution, but a **diagnostic
prediction**: what physical externality, evidence, structure or good the kind adds, and what a null arm would therefore lack in the
state if people respond to it. Nothing in the engine acts on it. The attention budget is withdrawn (lines per agent-day remain a
diagnostic).

### 2.2 Needs, and which are real now
A good matters only if something consumes it (02j; resources_and_goods.md's tests). Under that test:
- **Real now:** food (meat, fat, eggs, honey, fish); **light** for night work and the watch (tallow, rushlights, horn lanterns; the
  audit already requires a watch's light to be a thing the state holds); hides for the built uses; dung for fields; guarding and
  herding by dogs; draught.
- **Real once decided items are built:** parchment and **wax seals** for documents (D2-24 decided documents); **feathers and sinew**
  for bows once bows exist (weapons are decided; their grades are open); glue for arrows and joinery.
- **Waiting on a parked or future need:** furs and wool for clothing (D2-10 parked); castoreum and other remedies for illness;
  oil for light beyond tallow.
- **Never forced:** "status goods". Venison, furs, swans or a hawk become status only if people treat them so. The engine can make
  them scarce, durable, visible and hard to get (substrate); it must not attach standing to them (§9).

### 2.4 What this says
- **The carcass is the hub.** One beast feeds four or five chains. A `butcher` step that splits a carcass into meat, fat, hide, bone,
  sinew and (for deer) antler would serve wild and kept animals alike and raise the value of every take without any kind-specific
  code.
- **Light and documents are the two existing needs most dependent on animals.** Light needs fat or wax or horn; documents need hide
  and wax. Both are already required by decided or audited rules.
- **The bow is the main improvable hunting tool** and the most dependent: on yew, goose feathers, sinew or hemp, glue and metal.
- **Cord is the hidden spine of the take family** (snares, purse nets, clap nets, flight nets, fishing nets, seines, bowstrings).
  Nets are deferred in economy_later.md waiting for fibre; bast cord from lime trees (a coppice product) is the cheaper first rung
  and does not need flax.
