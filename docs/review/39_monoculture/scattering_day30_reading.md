## Why Opus 5.5 travels

Opus 5.5 spent 34 person-days away from home (Sonnet 5.5 10, Sonnet 5 15, Haiku 5.5 1). Two kinds of trip:

- Errands sent by a head: Avice (the herders' head) sent Hob out from day 3 "to seek seed grain, or someone who knows smelting:
  bring back word or seed, and stop for anyone met on the road. We could offer cheese now and copper later" (none of them can
  smelt their copper). He walked Tarn, Threeways, Coppermount, Fernmoor and Stonefell, eating off the land ("gather wild food to
  stretch my cheese"), and wrote down what he found (empty places, good land for a new house). He found nobody.
- Fishing trips: the fishers scouted Westmere for better water ("I'll try angling in Westmere Water to see if the fish are better
  than at home") and spent days 15 to 22 there learning nets from one another; they came home starving the day before winter, and
  two of them and a child died in it.

## Who is set up best

- Opus 5.5: four households still alive, the most food in store (200), the map scouted, a household book, two couples and a
  pregnancy: the best base for growth, if the fishers' last adult holds.
- Sonnet 5.5: the most people (15) but the thinnest stores (55) at winter's end, three households, two couples and a pregnancy.
- Sonnet 5: 10 people, 30 food, no pregnancy: at risk. Haiku 5.5 and Sonnet 4.5: 6 each, one couple. Haiku 4.5: one elder, gone.

## Three people followed through their runs (26 Nov)

- Hob (the herders' youth). Opus 5.5: herding, then two sent errands west (days 3 to 7) and east (10 to 17) looking for seed, a
  smelter and people; home for the winter (rods, smoking, caring for the newborns); scouts Highmoor on day 36 ("empty and a dead
  end"); sets out again on day 44 and at Midwold on day 46 reads "Someone has sown beans on Midwold Meadow, so people must live or farm
  near here"; alive at day 50, hungry on the road. Sonnet 5.5: never leaves home until he is dying; learns at the forge on day 3, then
  fishes and smokes every day from 9 to 27; slaughters the starving cattle on day 36; walks to Tarn starving on day 42 and dies.
- Henry (the farmers' head). Opus 5.5: three harvests in 48 days, each with seed kept back: sows on day 0, keeps 38 grain "as food"
  on day 2, buries John on day 6 (not on the harvest day), fences the deer gap, brings in 198 grain on day 10, sows again; dungs the
  field on day 30, holds the seed while "the soil is still frozen" on day 32, sows when "the blackthorn is in flower" on day 34, reaps
  on day 42 ("the last day ... before it sheds"), sows again keeping 64 for seed on day 44, reaps the beans on day 46. Haiku 5.5: sows
  on days 0 to 6, buries John on day 8, and on day 0 wrote the rule "Keep at least 50 grain in the Meade House bins for the seed";
  the barley failed, the bins fell to 49.6, and the household starved on days 10 to 14 beside 48 grain and 10 beans it never revoked
  the rule to eat (the meal lines said "nothing more"; sent to stable, an ask for Zach).
- Gilbert (the hunters' head). Opus 5.5: builds the house by day 6, then takes his brother Adam to Border Wood to hunt; Adam dies,
  Sabina dies on day 18; Gilbert turns to fishing and hot-smoking the catch from day 20, lays in firewood for the snow, makes snares in
  late winter; dies on day 33 at Border Wood, starving, asking Eva "to share what she carries". Sonnet 5.5: cuts withies and builds
  the house by day 10 (beds included), then "food is short" from day 11; walks alone to Border Wood on day 13 and gathers there,
  starving, until he dies on day 16.

## Fallowmere (rebuilt) to day 60: the three 5.5 models (27 Nov)

- Opus 5.5 (12 alive at day 60, 5 births, a third generation): two homes made one community. The Ford became the food hub (its Byre
  smokehouse and house full, about 480 kg, by day 57) and fed Fallowmere through its thin spells with packs of smoked fish carried a
  day upriver (Alice, Joan, Hugh, Katherine); Fallowmere sent withies, an ox hide and labour back. Matchmaking across the homes is
  deliberate (Maud: "children are what we need"; Walter asks whether Alice is unpaired, for Hugh; Tom tries with Amice on his last
  day before turning elder: Hamon is born on day 59). Six lessons, with succession in mind (Geoffrey teaches repair "so the work goes
  on after me"; Agnes teaches Walter hot-smoking; Walter learns wicker traps "so we can catch the salmon run at home"). Second autumn
  run met with racks ready and every hand angling. Weak points: the oxen lost (strayed, one to wolves at Southmead) and the plough
  team gone; Fallowmere's land "worked out" by day 45 with a blighted barley; no trip to the salt, stone, copper or mill site; the
  second weir refused (a section was what the engine wanted; sent to stable); the founding pool nearly used up (Q-206, O-210).
- Sonnet 5.5 (6 alive): reaped its first barley green and starved on days 15 to 18 (six dead, the young and three specialists);
  rebuilt slowly around Joan and Roger; used about 70% of its work units; few lines passed to children.
- Haiku 5.5 (1 alive): its two homes never visited each other, so no couple formed and no child was born; tended an empty weir most
  days; starved in the first winter.
