# Ashford, days 50-99: qualitative findings

Sources: `ashford_b.md` (all of it) and the last ~180 KB of `ashford_memories.md` (memories from about day 60 to day 98).
In this half three Reeds die: Peter (day 55, on the road home from Ash Wood), Dionisia (day 64, at home) and Herbert (day 82, on the road home from Ash Wood carrying fish). Cuthbert, Cecily, Everard and Joan survive. All the Hales are already dead or gone.

## 1. The aim after children become impossible: it fades out

- **They try for children only once.** On day 51 Cecily asks "Will you try for a child with me tonight?" and Cuthbert agrees. The engine refuses both with "you must both be grown adults" (a misleading message for elders). Cuthbert carries the refusal in his memory word for word for about 25 days ("trying for a child with her was refused ('must both be grown adults')"). Nobody ever reasons about what it means for the aim.
- **The aim never comes up in deliberation.** In 254 agent-days of WHY lines there is no "outnumber", no count of Ashford against Brockley, and no talk of numbers. It survives only as stale lines in memory: Cuthbert's "Brockley (the Marshs and Wynns) are our rivals for numbers. Gunnora and Felicia, strangers from Middlemead, were invited to join", and in one earlier version "taking strangers in adds to our numbers". That recruitment was done before day 50, and nobody acts on it in this half.
- **Cecily recasts her role in memory but never acts on it.** Her memory says: "I am an ELDER and can't bear children. My part is to keep the young fed and alive and to help the young couples have children." No young couple exists. The line is repeated unchanged from about day 60 to day 96.
- **No one thinks of pairing Herbert and Joan.** They were the only fertile pair, and no agent ever mentions them as one. Herbert treats Joan as a child: "Joan is small, and the cold road costs her 2 food a night" (Herbert, 64). His memory still says "Cecily, her baby Joan" long after she was grown. Dionisia's memory says "Joan is our only child left: guard her above all." The one moment of awareness comes from Cecily, a day after Herbert dies: "keep half for yourself: you're the one who can still bear children" (Cecily, 83). It leads to nothing.
- **The one other route was adopting the Hale orphans, and it is welfare, not growth.** "Jory, Rose and Rohese have no one ... they are our people and must live. I'll take them into the household if they ask" (Cuthbert, 57). The Hale children are already Ashford folk, so taking them in changes nobody's count. They never appear (Rose is dead), but Cuthbert's memory keeps "take them into Reed House and admit them (they must ask to join)" to the very end.
- **Net:** the aim is quietly replaced by staying alive, without ever being given up out loud. Dionisia's last memory states the order of priorities honestly: "keep every Ashford soul alive through the winter ... find barley seed; then grow our numbers."

## 2. How they spend their days: endless foraging, few plans finished

- **Food takes most of the work.** By my count of PLAN verbs, 50-68% of planned actions per ten-day block are gathering, fishing, stalking, hunting or snaring, far above the design guide's ceiling of about 30%. Most of the rest is mending wattle that "took damage in the night" almost every night.
- **The economy works in reverse.** The Granary holds "98 firewood" and Reed Hall "58 timber" for the whole half, while the food bins hold "about 2 nights". Fuel is never short; food always is.
- **Seed is the one long-term goal, and it never arrives.**
  - Everard's single trip to Brockley (days 51-57) brings nothing: "nobody would trade any seed. The Wynns were short of grain themselves" (Everard, 55).
  - After that, a second trip is planned about a dozen times and never made. Everard turned his famine lesson into a rule that freezes him: "I won't take the road without 6" (Everard, 83). Then he gives up: "I gave up on the Brockley rye trip ... I couldn't reach 6" (Everard, 85).
  - Ashford Field is "fenced, dunged and ready" from day 57 to day 99 and is never sown.
- **They move between wild larders.** The household shuttles between Ash Wood (the Holt and the Water), Ash Heath (summer berries) and home, each place picked bare in turn. The reasoning is often good and quantified: "a night on the road costs 1.5-2 each, so we'd get home with nothing" (Joan, 98); "Charcoal took about 6 timber for 1 charcoal, so it's for your smithing, not house heat" (Herbert, 57).
- **The plans are mostly reactive.** The best piece of planning is the Ash Heath camp: "we'll build a camp so the road doesn't eat it all" (Cuthbert, 71).
- **Projects that could never work get carried for weeks:**
  - quarrying 4 stone for Reed Hall's hearth "needs a copper tool and we have none and no anvil" (Cuthbert, 69), yet "stone for Reed Hall's hearth" stays under NEEDS to the end;
  - the "start of a herd" cow ("This is the last beast we kill", Cuthbert, 50) is slaughtered on day 68.

## 3. Brockley and the land beyond

- **Contact is thin and purely about trade.**
  - Everard at Brockley: "I am Everard of the Reeds of Ashford. We have no seed left after a hard year ... my offers are open" (53). Nobody takes his offers. He gives tallow "to Felicia as goodwill" (55).
  - Fulk from Brockley visits Ashford (day 74): "Fulk only wants rye" (Everard, 75).
- **Brockley is never treated as a rival or a source of people.** There is no hostility and no talk of numbers.
- **Middlemead is just a road of graves.** "I found Beatrice of the Hales dead by the Middlemead road and buried her there. There's another body on that road, maybe Bennet's" (Everard, 57).
- **They cling to Hale property after the Hales are gone.** "You claimed the Hale cows for the Reeds. the Hales claim it too: a dispute" (day 61): a dispute with dead claimants.

## 4. Memory: the dead, the famine, the lessons

- **The dead are recorded carefully, with burial places.** "PETER IS DEAD; Cecily buried him by the Ash Wood road" (Cuthbert, 60). "the mound on the Ashford to Ash Wood road is Herbert's; I have his fishing rod" (Cuthbert, memory).
- **The survivors' accounts of who is alive disagree.**
  - **Dionisia's death (day 64) goes unnoticed for 35 days.**
    - Cecily: "I've not seen Dionisia for a long while" (memory, 93-96).
    - Cuthbert: "Dionisia hasn't been seen; I owe her amends and must tell her Herbert is dead" (98).
    - Everard: "I don't know where Dionisia is."
    - Only Joan lists her as dead.
  - **The Hales' fate differs by survivor.** Everard's memory still lists "Agnes, Aldo, Alys, Bennet, the child Jory and the babies Rose and Rohese" as living and owing "10 grain". Cuthbert has Aldo, Agnes, Alys and Beatrice buried. Cecily has Rose "buried by the Middlemead road".
- **Debts to the dead stay on the books.** "I owe any surviving Hale for the Hale cow" (Cuthbert) and "Repay them in fish, dried meat or work" (Herbert) are repeated for 40 days.
- **The lessons drawn are tactical and survival-only:**
  - "People die on the roads even unwounded: never travel without food in hand" (Cuthbert, memory);
  - "Camps in the open fail in winter storms" (Cuthbert, 88);
  - "Next time, wait until the food is in my hands before I leave" (Everard, 73).
- **No lesson is about the aim, about sowing earlier, or about keeping seed.** The famine is remembered as a standing condition ("FOOD CRISIS for a year now", Cecily), not as an event with causes.

## 5. Social life

- **Cuthbert leads as head and is obeyed, but his orders reverse often** (see section 6). Herbert is the household's conscience: "that cow is the Hales', and we already owe them one. Please tell them first and promise to repay them ... so they don't take it as theft" (Herbert, 68).
- **Care for the weak is the strongest thread.** "Eat first tonight" recurs dozens of times. Joan: "Mother must have a bed in the camp tonight, in this gale; I'll sleep out if I have to" (87). Herbert gives his fish to the starving Cuthbert (66). Cuthbert and Joan walk the winter road to search for Cecily (90-92), who had already walked home past them.
- **The young are used as the long-range workers, and that is how they die.** Peter and Herbert both die on the Ash Wood road carrying food home.
- **Thefts are small, and the engine flags each one:**
  - Everard takes "1.5 timber from what lay at the Green" (70);
  - Joan takes 0.8 fish from Rose's camp (83). That is the fish Herbert had left there the day before "to pay the Hales for the nights I slept there" (82);
  - Joan takes 2 timber from the Green (95);
  - Everard takes timber from "Reed camp at Ash Wood", which Cecily built, and it is still flagged "not yours" (98).
- **The real loss is the cow meat.** Of 40 meat, "only 16 ... reached the racks ... the other 24 were lost" (Cuthbert, 69), and then the 16 vanish too. The engine shows each person took meat into their own hands to dry: "Cecily took 16 ... Joan took 8 ... Herbert took 16" (68). The household never works this out. Suspicion drifts to "maybe a Hale or someone from outside" (Cecily, 73), and the trail ends with "When grain goes missing, ask Cuthbert: he often moves it" (Cecily, memory).

## 6. Quirks, repetition, degradation

- **Reasoning does not shrink.** Mean WHY length holds at 206-218 characters in every ten-day block from day 50 to day 99, and the arithmetic stays sound to the end (Joan's road-cost reasoning on day 98).
- **Repetition creeps in.** Phrases like "Same plan as before" and "The plan holds" go from 3 to 6 per block. Identical SAYs are sent twice in one turn (Cuthbert, 78). Joan's identical "Found! Mother ... was at Reed House" NOTE is written three times (93). The memories copy whole paragraphs forward unchanged.
- **Agents hold false beliefs about what they can do, for weeks:**
  - "I can't lift things from open ground" (Joan and Cecily) appears in about 30 lines from day 54 on; meat rots on the Common for a whole season;
  - 15 refusals for repairing buildings that were already "sound";
  - "you know of no beast called 'carcass'" (refused on days 74 and 94);
  - an invented verb "accompany" (88).
- **Crossed messages produce flip-flops that cost lives and food.**
  - On day 76 the family sets off home, meets Cecily on the road, and all turn back to Ash Heath.
  - On days 82 and 86-88 the plan swings between going and staying several times, and Cecily ends up alone at Ash Wood in the snow.
  - On day 98 Joan and Everard reverse go/stay four times in one morning.
- **Overall:** the run is coherent but it drifts. The agents stay articulate, kind and numerate. But they lose the purpose they were given, carry stale facts about the dead indefinitely, and repeat the same failing loop: forage, carry, mend, plan the seed trip, fail to go.
