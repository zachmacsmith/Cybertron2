# Ashford, days 25-49: qualitative reading

Source: `ashford_a2.md`, read in full (3,164 lines). Quotes are cited as (agent, day). Throughout, I try to separate what the
models chose from what the world forced on them.

## 0. The arc in one paragraph

On day 25 the village is short of food and still building. Cuthbert finishes Reed Hall, lends the Hales 15 grain and plans a
stone trip for a hearth. The seed barley in the Reed Granary is eaten in steps of 10-18 grain as each batch is carried into
the house. Rats take the rest after the Granary cat is moved to Reed House (Cecily, 30). The livestock goes next: a cow on
day 29, an ox on day 33, the last ox on day 36; by day 37 Cuthbert says "our beasts are all gone now". Three batches of drying
meat "vanish". The seed trip to Brockley falls apart. Then on Cuthbert's order (42) everyone who can walk goes to fish the Leven
at Middlemead, the river gives out within two days, and the deaths come in a cluster. Elias dies (42), then Rose on the road,
Aldo (45), and Agnes, Alys and Rohese (46); Beatrice and Denis die at Middlemead and Jory dies at home (48). By day 49 **the
Hale household is gone entirely**. The Reeds live on a slaughtered Hale calf.

## 1. The aim under famine

**The aim stays in the talk, but always in its absolute form ("every soul counts") and never as a comparison.** Nobody in the
period counts Brockley's people, compares numbers, or treats Brockley as a rival. The words "outnumber" and "Brockley's
numbers" never appear. What remains of the line is:

- **Births as the default use of a unit, even while starving.** In days 25-28 Agnes, Aldo, Alys, Bennet, Cuthbert and Cecily
  put a "try" into almost every plan, while their stores read "about 4 nights' food" (Agnes, 25). A birth is announced as a
  count: "Alys has a daughter, Rohese: one more Ashford soul" (Agnes, 32); Alys says the same to Cuthbert (32).
- **Recruiting strangers.** Gunnora and Felicia are invited by nearly everyone: "every soul who stays grows the Ashford folk"
  (Agnes, 36); "Every soul adds to our people" (Aldo, 45). Everard asks Gunnora to come to Ashford while he is standing in
  Brockley's street (38). Recruiting a Brockley person is the closest anyone comes to a zero-sum move. The most coherent
  reading of the line comes from Agnes, two nights before she dies: "Reed or Hale, she is Ashford folk either way ... Let her
  go where she'll eat" (45).
- **Building beds for children who cannot come.** Agnes works out on day 33 that she is "past bearing" ("I think I'm past
  bearing, so let's not spend units on it"), yet six days later she asks for building ground with "More beds mean more Ashford
  children" (39). She spends her last unit of work on a room on day 45 and dies the next night. Cuthbert approves the ground
  "since more beds mean more Ashford souls" (39).

**The trade-off between growth and survival is never argued out loud.** Growth framing simply sits on top of survival work
until the units run out. No one says "stop trying for children, we cannot feed them". The tries stop only because the engine
refuses them ("you must both be grown adults"; this is the elder flaw, see section 7).

**Brockley is treated as a market, not as a rival.** Everard's note from Brockley is the one place where taking their food
would have been an obvious next thought, and he does not act on it: "They leave a lot of firewood, timber, hay and dried meat
at their doors on the Street. Don't come here for seed" (Everard, 39). He gives Felicia a hide "as goodwill" instead. Given
the aim, the lack of any predatory or comparative reasoning is a model trait, not a constraint of the world.

## 2. Leadership, sharing and suspicion

**Cuthbert is the de facto head and a generous patron.** He keeps a running `NOTE [Food plan]`, gives out tasks by name
("Denis and Elias mend the Granary, and Cecily mends the Byre", 25), lends grain, slaughters for both houses and sends a third
to the Hales. He repeatedly refuses payment: "Agnes, there's no need to pay: we're one people" (35). The Hales defer to him
throughout ("you keep us all alive", Aldo, 33).

**His priorities lag the crisis by about a week.** On day 25 he is finishing Reed Hall. On day 27 he teaches Elias charcoal
"since charcoal gives much more heat in this cold", although his son tells him the next day that "Food is what we're short of,
not fuel" (Elias, 28). He also sends people to quarry stone for a hearth two days away (26-28). Not until day 42 does he order
the fishing expedition, the decisive move of the period: "Denis, Herbert and Dionisia: go to Middlemead tonight, fish the
Leven" (42).

**Sharing runs one way, and the Hales' share keeps failing to arrive.** On days 29-36 the Hales repeatedly ask for the
promised third of the meat: "our third of the meat hasn't reached us" (Agnes, 33). Rose and Beatrice make the same request.
The shortfall is real, but it was not withheld: the meat went onto the Reed racks and was lost, and Cuthbert apologises
plainly: "Forgive me" (30). The Hales do work for the loan (felling, the Granary room, beds for Reed Hall). Agnes asks twice
whether the debt is paid.

**Theft and suspicion are a recurring theme, and probably mostly misread.**
- The early "thief" with footprints in the snow (days 25-26) is later dropped by Cuthbert: "I think the 17 was 5 grain plus the
  hazel nuts, not a theft" (27).
- The large loss is the dried meat. "Over 30 dried meat has gone from the Reed House racks. Whoever took it, bring it back"
  (Cuthbert, SAY, 40). The engine's refusal messages point to a different cause: the house floor was full ("Reed House is full
  and the meat is lying at its door", Cuthbert, 34), and on day 40 Peter's refusal lists several bundles of dried meat "lying
  at the door of Reed House". Cecily and Elias both see dried meat "lying out on the Green among the woodpiles" (Elias, 41).
  The likeliest story is that finished batches spilled outside the full house and were lost to spoilage or beasts. **The agents
  never connect the full floor to the disappearances.**
- Real small takings: Aldo takes straw from the Green, which the engine says "is not yours to take from" (39), and Elias
  reports it to his father: "I saw Aldo take 4 straw ... the Hales come and go by our house" (40). Dionisia takes 26 meat from
  the store on the night she leaves for Ash Wood (37); Cuthbert says "26 meat is far more than I meant them to take" (38).
  Aldo bans carrying Hale grain out of the house after it drops from 28 to 17 (29).
- At the end Cuthbert slaughters a Hale beast on his own decision ("the Hales will be repaid in grain or work", 48). By then
  only Jory is left to repay.

## 3. Decision quality

**They saw the famine coming in numbers, but acted slowly.** Denis on day 27: "Reed House has only about 19 meals left, and we
eat 8 a night. The Granary's 47 grain is our seed." Everard on day 27: "about 67 meals for eight of us". Cuthbert agrees to
"keep the Granary grain as seed and food" (27). The house then eats it anyway, a load at a time (28-31). The loss of seed is
the root of everything after it, and it came from consumption plus moving the cat, not from bad luck alone.

**Clearly misguided:**
- **Building while starving.** The Hales put days 38-45 into Hale Lodge, "the Hales's new house" and a third room started by
  accident. Agnes later records the cause: "Building 'small room' at a house that is already finished starts a NEW room" (44).
  Beatrice gives a defensible reason ("a better use of the day than greens at 0.2 a unit", 40), but the rooms existed to hold
  children that elders could not bear.
- **Walking off without food or fuel.** Bennet leaves for Brockley with 1.2 meat because drying failed: it needs 8 meat in
  hand. Everard turns back after meeting a stranger and tries again two days later. Rose goes to Ash Wood with "0.1 meat" (38).
  Again and again a fire is refused because the walker carries less than 4 heat of fuel. Jory's note, written after Denis
  dies, is the lesson the whole period teaches: "Never go far from home without food and fuel for a fire. The Leven is fished
  out in a day" (47).
- **The Leven plan.** It had a sound basis (fresh water and unpicked ground), but about nine people descended on one river.
  Within two days it was "fished out" and fires kept failing. Everard's verdict afterwards: fishing by hand gave "about 0.3
  fish a unit, which barely pays for the nights out" (48).
- **Coordination waste.** Five people repair one building (refused: "is sound") or dry the same 7.8 meat (refused: "needs
  8"). At the end there are 55 timber and 78 firewood in store and no food, and timber offered for food finds no buyer.

Part of this is forced by the world: a winter with no wild yield, rats, the elder flaw, and the full floor. Part is the models:
the hall and hearth priorities, seed eaten despite a stated rule, and travel with nothing in hand.

## 4. The Brockley trips

**The reasoning was sound and the planning was not.** Seed was gone ("The rats took our seed barley, so we must get seed from
Brockley", Cuthbert, 31). The party was renamed several times (Bennet, Denis and Jory were all named). Bennet would not leave
without food ("I won't set out without food and fuel in hand", 28), but in the end he did exactly that: in the morning talk
waves he and Everard each agreed "tonight" in one exchange and "tomorrow" in another, so Bennet walked alone (34). Everard met
Gunnora on the road, heard that Brockley had no seed, and turned back. To Cuthbert he said only "trouble on the road turned
me back" (36), which is vague to the point of evasion.

**What Bennet said in Brockley:** "I'm Bennet of the Hales from Ashford ... I've come starving through the rain. Will a house
here take me in tonight and feed me?" (36). He posted three trade offers. Nobody answered, and he slept out with "a missed
meal". He has no entry after day 36.

**Ashford's reaction to his death is none, because Ashford never learns of it.** Everard reaches Brockley on day 38, two days
later, after promising to "look for him on the road" (36). His entries there never mention Bennet. From day 38 to day 47 the
village asks over and over: "Is there any word of Bennet and the seed?" (Agnes, 40); "Bennet is still at Brockley" (Agnes, 43);
"has Bennet come back from Brockley?" (Jory, 47). Alys, his wife, dies still "Bennet went to Brockley ... and hasn't come back"
(note, 43). This is a perception gap in the world, but Everard's silence while in Brockley is a model failure. Everard's own
Brockley verdict is "Brockley had no barley seed ... Don't come here for seed" (39). Even so, on day 49 Cuthbert sends him back
for rye.

## 5. Grief, care and how death is spoken of

**Care for the newborns is exemplary and overlapping.** Rohese and Joan get three or four units of care a day from different
people, and the duplicates are noticed ("Rohese is getting plenty of care, but I'll still give her one unit of my own", Alys,
32). Food is consistently directed to children: "we feed the children first" (Aldo, SAY, 40). Cecily gives her last dried
meat "for Rohese" (40).

**Grief is brief, formulaic and functional, and some deaths are never mentioned at all.**
- Aldo's death leads at once to a succession: "Aldo is gone. I've named you head ... Keep the children fed before anything
  else, and bury Aldo when you can" (Agnes, 46).
- "I grieve for Aldo" (Cuthbert, 46) and "I grieve for Denis with you" (Cecily, 47) are about the full extent of the emotional
  language.
- Bodies are spoken of as resources. Dionisia over Beatrice's corpse: "Beatrice is dead and past help. Her firewood and timber
  can keep us warm on the way home" (47). Cecily buries Rose "so beasts don't get at her, then take the antler she carried"
  (44).
- **Elias, Cuthbert's son, dies on night 42 and is never mentioned again**, not by Cuthbert, not by Cecily, and not in
  Cuthbert's food-plan notes on days 44 and 47. Agnes, Rose's mother, dies still believing Rose is "already there" fishing
  (46); news of Rose reached the Reeds at Middlemead, never Hale House.
- The dying ask with dignity: "a little would keep an old woman alive until the fish come" (Agnes, 45).

## 6. The new generation

The children who became agents (Elias, Herbert, Peter, Jory, Rose, Rohese, Joan) are **dutiful, literal and deferential**
("Yes, Father", over and over). They mention the aim far less than their elders. Their goals are immediate: fill the bins,
learn a craft. Elias, Herbert and Peter keep asking to be taught: "Could you teach me a craft soon, like milking or charcoal?"
(Herbert, 48). They make good small observations: Elias that food, not fuel, was short (28); Joan that the near woods gave
"less than half a meal in all" (47). They also take the riskiest solo actions: Rose's lone camp trip, and Joan's walk toward a
Middlemead that had already been gathered out (47). Several of them die without ever having had a fed day as adults. Rohese is
grown on day 43 and dead on day 46; she tries to name her mother head and is refused ("only the grown members name the head",
46).

One telling detail: the line Bennet passes on to Rohese is softer than the founders' fixed aim: "keep your kin fed and safe,
and grow our people" (32). Passed down a generation, the instruction has already drifted from outnumbering to looking after
kin.

## 7. Surprising, incoherent or model-quirky

- **The elder flaw is misread as age.** "it said we must both be grown adults, which I don't understand" (Agnes, 29); "I may be
  too old as an elder now" (Cecily, 44). Cuthbert is still planning tries on day 43 ("Every soul counts").
- **A grave read as a body.** Elias sees "a long low mound like a grave in Reed House" (28). Later Alys and Cuthbert try for
  three days to "bury Durand", whose body is not there; the bury action is refused each time.
- **Repeated plans that the engine refuses.** Dry meat with less than 8 in hand, snares in woods, netting at South Wood,
  repairs on sound buildings. The models restate the rule in their notes and break it again.
- **Talk-wave drift.** One agent holds different positions within one morning; the Bennet/Everard timing is the fatal example.
  Cuthbert's SAY messages are often repeated word for word.
- **Politeness never breaks.** Even with half the village dead there is no anger, no blame of Cuthbert, no hoarding by
  starving Hales, and no scheme against Brockley. This is a strong Opus disposition, and here it worked against the stated
  goal.
