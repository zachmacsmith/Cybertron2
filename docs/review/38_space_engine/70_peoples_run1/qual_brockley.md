# Brockley (Sonnet founders): qualitative reading, days 0-99

Sources: brockley_a.md (days 0-49) and brockley_b.md (days 50-99), read in full (783 agent-days); brockley_memories.md sampled early, middle and late, and searched for the aim, Ashford, Bennet, Everard and children.

**Corrections to the brief.** Brockley tried for children twice, not once. Emma and Fulk tried on days 0-1, which gave Orabel. Hamon and Hawise tried on days 22-23, which gave Ellen, born the night of day 26. The three survivors at day 99 are Hamon and the two Brockley-born girls, Orabel and Ellen. Both girls went from "youth" to "elder" with no adult stage between (Orabel: "I am Orabel, an elder of the Marsh household", memory day 73). The other founders died in this order: Gervase (day 34, on the road to Ashford), Hawise (about day 32, on the Brock Wood road), Ivo (about day 36, on the same road), Gunnora (about day 42, at the Isel in Brock Wood), Jocelin (57), Felicia (66), Isolda (85), Fulk (87, on the road home from Middlemead) and Emma (93).

## 1. The aim: picked up once, then turned into "keep everyone fed"

The aim does show up in reasoning, but only in the first days and only as a reason to try for a child: "Having children serves our people's aim" (Emma, 0); "try for a child with Emma to grow our people" (Fulk, 0). The word "outnumber" appears exactly once in all 100 days of memory: "grow the household, since the aim is to outnumber other peoples" (Hamon memory, day 0). No reasoning line in either digest says "outnumber". None compares Brockley's head count with Ashford's.

**Why trying stopped.** Food is the explicit reason, and it was written down as a rule from day 0. Hamon carried the same clause for 21 days: "Try for children with Hawise when food allows" (memory days 0, 3, 15, 18). He acted on it only once the Marsh household's ox meat filled the Wynn store: "try for children with Hawise now that food allows, since more of our people is the aim" (memory 21). On day 24 he wrote "Hawise is near her time, so no more trying". After Hawise disappeared, the clause dropped out of his memory altogether.

Emma and Fulk never mention trying again after Orabel was born on day 4. Neither does the third couple, Felicia and Gervase ("Gervase my partner"), nor Isolda and Ivo ("Ivo my husband"). For them the aim was not argued against; it just faded.

You can watch it fade in Fulk's standing priorities:
- day 9: "grow our people: protect Orabel first"
- day 45: "find food ..., then grow our people"
- days 78-84: a closing fragment, "Then grow our people."

Others recast the aim as survival:
- "Keep kin fed and alive; people are what count" (Emma, memories 58-67)
- "keep every Brockley soul fed, children first" (Felicia)
- "keep all our folk fed and alive so the Brockley folk grow in number" (Jocelin)

By the end, the late memories of Hamon, Orabel and Ellen carry no aim at all. The one decision that clearly protected the aim was a refusal to defect. Asked to join the Reeds at Ashford, Gunnora wrote: "My people come first, so I stay with the Marshs" (memory 38). Felicia also declined, but gave no reason.

## 2. Everard and Bennet: noticed and written down, but nobody acted

**Everard's first visit (around day 21, asking for rye).** He appears only in the nightly memories, as a fact to be wary of: "a stranger; unknown intent" (Hamon, 21); "find out who he is and be careful" (Hawise, 21). Afterwards: "Everard of Ashford came on autumn day 6 asking for rye seed and left; we have no rye" (Emma, 25). Nobody's plan for the day ever turned to him.

**Everard's later visits (days 35-38 and 53) were treated as trading chances.**
- "Do you carry any barley seed or grain?" (Felicia SAY, 38)
- "sell nothing cheaply to Everard" (Fulk, 39)
- "we should not give him our hide cheaply" (Gunnora, 38)

Everard "asked for a place by the fire, offering a hide" (Gunnora memory 38), and no one hosted him. Later he came "asking after Bennet of the Hales, who came for seed and has not gone home" (Hamon memory 54). Nobody told him anything.

**Bennet.** Bennet first appears at Middlemead, where two Brockley people dealt with him purely as a way to shed weight:
- "Bennet is a traveller carrying timber and I have no quarrel with him ... I'll take the swap" (Gervase, 34)
- "I give 2 tallow and the hedge fruit to Bennet to get under the limit" (Gunnora, 35)

When he arrived starving at Brockley on days 36-37, no reasoning line or speech on those days mentions him. The perception did reach the nightly memories, in detail:

> "Bennet of the Hales ... arrived starving ... he asked for a house to take him in and feed him, and for seed barley. I do not know where he is now or what was decided." (Emma, memory 37)

That same night Fulk's memory gives the Marsh store as "about 41 meals ... food lasts about 10 days".

Over the next weeks Bennet's plea shrank into an entry in a list of skills: "Bennet of the Hales could teach quarrying/masonry" (Isolda, memories 43-49). This was not a decision to let him die. The agents processed him as information and never as something that called for action. Each one assumed someone else had decided.

**A mechanical barrier may have helped this along.** Two agents believed they could not address strangers:
- "Names in the tell action are limited to my own people" (Felicia, memory 38)
- "Everard cannot be named in offers or hosting" (Hamon, memory 54)

Speaking aloud (SAY) and giving goods to strangers both worked, though: Felicia's SAY to Everard and Gunnora's gift of tallow to Bennet show it. Feeding Bennet was always possible.

## 3. Food strategy

**The green barley was lost to agents copying each other.** Felicia started it on day 1: "reap the ripe barley that still stands". By day 2 she had noticed: "I wrongly cut the barley green; stop reaping". Gunnora told her to stop (2) and Fulk told Gervase the same (3). Then the field was cut anyway:
- Hawise planned six reaps (3): "help bring in the barley"
- Emma (4): "The barley is being cut, so I help reap it"
- Gunnora, the same Gunnora who had said stop, planned six reaps (4): "Reaping is my skilled work"

On day 5 every attempt got "there is no crop in Middle Field". Each agent had read the others' reaping as proof the crop was ripe: "others have been reaping it" (Felicia, 5); "since Gervase was already reaping it" (Gunnora, 6).

The green grain then sat in a heap on the Street that the take verb could not reach ("there is no building called ''"). Weeks later people still believed the barley was standing:
- "I will reap the barley in Middle Field, which should be ripe by autumn" (Fulk, 16)
- "Where is the barley standing?" (Isolda, 17)

Brockley sowed no grain for the rest of the 100 days. Five seed trips (Gervase, Gunnora, Felicia, Fulk twice) found that Ashford had none either. Two of those travellers died on the road: Gervase on the way out, Fulk coming home from his second try, which never reached Ashford. Every herd beast was eaten, including a Marsh bull Hamon killed "by mistake (the engine took it for a Marsh beast)" (memory 36). Food work dominated: about 68% of 2,540 planned actions went to getting food, half of all actions to "gather wild food" alone.

**The fatal trips, and the logic behind them.** The trips followed one simple rule: if the store is falling, go somewhere fresh. Nobody applied the obvious corollary, that the store is what keeps you alive on the night out.

- **Hawise (day 29).** She left three days after giving birth with "about 4 days" of food: "Others can mind Ellen meanwhile". The baby went with her anyway. On day 30 at Brock Wood: "I'm starving ... I'll fish". On day 31: "set off on the road to Brockley ..., where there is a store and a fire". She did not arrive.
- **Ivo and Isolda (day 33).** They went out to find Hawise and Ellen carrying nothing. Ivo at Brock Wood (34): "Ellen is starving and I carry no food." He died on the road home.
- **Gunnora (day 39).** "the store will last about a week. I'll leave the hide and tallow in the store ... then go to Brock Wood". At Brock Wood she ate nothing two nights running, then died at the Isel.

Each death came while the household store held food (37-41 meals in the Marsh house).

Agents did learn part of the lesson: "do not wander away from the household store without carrying food" (Ellen, memory 38); "Carry food for the road, because a night out in the cold leaves you starving" (Fulk, 61). Nobody put the contradiction itself into words, that the trips killed more people than the shortage did. Fulk kept making the same trip until it killed him on day 87.

## 4. Social organisation, the baby, grief

**Leadership.** Fulk acted as leader and was generous across the two households. He gave the Wynns meat three times without asking for payment. His justification is the only place in the run where the aim was used to make a decision: "Wynn are kin and count toward our people" (13); "We share one people" (11). He also refused to teach Orabel netting: "Not today, child; ... we need food now" (42). No skill was taught to anyone in 100 days.

**Baby Ellen.** The adults planned her care as if a baby stayed where it was told:
- "TELL Hawise: ... Take Ellen and come home" (Hamon, 31)
- "I'll bring Ellen home" (Isolda, 35)

No verb carries a person, and Ellen was left at Brock Wood twice. By day 38 she was a "youth" walking home alone and starving. She told her father: "I saw a fresh grave and a body dead some days by the road" (39). Five days later Hamon took her back to the Isel to fish.

**Grief was a matter of stock-taking.**
- "Hawise is past help. Her crude tool and fishing rod are useful to the Brockley folk, so I'll take them ... rather than spend the time on burial" (Felicia, 32)
- "Gervase lies dead by the road. I'll take his acorn meal" (Gunnora, 35)
- "Gunnora is dead at the Isel. I'll take her hide and tallow home" (Emma, 50)
- "Fulk is dead, so I name a new head" (Emma, 88)

The worst failure was over the deaths. Felicia's memory says "I buried her by the road to Brock Wood", and Fulk buried Ivo (39), but neither ever told Hamon. For 60 days Hamon carried "wife Hawise (missing ...; I fear she is dead)". On day 72 Emma told him "No news of Hawise or Ivo". On day 99 he set out "to examine the graves ... for news of Hawise and Ivo."

## 5. Against what the aim should produce

A people told that its head count matters above all should keep trying for children, take in strangers (a starving Bennet asking for a house was an offer to add to Brockley's count) and treat Ashford as the other side of a contest. Brockley did none of these:
- Three fertile couples tried on four days in total, each try gated on food.
- No stranger was taken in.
- Ashford was never a rival, only a place that might sell seed: "Ashford folk ... are starving too", written with no thought of what that meant for the comparison.

The aim sat in the background as a slogan ("then grow our people"), and the working goal was household survival and fair shares between the two Brockley houses.

## 6. Quirks and how it broke down

- **Refusals repeated without learning:**
  - 87 planned repairs, with 48 refused as "is sound";
  - 43 give commands refused, the "give ... for" form drawing from the store rather than the hands ("only 0 X in the store");
  - 12 snares set in woods, where snares cannot be set;
  - 5 attempts to travel straight to Ashford ("no way from here").
- **Rules invented after a refusal:** "I can't take things from the open Street, only from buildings" (Emma, 21).
- **Talking about the engine:** "the engine took it for a Marsh beast" (Hamon); "can't be taken with my schema" (Gunnora, 21).
- **Memories frozen in place:**
  - Emma kept writing "Hawise, Isolda, Ivo, baby Ellen" from day 61 to day 82, after Hawise and Ivo were dead and Ellen was a youth.
  - Orabel at day 97 listed "Hawise, Isolda, Ivo, baby Ellen" as people she could learn crafts from, and thought Felicia and Gervase were "not seen for about 50 days".
  - Hamon carried Jocelin as "dries meat, forages" after Jocelin died.
- **Late-game routine:** from about day 60 onward, nearly every entry is the same template, rotating one unit of foraging per place ("one unit per place ... no overwork"), with no new plan, no talk, and no mention of children or Ashford.
