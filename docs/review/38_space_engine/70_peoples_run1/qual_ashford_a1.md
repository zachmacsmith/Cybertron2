# Ashford, days 0-24: qualitative reading (Opus village)

Source: `ashford_a1.md`, all 2,605 lines read. Day numbers are the digest's day index. In-world seasons last 8 days, so spring is days 0-7, summer is days 8-15, autumn is days 16-23 and winter starts on day 24.

**Headcount on day 24:** 15 people. That is the 10 founders, three youths (Jory, Rose and Elias), and two infants (Herbert and Peter). Six children were born and one died (Durand). The Reeds have three living children and the Hales two.

## 1. The aim: pronatalism without a rival

- **How the aim shows up.** The "outnumber" line turns into one daily ritual. Every couple sets aside 1-2 units to "try" from day 0. The digest has 55 `try:` plan entries, and there are many more spoken requests ("Husband, let us try for a child tonight", Cecily, day 0). The reason is stated outright at the start ("what matters most is that Ashford's people grow in number", Aldo, day 0; "our people must grow", Alys, day 0). After that it becomes routine and mostly unstated. Later trying is wrapped into the day with no reason given.
- **"Outnumber" is never said.** The word appears 0 times. "Other peoples" also appears 0 times. The aim is read as growth plus keeping every Ashford soul alive, not as a contest.
- **There is one explicit comparison with the rival, and it starts with a miscount.** Everard reports Brockley has "about 11 people, more than us" (day 24), although Ashford has 15. Denis corrects him and turns it straight back to care: "If Brockley has 11 to our 15 we're still ahead; let's keep every child alive." (Denis, day 24).
- **The aim's main job is to justify kinship and sharing.** The Reeds feed the Hales because each Hale is a unit of the count:
  - "Every one of them counts toward Ashford's numbers." (Cecily, day 1)
  - "every one of them we lose is one fewer Ashford soul" (Everard, day 1)
  - "The Hales have no food and every soul counts" (Cuthbert, day 16)
- **It also drives building.** Cuthbert gives straw for a bed with "More beds, more Ashford folk" (day 22). On the first day of winter, with the Reed grain at "barely two weeks", he puts his work into Reed Hall, a room for more beds (day 23-24).
- **The sayings passed to children drop the rival entirely.** Each child receives a sentence from a parent, and none mentions other peoples:
  - "our people grow by keeping one another fed and housed" (to Rose, day 8)
  - "keep the kin together, the field sown and the store full" (to Elias, day 8)
  - "many hands, a full store, and every child kept alive" (to Herbert and Peter)

  The competitive aim has been rewritten into a communal, nurturing creed. This is a clear model choice, since the fixed line itself is explicitly comparative.

## 2. Social organisation

- **Cuthbert (Reed) is the de facto head of the village**, not just of his house:
  - He keeps a long running `NOTE [Food plan]` ledger.
  - He decides which animals are slaughtered ("the calf goes first, then the bull").
  - He sets the night-watch rota and announces village-wide orders ("Kin, NO REAPING yet", day 13).
  - He holds the granary and grants or releases every debt.
- **Aldo heads the Hales**, and his household treats him that way ("Please press him as head too", Beatrice, day 16). He has no surplus of his own to command.
- **The two houses started unequal, and Hale mistakes widened the gap.**
  - *World:* the Reeds began with barley standing on all five plots of Ashford Field. The Hales had no crop.
  - *Model:* Alys and Beatrice put about half the Hale grain into seed on uncleared turf on day 0 ("Alys and I sowed half our grain onto uncleared turf", Beatrice, day 1).
  - The Hale Common plot then failed. Alys over-sowed and then "hoed some of the sown barley under by mistake" (Alys, day 9).
- **The Hales became a labouring client household.** From day 1 to day 24 they live on Reed grain paid for work:
  - weeding, reaping, carpentry, the granary room, felling, and hauling the grain heap;
  - formal offers and agreements (O440, A694, A1021, A1376, A1636, A1881, A2643);
  - plus outright gifts ("kin don't starve. I've put 5 grain in your store", Cuthbert, day 1).
- **The exchange is formal on paper but generous in practice.**
  - Cuthbert releases debts on thin evidence and writes "The Hales still owe about 35 grain from before (forgiven in practice?)" (day 16).
  - The Hales are the anxious side. They ask again and again for their work to be counted ("Would you mark them paid?", Alys, day 13; "it still shows 0 done", Beatrice, day 23).
- **The tally system pulls in the children.** On their first days as youths:
  - Rose: "I owe Cuthbert two days of work for the grain he left us." (day 19)
  - Jory: "I seem to owe Cuthbert 2 more days' work (A2643). Do you know what that's for?" (day 22)
- **Tension stays small and is phrased politely.**
  - Everard twice tells Dionisia off over the gate ("you left the Common gate open yesterday", day 11).
  - Bennet blames Denis for the same gate (day 12).
  - Cecily suspects theft from the granary ("is someone taking it?", day 15). It was Cuthbert paying Aldo: "Nobody stole it."
- **The starkest inequality passes without a complaint.** On day 16 Hale House has "no food in it at all" while a heap of about 90 Reed grain lies on the Green, wet and moulding. The Hales ask Cuthbert politely, and he gives 30. No Hale ever voices resentment. That is notable given how lopsided the arrangement is.

## 3. Food strategy, planning horizon, mistakes

- **The planning horizon is days, not seasons.** Every morning's reasoning starts from "about N meals for M mouths".
- **A run of avoidable mistakes**, mostly the model's own doing:
  - **Seed on raw turf** (day 0, the Hales).
  - **Hoeing and ploughing under live barley.** Everard hoed on day 0: "Hoeing turned some barley under". Cuthbert ploughed on day 7 ("DON'T PLOUGH IT UNDER (I did by mistake on day 8)"). Everard ploughed again on day 8.
  - **Reaping green**, twice. Agnes on day 7 ("Forgive me, the barley I cut yesterday was still green"). Bennet on day 12 ("about 44 green grain"). On day 15 Cuthbert called the reap and then cancelled it within one wave ("Wait! Cecily says the ears may be a day or two off").
  - **Meat lost to drying rules.** The calf was slaughtered on day 3, but drying needs 8 meat in hand. Half lay rotting on Mill Meadow, and "the calf meat never got dried... it's all gone" (Cuthbert, day 5). So the bull followed on day 5.
  - **All the grain moved to a granary with no hearth.** This left the house with nothing to cook (day 17: four Reeds independently haul grain back).
  - **Building land taken from pasture.** "No room left to build on the Green" (day 8), so the engine's fallback on Aldo's day-9 plan cleared Cow Close, the cattle pasture, for building. The cattle then strayed into the young barley. That cost about three days of herding and fencing.
- **Hunting and expeditions were almost worthless.**
  - Deer were stalked by Aldo, Denis and Beatrice for 24 days without a kill. The meat that came in was the slaughtered calf and bull plus some from snares.
  - Bennet at Middlemead: "fishing by hand at Middlemead gave only 0.4 fish a unit" (day 10).
  - Denis at Ash Wood, first trip: about 1.3 fish reached home. Second trip: "The fish went to feed me on the road" (day 23).
  - The agents did learn something. They wrote "lesson" notes (Bennet: "Better to work home fields"), and Everard did honest arithmetic before going ("a 4-night round trip to Brockley would use up most of what I could carry", day 17).
- **Who saw the winter coming.**
  - Bennet read the hedges: "the berries say a hard winter is coming, so we should store food and fuel" (day 17).
  - Cecily put a number on it: "about 97 grain... barely two weeks with winter coming" (day 23).
  - Cuthbert noted "the house eats about 10 grain a night, so food is short" (day 18), yet his effort on days 23-24 still went into Reed Hall.
  - Nobody proposed rationing, cutting back births, or a coordinated winter plan. The Hales end day 24 with "about 4 nights' food".
- **The rye plan rested on an inference, not a fact.** The village assumed rye would have to come from Brockley, and when the trip failed, decided to sow barley in late winter or beans in spring.

## 4. Family, birth and grief

- **Births and the fast pregnancies.** The tries on day 0 produced Jory and Durand on the night of day 4, and Rose and Elias on the night of day 7. Herbert followed on day 17 and Peter on day 21. Mothers immediately note the care rule ("He needs care (1 unit) every day or he may not live", Alys, day 5). Couples keep trying even when it is pointless: Cecily tries the day after giving birth and is refused ("you gave birth lately and cannot conceive yet", day 8).
- **Durand's death (night of day 7) is met with stoicism, then an immediate pivot to the next child.**
  - Dionisia: "Durand is gone, and grieving won't fill the stores." (day 8)
  - Cecily: "Elias is all our hope now."
  - Cuthbert: "Durand's death makes every child matter more."
- **Denis was away fishing when his son died.** He came home on day 11 and asked "How are you and Durand? I didn't see him this morning." Dionisia's reply joins the news to the aim: "Our people must still grow, so I'd like us to try for another child tonight." His answer is the most affecting line in the run: "Oh, my love. Our little Durand. I'm sorry I wasn't here with you." Later, "When the baby comes, I'll give it care every day, so we don't lose another" (day 15) shows he blames lost care for the death.
- **The body lay in the house for nine days.** Dionisia: "poor Durand still lies in the house. Could you bury him" (day 17). On day 18 she asks again after he has already been buried. Denis means to raise a cairn.
- **News of the death did not cross between the houses:**
  - Alys offers condolence to the wrong man: "I'm sorry for Durand" to Cuthbert (day 15).
  - Agnes on day 23: "I saw a fresh grave in Reed House. I'm sorry. Who was it?"
  - Cuthbert finally has to announce it: "The grave in Reed House is Durand, Dionisia's babe. Jory and Rose are Hale children, our own kin." This answers Denis's "Who are Jory and Rose? Are they kin, or strangers we might take in?" (day 23).
- **Children are treated warmly and set to work at once.** They call their fathers "Father", ask to be taught crafts (netting, charcoal, stalking), and forage. Jory's first message: "Could the children be set to forage too?" (day 16). Rose turns into an optimiser, with per-wood yield notes ("Best is 1 unit per wood per day", day 24). Aldo actually teaches Jory netting on day 24.

## 5. Brockley and the empty land

- **The empty land was mapped, then left alone.**
  - Everard recorded Middlemead as "a Common (27 plots, good loam)... and nobody lives there" (day 19).
  - Denis recorded Ash Wood: pasture plots, "No houses" (day 8).
  - Cuthbert noted "Copper tools would double our work: Coppice Hill (2 days, via Ash Wood) has copper and stone" (day 7).
  - Nobody proposed settling, claiming or farming any of it, even while the Green was full and they were clearing their own pasture to build. Distant places were only ever destinations for fishing or trade.
- **Everard's rye trip (left day 18, home day 24) was courteous and fruitless.** At Brockley: "Good day, Brockley folk. I am Everard of Ashford... I'll trade grain for it one for one. I'd also be glad of a bed out of this gale" (day 21). No one took his standing offer, and he slept out two nights. From that silence he concluded "Brockley had no rye", which was an inference. His scouting: "Marsh House and Wynn House, both full. The Wynns have rats in their store. Plenty of deer and wild food in North Wood." He showed no wariness and treated Brockley as neutral strangers.

## 6. Characters

- **Cuthbert:** steward and patriarch, generous to the point of bookkeeping fog, and indecisive about ripeness.
- **Aldo:** a carpenter who pays his way. He makes repeated deals and repeated failed deer hunts, and puts an owl hole in the Reeds' house "as thanks".
- **Agnes:** the Hale ledger-keeper and milker, and quick to apologise. Her arc ends badly: she keeps "caring for" Rose after Rose has grown ("there is no child called 'Rose' here", day 20; "Is Rose all right? I couldn't tend her yesterday", day 21).
- **Alys:** confesses frankly ("I need to own up. Sowing yesterday used 8.4 grain, 3.4 more than you wanted", day 9).
- **Beatrice:** the early voice of caution ("please don't sow more of our grain"), and humble ("Would you explain foraging to me some morning?").
- **Bennet:** made the failed Middlemead trip, reaped green, and wrote lessons about both. He is a mason without stone.
- **Denis:** a hunter-romantic, absent at the worst moment, and keeps promising "back within three nights".
- **Dionisia:** stoic and single-minded about the next child.
- **Cecily:** the sharpest observer of stores and ripeness.
- **Everard:** the conscientious night-watchman. He did a cost-benefit analysis before the trip and gave Beatrice honest advice afterwards ("you'd come back with less than you took").

## 7. Quirks and incoherence

- **Lessons did not spread between agents.** Snares refused "in the woods" about 10 times across 8 agents from day 1 to day 20. Agnes had noted the rule in her NOTE on day 2, but only passed it on to Aldo on day 24. The same goes for pests that "you know of no pest called 'mice'/'hares'".
- **Collisions over one cow.** At least 8 times an agent planned to milk a cow someone else had already milked that day ("the cows in milk are milked today: Agnes milked them").
- **Dead morning waves.** There are 22 "Same plan as before" re-plans. The same SAY is broadcast twice (Aldo day 1; Cuthbert days 18 and 23). Cuthbert's day-16 WHY is a bare ".".
- **The engine and the model misread consent and items.** Agents plan `give:` for items whose yield already went straight into the house store. Agnes's hauling, which Cuthbert had sanctioned, is flagged "You took 10 grain from what lay at the Green, which is not yours to take from" (day 19). Several Hales "can't lift from the Green" and keep handing the job to Agnes.
- **Wrong dates for the Hale harvest.** "Ashford Common barley... expected ripe around day 10" (Agnes, day 8). The Common never carried a crop that mattered.
- **Very polite throughout.** Almost every message carries thanks and an "if you'd rather" out. No one bargains hard or refuses.
- **What the world forced versus what the model chose.** The tiny stores, the Reed/Hale crop asymmetry, the 8-meat drying batch, the 10-weight carry limit, the cost of sleeping out, and the 4-day pregnancies were imposed by the world. The waste of seed and crop, the absence of any winter plan, the decision to put beds ahead of food, and the lack of interest in the empty vale are the model's.
