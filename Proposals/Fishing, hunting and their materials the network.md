## Summary

One network of land, water, seasons, materials, techniques, gear and works for taking fish, game and fowl, built so that each
piece is worth having only because of the others, and so that villages come to need each other. It adds **four goods** (withies,
cord, flax, bone), **one crop** (flax), **about a dozen pieces of gear and four works** made from them, **one act** (dressing a
kill), and reshapes the methods into **five for the water and seven for land and air**, with the water split into three stocks.
It rests on one rule decided first: **the wild produces per day what it produces per real day** (§1). Everything is on the shared
basis (1 food = 2,500 kcal, a unit of work = 2 hours; [[D2-258 The food values are put on one physical basis|D2-258]], [[D2-259 The food values are anchored on real values|D2-259]]) and the compressed year (32 days, seasons of 8; [[D2-209 Brief 5 (river and water) is built|D2-209]]).

The research is in `docs/review/37_growth/02zzzh_hunt_fish/` (fishing_ladder, hunting_ladder, fibre_and_cord,
withies_wood_materials, engine_audit, byproducts_and_substitutes). It gathers the signs of the river ([[W-72 Signs of the river|W-72]],
proposals/river_signs.md), the fishing bugs ([[Q-79 Whether to fix the fishing bugs the live runs exposed|Q-79]]), the hunting and resources build that was benched ([[L-111 The hunting and resources build, a soon follow-up|L-111]]), the shallows stock
(Zach, 16 Nov), fowling split from fish nets, and carrying ([[W-71 How carrying works in the live runs|W-71]]).

## What the design is for (the tests every piece must pass)

A good, a technique or a work enters only if it does at least two of these:
1. **Two or more ways to get it, with different needs** (land, labour, skill, season, another group), so having it is a choice.
2. **It feeds two or more livelihoods,** so one investment lowers another's cost and someone becomes a supplier.
3. **It ties people to a place, a season or another group** (a chokepoint, a calendar, a dependence; `docs/scenario_design.md`).
4. **It makes a conflict:** a shared flow taken first by the one with capital, land wanted for two uses, or harm done downstream.

And the whole must keep four rules: methods are general, gear decides what they reach ([[D2-204 People answer the wild by general methods, not a technique per kind|D2-204]]); ladders are shallow, each method
works with nothing at a low take and the full chain is worth much more, with a few switches of paradigm (Zach, 15 Nov and 22 Oct);
real values within about 20%, every departure named ([[D2-259 The food values are anchored on real values|D2-259]]); food binds without taking more than about 30% of the work
(scenario_design).

By that test about 50 resources discussed since October come down to the ones below. Left out, each with its reason, in §10.

## 1. The rule underneath: the wild's clock

Breeding and regrowth now run on the 32-day year while people eat every day, so a wood feeds about 365/32 ≈ 11 times as many
mouths as a real one (hunting_ladder; it explains brief 4c's master hunter holding 2 to 3 food a unit for 16 days on one wood). The
same holds for resident fish (fishing_ladder) and for wild plants. The runs were already put on the real year by 32/365 ([[D2-258 The food values are put on one physical basis|D2-258]]).

**Proposed rule:** every wild stock produces, each day of the compressed year, what it produces on a real day (its real yearly
production ÷ 365). Calendars stay compressed (the seasons, the runs' windows, a withy bed's years); daily rates stay real (what an
hour of work takes, what a night's trap holds). A departure is a named lever ([[D2-259 The food values are anchored on real values|D2-259]]).

What follows from it, and is wanted: a village's ring of woods feeds a handful of people on game, not dozens; hunting pays as a
trade and on trips to fresh ground ([[D2-255 A very skilled hunter brings in a disproportionate amount of food…|D2-255]]), not as everyone's living; the shallows strip in days and come back slowly; the runs
and the keeping of their glut are where water food is rich. 37.4 to confirm the reading of the renewal code.

Gear and works carry the same split: **their life is counted in uses**, sized so that ordinary use wears them out in two or three
world years, and mending restores part of it. A withy bed's three real years to full yield become three world years (as the
salmon's return, [[D2-209 Brief 5 (river and water) is built|D2-209]]). Processes measured in real weeks (retting, drying, the salt's cure) become nights at about 1/11, rounded
to whole nights.

## 4. The materials' techniques: how each is got, and the switch in each

Each material has a way open to anyone with nothing, slower and seasonal, and a way that needs land, a work or another group,
cheaper per kilogram once it is in place.

### 4.1 Withies

| Technique | Where | Gives | Work | Season | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **cut withies** (gather; basic) | wild willow on wet ground | about 1.5 bundles a unit | 1 | winter full; summer a third | draws the willow stock; overcutting and grazing run it down |
| **cut withies** | a managed wood's young growth, or a withy bed | about 4 bundles a unit | 1 | as above | a managed wood's young growth is shared with firewood: the same `cut` |
| **plant a withy bed** (work; basic) | a wet parcel the household holds | a bed of about 200 m² | 1 bundle (the cuttings) and about 10 units | winter | gives nothing its first world year, half its second, about 30 bundles a year from its third, for about 30 years; beasts that reach it eat it (a fence or hurdles keep them off) |

**The switch:** the withy bed. Wild willow is free and close to any fen, but slow and thin; a bed is three years of waiting, a
parcel of wet ground held, and a fence. One bed keeps a weir section and a household's traps in repair (withies_wood_materials).

### 4.2 Cord

| Technique | Where | Gives | Work | Season | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **twist cord** (make; basic) | at a place with nettle or bast in season | about 0.04 cord a unit (25 units a kg) | 1 | late summer and autumn (nettle), late spring (bast); out of season a quarter | wild cord: labour only, coarse; enough for snares, lines and a coarse net |
| **sow flax**, **pull flax** (the farming verbs; the crop row `flax`) | a garden close or a plot | flax straw | as grain | spring, summer | land and manure taken from grain |
| **ret flax** (a set, like a trap) | in still or running water: 1 night; or spread on meadow grass: 3 to 4 nights | retted flax (about 0.7 of the straw by weight, calibrate) | 1 to lay, 1 to lift | late summer | **in water it fouls the parcel's water for about 3 nights**: its fish leave or die (the resident and shallows stocks fall there), beasts will not drink, and a parcel downstream on the same reach smells it; on grass it fouls nothing but lies on the meadow |
| **dress and spin** (make; a craft) | at a house | 1 cord from about 8 retted flax | about 7 to 8 units | best in winter, when other work is thin | about 3 times cheaper than wild cord per kilogram |
| **dig a retting pit** (work) | by water on a household's parcel | a pit that rets without fouling the river | about 10 units | | capital that removes the harm |

**How much cord a household needs:** a line of snares 0.02, a line 0.01, a fish net or a fowling net about 1 (every two or three
world years), a seine about 10 (a crew's, later). A fishing household needs about 1 to 3 kg a year: about 25 to 75 units of wild
twisting (an eighth to a third of one person's world year of about 190 units), or 8 to 24 kg of retted flax, which a garden close
of flax gives. **So flax is a close, not a field,** and one flax household can supply a whole fishing village: the trade is
cord, and the conflict is the retting.

**Rawhide thong** (cut from a hide; basic; 1 unit for snare thong): it does for land snares only, and rots in water. Kept as a
stand-in a hunter has without flax or nettle; not a cord good (it is part of making snares from a hide, §5).

### 4.3 Bone, hide, tallow: dressing the kill

**dress a kill** (work; basic, with a craft to learn): one act on a carcass (game, fowl in a bag, a slaughtered beast).

| Who | Gets | Work |
|---|---|---|
| anyone | the meat less about 15%, the offal, one hide (from beasts that have one) | about 0.5 unit for a deer, less for small game |
| one who knows the craft | all the meat, the offal, a whole hide, the tallow of a fat beast (deer and boar in autumn, farm beasts), bone (about 5% of a deer's live weight, calibrate), antler from a stag in season | the same |

Today a kill arrives dressed at no work (engine_audit): this makes the kill's by-products a skill, gives the hunter something to
sell besides meat (hides for packs, bone for hooks), and makes a careless butcher waste. Fish stay gutted at no work until the
salting rate ([[Q-73 Whether salting fish and meat stays at 4 kg a unit of work|Q-73]]) decides whether gutting is its own work (02zzx: gutting, not salting, was the labour).

## 5. Gear: made, held, set, lifted, worn, mended

Every piece is an item one holds (in hand or store), with a **life in uses**. Novices take about twice the work to make it.

| Gear | Made from | Work | Life (uses) | Used by | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **wicker trap** | 0.4 withies | 2.5 | about 30 sets; a spate can wreck it | set gear | holds about 10 kg a night (built); takes the runs and the edge |
| **snares** (a line of 5 nooses) | 0.02 cord, or a little rawhide | 1 | about 10 sets | snare | as built; rawhide snares rot in a wet night |
| **line** (hand line with hooks) | 0.01 cord and hooks | 0.5 | about 30 uses; hooks lost now and then | line fishing; set lines | reaches the open water |
| **fishing rod** | 0.1 withies (and a line) | 0.5 | about 60 uses | line fishing | raises the line's take (about ×1.5); replaces today's rod of 0.5 timber and 0.1 hide |
| **bone hooks** (5) | 0.05 bone | 1 | break and are lost | lines | anyone with bone |
| **copper hooks** (10) | 0.05 copper | 1, a smith's craft | last longer | lines | take somewhat more (about ×1.3; calibrate): a dependence on the forge |
| **fish net** (a gill or dip net, 20 m by 1.5 m) | 1 cord | about 30 | about 60 to 80 uses | edge fishing in a run (dipped at a pinch); set gear (set overnight in open water) | a set net saturates near 6 kg a night |
| **fowling net** (clap net, 10 m by 2 m) | 0.8 cord | about 30 | about 60 to 80 uses | fowling | raises fowling's take about ×4 over hands |
| **creel** (a fishing basket) | 0.3 withies | 1 | long | carrying | carries the catch: about +5 to what one can carry, for fish and small goods |
| **pack basket** (frame and straps) | 1 withies, 0.1 hide | 2 | long | carrying | about +15 (a real 25 to 30 kg load with a basket: [[W-71 How carrying works in the live runs|W-71]]); replaces today's pack of 1 hide and 1 timber |
| **hurdle** | 1 withies | 1.5 | long | folds, fences, fish garths later | a fence's panels in withies instead of timber (herding's capital) |

**Set and lift are two acts.** *Set* places traps, lines or a net one holds at a place (a line of up to about 6 traps an act, as a
snare line lays 5; real work sets 6 to 10 a unit). *Lift* takes the catch from all of one's own sets at a place, and re-sets them
unless told not to; it costs a unit for the place, not one a trap. This ends the confusion in about 40% of trap acts ([[Q-79 Whether to fix the fishing bugs the live runs exposed|Q-79]]; 02zzy2).

**Wear reaches use.** Each set, cast, hunt or fight takes a use from the gear (weapons too: [[D2-46 Weapons wear|D2-46]]; today only ploughs and hoes wear,
engine_audit). **mend gear** (work) restores about half a piece's life for a tenth of its materials: a net is mended, not
remade, until it is rotten.

**Bait.** Offal on a set line or in a trap raises its take (about ×1.5, calibrate) and is used up: the kill's waste gets a use and
hunters and fishers a reason to trade.

## 7. The methods

### 7.1 The water: three stocks, the runs, five methods

The water holds **the shallows** (small fish, crayfish, eels at the edge; small, quick to strip, quick to come back), **the open
water** (the body's resident fish, [[D2-229 One fish stock per water body|D2-229]]) and **the runs**. Kept fish (a keep box, a pond) come later.

| Method | Reaches | Bare | Gear that raises it | Take (food a unit; real anchor) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **fish at the edge** (today's `fish by hand`; basic) | the shallows; a run where it passes a shallows or a pinch point | hands | spear (×1.5), fish net dipped (×3 in a run) | 0.1 to 0.3 at the edge; in a run at a pinch, a few times that |
| **line fishing** (today's `angle`) | the open water | a line | rod (×1.5), copper hooks (×1.3), bait (×1.5) | 0.1 to 0.5 (today's 1.5 a unit is 1.5 to 3 times real: lowered, or the factor named) |
| **set gear** (today's `trap in water`, widened) | traps: the runs and the edge; set lines and set nets: the open water and the runs | (needs gear) | the trap, the line, the net; bait | 0.4 to 2 off a run; a run night 5 or more, until the trap is full |
| **draw a net** (later, with the seine) | open water and runs, by crew | | the seine, a boat on a lake | a crew's |
| **the works** (built) | the runs | | weir sections, the gap | 5 to 18 on run nights |

What reaches what is decided by the gear and the stock, not by a technique per kind ([[D2-204 People answer the wild by general methods, not a technique per kind|D2-204]]): a wicker trap meets an eel, not a
salmon in open water; a net meets both; hands meet the shallows.

### 7.2 Land and air: seven methods

| Method | Reaches | Gear | Take (food a unit; real anchor) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hunt the wild** (built) | game in a wood or area | a weapon (crude weapon; the bow later) | novice 0.08 to 0.3; capable 0.4 to 1.2; master 1.2 to 3.2 near home, 3 to 6 on fresh ground, capped by the carry ([[D2-255 A very skilled hunter brings in a disproportionate amount of food…|D2-255]]) |
| **stalk**, **drive** (built) | as built | as built; the drive needs a crew | |
| **snare** (built; gear now) | small game on runs | snares | 0.4 to 1.2 |
| **den work** (built) | dens, lodges | | |
| **gather young and eggs** (built; widened to eggs) | nests, colonies | | spring |
| ★ **fowling** (new; split from `net`) | wildfowl on wet ground, at dusk and dawn; pigeons on fields | hands in a freeze; the fowling net (×4) | 0.24 to 2.2, rising in a freeze when fowl crowd the open water |

**Fowling apart from fish nets:** birds get a `flies` trait; the fowling net meets what flies, fish nets what swims. Wildfowl numbers
on wet ground go to real winter counts (refuges hold 400 to 1,500 waterbirds a km², a wet district 50 to 300; the engine's 4 to 52
an area is low; hunting_ladder).

### 7.3 Keeping (built; recalibrated)

Smoking (fuel and a smokehouse), salting (salt; [[Q-73 Whether salting fish and meat stays at 4 kg a unit of work|Q-73]]'s rate; barrels later), drying at the hearth or in the wind. The keeping, not
the catching, is what turns a run's glut into a winter's food, and its rates are the binding limit ([[D2-258 The food values are put on one physical basis|D2-258]]). [[Q-73 Whether salting fish and meat stays at 4 kg a unit of work|Q-73]] proposes salting
at about 20 kg an hour (02zzx).

## 8. The network: who needs whom, what fights what

### 8.1 The graph in words

- **Withies** ← wild willow (fen, banks) or a withy bed (wet ground held for years) or coppice (shared with firewood) → traps,
  weir wattle, racks, creels, pack baskets, hurdles, rods → **fishing, carrying, herding (folds), keeping.**
- **Cord** ← wild nettle and bast (labour, season) or flax (a close, manure, retting) → snares, lines, nets → **hunting, fowling,
  fishing**; later rope → building, boats.
- **Flax** ← good land (against grain) → retting (against the river's fish downstream, unless a pit is dug) → cord.
- **Kill** → dressing (skill) → meat, offal (→ bait), hide (→ packs, thongs), tallow (→ light), bone (→ hooks, points).
- **Copper** ← the smith → hooks and points that outlast bone: **a dependence on the forge.**
- **The runs** → the weir (upstream first for eels, downstream for salmon) → the keeping (salt, fuel, smokehouse) → winter.

### 8.2 The dependences between groups (the leverage)

- **The fishing village needs cord** (its nets) and **withies** (its traps and weir). It can make both slowly itself, or get cord
  cheaply from a flax household and withies from a bed it plants and waits for.
- **The flax household needs a river to ret in, or a pit,** and the fishers downstream need it not to ret in theirs: a conflict
  with a capital answer (the pit) and a social one (an agreement, a rule).
- **The hunter sells hides and bone**: packs, thongs and hooks are made from them.
- **The smith sells copper hooks**: better than bone, from ore and a forge only some villages have.
- **The salt spring's holder, the weir's holder, the withy bed's holder** each hold a chokepoint others work around or pay.

### 8.3 Big works that take a shared flow, and harms between ladders

Already able to emerge: a weir upstream takes an eel run before the village below; salmon return from the spawners that got
through, so a weir with its gap shut, or a mill holding half the run, shrinks next year's run for everyone; resident fish are one
stock every method shares; wary game carries one hunter's pressure to the next. Added by this design: retting fouls the water
downstream; flax takes grain's land; a withy bed and a coppice renew where wild cutting runs the willow down; wild cord costs a
third of a person's year where flax costs a garden close. Not added now (no phase needs them): a binding close time or mesh rule,
the decoy's quiet zone, kept stocks (park, warren, pond), rabbits and pigeons eating crops (§10). The history (byproducts §4):
Magna Carta c. 33 against fish weirs; the 1285 salmon close season and weir gap; London burning fine nets; the 1534 close season
on moulting ducks; England's shift from freshwater to sea fish around AD 1000.

### 8.5 The year's work (one compressed year of 32 days, seasons of 8)

| Season | Water | Land and air | Materials and making |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Spring** | the shallows waking; few runs | eggs and young; game thin | strip bast; sow flax; plant a withy bed (late winter) |
| **Summer** | low water: the shallows thick; the moult | hay (farming) | pull flax; ret it (the downstream fishers watch the water); twist nettle late |
| **Autumn** | **the eel runs and the salmon** (set traps, work the weir, smoke and salt the glut) | deer in their fat; the hunt's season; nuts | make and mend traps before the runs; twist nettle |
| **Winter** | ice; little fish | **fowling** on the flooded meadows; trips for game | **cut withies; dress and spin; make and mend nets** |

The year binds: the runs fall when the deer are fat and the nuts drop; the materials are made in the slack of winter and spring,
before they are needed. A household that has not cut withies and spun cord by late summer meets the run with no traps.

## 11. How it is built (feature units, seams, ownership)

Written as its own units ([[D2-244 The engine's reworking into feature packages|D2-244]]; feature_packages.md §9-10), each with its data, verbs, signs and processes listed together:
**materials** (withies, cord, flax, retted flax, bone; cut withies, twist cord, ret flax, dress and spin, the withy bed, the
retting pit; the fouling process), **gear** (items with life in uses, set and lift, mend, bait, wear reaching use), **the water's
methods** (the three stocks, edge, line, set gear; the weir and racks recosted), **fowling** (the method, the `flies` trait, the
fowl's numbers), **dressing the kill**, and **the wild's clock** (§1, 37.4's renewal code).

Gaps it closes or meets: traps, snares and nets cost nothing today (`wild.set_work`); wear never reaches the take; the kind's traits
decide what a method takes (the net meeting salmon); no butchering act; the materials' weights not on a kilogram basis (timber 2,
firewood 1, hide 1: a unit of each should be a stated number of kilograms, and the carry limit read against it); stale records
([[D4-122 Fish on the hearth rack|D4-122]]'s dried fish, marked to 37.4).

Existing worlds keep running: a world without this unit's goods seeds its households with the gear their arm or archetype holds,
and the old free sets go on working there until the world is remade.

Ownership: 37.2 the materials, the gear's data, the water's and wild's methods, fowling, the worlds and the lore; 37.4 the core of
senses, the act results, wear, memory and the clocks.

## 13. The order the yields should keep (calibration targets)

Against plough farming at 2.4 to 2.8 food a unit:

| Method | Food a unit of work |
|---|---|
| hands at the edge, line fishing | 0.1 to 0.5 |
| set gear and snares off a run | 0.4 to 2 |
| set gear and the weir on run nights | 5 to 18, for a few nights a year |
| hunting: novice / capable / master near home / master far away | 0.08–0.3 / 0.4–1.2 / 1.2–3.2 / 3–6, capped by the carry |
| fowling | 0.24 to 2.2 |

And the materials' share of a household's work: a fishing household spends roughly 10 to 20% of its world year making and mending
its gear and materials (cord, withies, traps, a net), less with flax and a bed, more by wild cord and wild willow. The stocks and
the keeping, not the hourly rates, are what stop wild food feeding everyone.

## Why

Calibrating fishing or hunting alone does not hold: the take depends on the gear, the gear on its materials, the materials on land,
seasons and other people, and what people choose on what they can sense (Zach, 21 Nov: "I don't think calibrating really makes
sense without this"). The live runs showed all of these failing together (02zzz, 02zzy, 02zzy2).

## Alternatives considered

- **Calibrate take rates and keep gear free:** quick; leaves capital as racks and a weir only; rejected by Zach's ask.
- **A deep ladder of rungs per livelihood:** rejected (Zach, 15 Nov).
- **All 50 resources:** most add a good with one source and one use; they add goods without adding links (§10).
- **Keep the 32-day production as a generous named lever:** keeps wild living about 11 times too rich per mouth and hides every
  other calibration under it (§1).
- **Flax as a field crop like grain:** a household's cord needs are a few kilograms a year; a field would make cord far too cheap
  and flax a rival to grain everywhere. A close keeps it small and makes the flax household a supplier.
- **Cord and twine as two goods:** fine twine is three to four times the spinning a kilogram; folded into the net's work instead.
