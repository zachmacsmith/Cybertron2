## Purpose

Every act of taking is the same four parts (verb_grammar; wave3_taking §1.1): **a shape** (set, tend, gather), **a technique** (wicker
traps, snares, a gill net, a set line, the edge, a line, fowling, the hunt), **the gear** it uses (held, worn), and **a target**: a
stock for a worked take (U1's shallows, open water, a run, an area's game), and a thing for every act on placed gear. Placed gear is
one's own (or one's household's) tagged thing, standing until taken up. Set lays gear from one's hands; tend goes back to it. That one
change ends the set-for-lift confusion (about 40% of trap acts in the live runs, 02zzy2), because setting is impossible without gear in
hand and the refusal names the gear already standing. Each result tells the truth on two axes (what one took; what the water or the
wood held), and never says a water is empty when it is not.

### 2. Placed gear: the set thing, whose it is, whose the catch (gap G12, designed here)

A set is a container `kind: set` as now (things.py), with these fields; the new ones starred:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `by` | who set it |
| ★`household` | the setter's household at the setting (`w.household_of(by)`); fixed for the thing's life |
| `tag` | its T-number (tags.py, given at setting as now), **shown** wherever one's own things are listed (fault 8) |
| `set.method` | the technique |
| ★`set.gear` | `{item: n}` laid: 6 wicker traps, 25 snares, 1 gill net, 1 set line |
| ★`set.pieces` | one entry a piece of gear: `{"life": left (U3's rule), "torn": bool}`, so a line wears and is torn piece by piece |
| `set_day`, ★`set.tended` | the night set; the last day tended |
| `holds`, `caught`, `catch`, goods | as now: kg for traps and nets (hold 10 kg a trap, [[D2-258 The food values are put on one physical basis|D2-258]]), heads for snares |
| ★`set.sprung` | for snares: nooses sprung empty (they catch nothing until tended) |
| `robbed`, `robbed_by`, ★`set.scavenged` | another person; an otter (as now); a fox, crows or a stoat at a snare's catch |
| ★`set.state` | `fishing`, `idle` (iced, U1 §4), `spent` (every piece torn or worn out) |

**Whose it is.** `things.owns_set(c, name)` (things.py:289) becomes true when: `c.by == name`; or `name` is a living member of
`c.household`; or the thing is a weir's traps and `name` is of the weir's `crew` or a member of a household holding the weir by its
claims; or an agreement lends the thing's use to `name` (Gaps: the agreements' term for a thing's use).

**Whose the catch.** A tend puts the catch into the tender's hands, whoever of the owners tends. The household's book (U6, "where goods
go") then sends it where the household keeps fish; nothing here moves it. A weir worked by a crew from several households shares its
catch as their agreement says (today's rule; unchanged). Gear taken up (`up`) goes into the tender's hands: it is still the
household's, by the same book.

**A member who leaves** a household keeps no ownership of what that household set: the thing's `household` does not change. To tend
it after leaving is to name another's thing.

**Theft.** A tend or a take from a set one does not own, named by its tag or a known name, is done, worded as theft (§5), and recorded
in the day's thefts as a store theft is (`today["thefts"]`, verbs.py:1670-1676) with the thing in place of the building, so its
witnesses are settled by the same rule ([[D4-81 Who stole is known only to those who saw it|D4-81]]); the setter's next look shows it (`robbed`, things.py:363, as now). Unnamed, another's
thing is never touched.

### 3. Set and tend: the two acts on placed gear

**Set** lays gear from the hands (or a creel) at a place: up to **6 wicker traps** ([[D2-264 The fishing, hunting and materials network|D2-264]]), **25 snares** (hunt_fish §7.7), **1 gill
net**, **1 set line** an act, for **1 unit**. With fewer in hand, it lays what one holds. It is refused with none in hand, naming
one's gear already standing and how to tend it.

**Tend** goes to one's own placed gear at a place (or the one named by tag): **1 unit for all of one's own of that technique at the
place** (a snare line by its tag, wherever its parcel). For each thing tended: the catch into hand; each sound piece set again (snares
reset, traps rebaited if bait is in hand, [[L-145 Offal's uses bait for set lines and traps, dog food|L-145]]); torn pieces dropped; with `up: true` the sound pieces come back into hand instead;
with `to` (snares) the line is moved to another parcel at no extra cost. A tend of the weir's traps empties them (1 unit, as now; its
gap's work stays in `set {"thing": "gap"}`, WATER_WORKS weir `units` shut 2, open 1: [[D2-219 A run night's work counts shutting|D2-219]]'s three units a run night).

**A technique named alone** (today `gather {"what": "wicker traps"}`, the path agents use; or a bare `{"do": "wicker traps"}` under
U9's shapes): (1) one's own gear of it stands at the place: **tend** it; (2) none stands and one holds the gear: **set** it; (3)
neither: refused, saying where one's gear is or that one has none. The result always says which was done and how to ask for the other.

**The verb lines** (engine/verbs.py VERB, 37.4's; about 80 words for both):

```
- set {"what": gear you carry (wicker traps | snares | a gill net | a set line), "at": place, "qty"?: how many}: lay gear you hold
  where it catches by night; it stays, yours, until you take it up (1 unit; up to 6 traps or 25 snares). Also {"thing": door | gap,
  "to": ...} as before.
- tend {"thing"?: a tag (T7), "at"?: place, "up"?: true, "to"?: a place to move snares to}: go back to your set gear there: take what
  it caught and set again what was sprung; up: bring it home. With no tag, all of yours of that kind there (1 unit a place).
```

The word `set` now carries two argument shapes (gear by `what`; a door or a gap by `thing`), told apart by the argument, until U9's
`change` shape takes doors and gaps (wave3_taking conflict 4). The resolver reads lift, check, empty, walk and collect as tend
([[D2-294 Going back to one's set gear is the act 'tend'|D2-294]]), and "take from my traps" as a tend of that thing.

**What the person sees before** (the "Here" view, things.set_line, 37.4's; U9 places it): one line a thing of one's own, with its tag
and count: "your traps in the Isel (T7: 6 traps, set autumn day 2, last tended this morning)"; "your household's traps in the fen (T8:
6 traps, set by Nigel autumn day 3)"; "your snares on the Leaze (T4: 22 set, 3 sprung)"; "the weir's traps at the Isel, which you
work (T3)".

### 5. Result wording rules (every act above)

1. **Two axes.** What one got; and, separately, what the water or the wood held, read from the night's river record (U8 §1) or the
   wild state, graded for one who fishes or hunts. The second never depends on the first (Zach, 21 Nov: "on an independent axis from
   their actions").
2. **Never "empty"** unless the stock is at its refuge and no run passes.
3. **Tags** of every thing touched, and of one's other things still standing at the place.
4. **Weights and meals:** kg from the goods' weights (data.py ITEMS), meals from the food values as the store line gives them ("food
   worth about 3 meals"); the doer's own count of heads ("11 eels", "about 200 eels").
5. **Scale words** in looks and signs, not counts: `catch_words` (things.py:337) goes past "a dozen or more" to "scores of" (13 to 99)
   and "a hundred and more" (100 or more).
6. **Theft named for what it is:** "..., which are not yours to take from. Whether anyone saw, you will learn." (as the store's).
7. **Which shape was done**, when a technique was named alone: "You tended T7 ... To lay the 6 traps you carry beside them, set wicker
   traps."
