## What this wave already gives hunting

Snares as gear set and tended, with the snare's night clock (alive, dead, scavenged, spoilt); the hunt as an act on a stock with its
party; dressing a kill as its own act (meat, hide, bone); fowling split from fish nets; hides into tanning ([[D2-281 Tanning comes in this build, with bark as a new good|D2-281]]); the carry limit
binding how much of a deer comes home; the very skilled hunter ([[D2-255 A very skilled hunter brings in a disproportionate amount of food…|D2-255]]). Weapons, kept beasts, most of a kill's by-products, the
wild's other resources and the law of the woods are left out.

## What could be fleshed out

1. **Weapons and tools:** the spear and the bow (a yew or ash stave, sinew, fletching), arrows as a use-up good, game nets and pits;
   skill and tool as multipliers (hunting_ladder §3.3).
2. **Kept beasts as tools:** dogs (fed, bred, trained; the hunt's biggest multiplier), ferrets for rabbits, hawks as a lord's
   sport; their upkeep (hunting_ladder §4.3).
3. **Game and habitat:** wildlife by terrain ([[L-37 Wildlife by terrain|L-37]]), the seasons of game (rut, young, winter thinness), sustained yield on the year
   clock, pressure and wariness (exists), scavengers and predators.
4. **What a kill gives:** meat (fresh, dried, salted, smoked as separate goods, [[D2-295 Each depth of cure is its own good|D2-295]]), hide, bone, antler, sinew, fat and tallow
   (lights), feathers and down, gut.
5. **The wild's other resources:** honey and wax, hazel nuts and acorns (pannage for pigs), berries, mushrooms, herbs, reeds and
   rushes (thatch, lights), eggs.
6. **Keeping instead of catching:** warrens, dovecotes, deer parks, fish ponds, decoy ponds: land taken from the common.
7. **The law of the woods:** forest law, game reserved to lords, poaching, a forester and a woodward: chokepoints that need people to
   enforce them (scenario_design.md).
8. **The hunt as a crew:** drives, beaters, sharing the kill, who gets which part.

## Why beside timber

The woods are one place: the trees (timber, coppice, bark, fuel), the game's habitat, pannage, wood pasture, the bow's staves, and
the forest law that governs both. A thread on the woods as a place, timber and the wild's life together, avoids designing the same
land twice. It is too large for one wave: a first wave on the woods as a place (timber, coppice, wood pasture, game and its seasons,
forest rights), a second on the hunter's tools, kept beasts and the wild's other resources.

## Open questions

1. One thread for the woods (timber and the wild together), or timber first and the wild after?
2. Which first: weapons and dogs, or game by habitat and season?
3. Forest law and poaching in the first wave, or later?
