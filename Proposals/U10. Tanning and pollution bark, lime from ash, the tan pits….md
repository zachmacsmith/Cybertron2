## Purpose

Leather is the material that ties the hunter and the herder (hides), the woodsman (oak bark in a short spring season), the smith
(bellows), the carrier (the frame), the ploughman (harness) and everyone who walks (shoes, if they come) to one skilled household
with pits by the water: a chokepoint with a long wait (scenario_design §1). Tanning and retting are also the network's harm done
downstream: the water a tanner or a flax grower needs is the water the fishers below take their living from (hunt_fish_materials
§8.2). This spec makes both real in numbers: what goes in, how long, what comes out, who sees it, and how much of the river dies.

## 2. Tanning

### 2.1 Hides in

Hides come by weight (basis §2 "Hide"; U3 §1.4): a red deer, a boar, a calf or a young beast 1 hide unit (about 6 kg green, 3 kg
dried); a roe 0.3; a cow 3.5; an ox or a bull 4; a bear 3. The small furs give pelts, which are not tanned here (fur later). Hides
come mostly in autumn, from the deer in their fat and the slaughter before winter. A dried hide keeps a year: a quarter lost by 33.8
nights, 0.85% a night well kept, the open wet x3 (U3 §1.4; [[D2-289 A shelf life 'keeps d days' becomes a nightly loss read as about a…|D2-289]]).

**Light and heavy:** a hide of 1 hide unit or less is light (upper leather for shoes, straps, the frame); a hide of more is heavy
(harness, soles, the bellows). Light and heavy go in separate lots, since their nights differ.

**What a season gives:** one peeler working every unit of the six days (four spring days at 6 units, two summer days at 7; basis §1)
has 38 units: **57 bundles, enough for about 28 hide units**. A peeler who also sows peels four days (24 units, 36 bundles, 18 hide
units). The season's length, not the wood, sets a tanner's scale, and the peeling clashes with spring sowing (wave2 §5.2).

**Bark's other buyer:** barking a net (U3 §3.4) takes 0.5 bark a net a year, about 5 kg a fishing household. Bark is the fishers'
reason to buy from the peeler.

### 2.3 Lime from wood ash

Real unhairing used lime or wood-ash lye. **Liming needs ash from a hearth of the tanner's household within the village**: a hearth
or fire ring of a building the household uses that burned fuel on any of the last 8 nights. No ash good is counted (Departure 1).
Refusal: "There is no wood ash to hand for the lime pit: the pits want ash from a hearth kept burning." Burnt lime (limestone or
chalk in a kiln) joins with mortar and lime burning in the timber and building economy ([[W-76 The timber economy|W-76]]).

### 2.4 The tan pits and the process

**The work** (U7 prices it; the inputs handed to U7 are wave1 §3.7's: 2 to 4 units at 1/7, 4 timber of planks or wattle, clay
puddled on the spot; 20 world years' life): `build tan pits` on a parcel that borders a water parcel (or a fen), the household's or
granted. It holds **a lime pit and 2 to 4 tan pits** (a `pits` count at building, each pit added later as a fitting), **each pit 20
hide units** [e: a pit of about 3 m3 takes a layered batch of 20 to 30 deer-sized hides]. Its water goes to `below`: the bordering
water parcel furthest downstream (`wild._upstream` order), fixed at building and named in the build's result: "the pits' water runs
into the Isel at Long Reach".

**The process rows** (U4's `PROCESSES` table; the lot, the container and the night step are U4's):

| Stage | Act | In | Out | Nights | Labour (quantity work at real hours) | Inputs | Fouling (§3.2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lime | `make lime hides` (load the lime pit) | hide | limed hide | **2** (basis §1: 2 to 4) | 0.25 unit a hide unit | ash (the condition, §2.3) | 3 FU a hide unit when the pit is emptied at the next stage |
| scrape and lay | `make tan hides` (unload the lime pit, scrape, load a tan pit) | limed hide | tanning hide | **light 16, heavy 32** | 0.5 unit a hide unit | **1 bark a hide unit** | 0.1 FU a hide unit a night while in |
| strengthen (twice) | `work strengthen the pits` | | | at a third and two thirds of the nights (light: nights 5 and 11; heavy 11 and 21), within 2 nights | 0.125 unit a hide unit each time | **0.5 bark a hide unit each time** | 0.5 FU a hide unit (old liquor drawn off) |
| lift, dry and curry | `make curry leather` (unload, dry, curry) | tanned hide | **3 leather a hide unit** | | 0.5 unit a hide unit | **0.1 tallow a hide unit** | 3 FU a hide unit (the spent liquor let out) |

In all: **1.5 units, 2 bark (20 kg) and 0.1 tallow a hide unit**, and 18 or 34 nights (wave1 §3.1: 1.5 units a hide; about 10 kg of
bark a hide at full use, 20 in practice). Each act takes up to its batch of hide units for its units (U3 §2.1; [[D2-247 A process of bulk goods has a most it takes at once, not a least|D2-247]]), so a lot of
6 deer takes 1.5 units to lime, 3 to scrape and lay, 0.75 twice to strengthen and 3 to lift and curry.

**Conditions a night must meet** (U4's night step reads them): the lot counts a night if its strengthening is not overdue; **a lot
overdue past its 2 nights' grace pauses and its hides rot at 2% a night** [e] until it is strengthened (U4's failure); **a spate or a
flood** at `below` floods the pits and takes a tenth of a lot in a spate, a third in a flood [e, beside a spate's 0.5 on set traps]. A
limed hide left in the lime pit past its 2 nights loses 5% a night [e]. Tanned hides left in the pit past their nights are kept, no
worse (longer is stronger leather, wave3_keeping §2.2).

**Failure** (a skilled craft, trying_and_learning §1, wave4 §4.2): the try is drawn **when the hides are laid** in the tan pit, by
level (works 0.15 aware, 0.35 rough, 0.65 capable, always at known), and shown **when they are lifted**: a failed lot loses **a half
of its hides at aware, a third at rough, a fifth at capable** (W2-8, default taken). A pit of twenty hides does not all rot at once;
a skilled craft fails late and costly but not all or nothing. The lost hides are gone (rotted at the core); the bark is spent.

**Heavy leather** is a capability point (§2.7): before it, laying a heavy hide is refused: "You have not the hand yet for a cow's
hide: deer and calves only."

### 2.5 Acts and results, in their words

- **Lime:** "You laid 6 hides in the lime pit at the Tanners' pits (1.5 units), with ash from the house's hearth: they are ready to
  scrape in 2 nights."
- **Scrape and lay:** "You scraped the 6 limed hides and laid them in the second pit with 6 bark (3 units): light hides, 16 nights in
  the pits; the liquor wants strengthening about the 5th night and the 11th."
- **Strengthen:** "You strengthened the liquor in the second pit with 3 bark (0.75 units): next about the 11th night."
- **Overdue** (the household's own containers' lines, U4 and U6): "The second pit's liquor is spent: the hides in it are rotting a
  little each night until it is strengthened."
- **Lift and curry:** "You lifted 6 tanned hides from the second pit and curried them with 0.6 tallow: 18 leather (3 units)."
- **Lift after a failure:** "You lifted the hides from the second pit: 2 of the 6 have rotted at the core and the hair slips, and are
  lost; the liquor was too strong at the first and sealed them raw. 4 tanned hides are left, 12 leather once curried." The cause is the
  next lesson not had, or the true one the state holds (an overdue stage, wet bark) (trying_and_learning §3).
- **Refusals:** no pits: "There are no tan pits here you can use: hides are tanned in pits dug by water (build tan pits)." No room:
  "The pits at the Tanners' are full: 40 hide units lie in them, the first out in 6 nights." No bark: "It needs 6 bark for 6 hides;
  you have 2 to hand (oak bark is peeled in late spring)."

### 2.7 Shoes as an option (W2-7)

[[D2-281 Tanning comes in this build, with bark as a new good|D2-281]] lists shoes among leather's uses; [[D2-10 Clothing is parked|D2-10]] parks clothing and lets shelter carry the cold, and the engine has no cost to walking
or the cold by what one wears (G2). Shoes therefore need an effect, or they are a demand that does nothing (a dial, scenario_design
§4). Designed, behind a world setting `shoes` (default off until Zach answers):

- **The cold out of doors:** a person who spends the day working or walking out of doors on a frost or snow day **without shoes** eats
  **0.25 food more that night** ([[D2-292 The food unit|D2-292]]: more for cold) [e]; with shoes, nothing more. A winter of about four frost days costs the
  unshod about 1 food, about the worth of a pair's making.
- **Wear:** shoes wear by days worn (U3 §3.3: 12 days, real 120 to 240 days of wear at 1/11.4; rawhide shoes 3); resoled with 0.05
  leather for half their days back (U3 §3.4).
- **A bodily sign to the doer** (guides/senses.md, the four kinds: to the doer): on a frost day out unshod, "your feet are numb and
  cut with the cold" (touch, doer, own 2.0).
- **In the person's own things:** "shoes (worn: about 4 days' wear left)".
- **Code:** `needs.py`'s night need adds the term when `w.setting("shoes")` is on (37.4).

With shoes off, leather's demand is 2 to 4 kg a village a year and the tanner a small by-trade; with shoes on, 12 to 14 kg and the
tanner a chokepoint for a region.

## 3. Pollution: retting and tanning in numbers (G3)

### 3.1 The state

- **`w.s["fouling"]`**: `{water parcel id: F}`, the fouling in that parcel's water tonight, in **fouling units (FU)**. **1 FU is the
  oxygen demand of 1 kg of flax straw retting in water** (about 80 g [e]: retting releases 5 to 10% of the straw's dry matter as
  rotting gums and pectins). A fen parcel holds its own (its pools and dykes).
- **`w.s["fouling_from"]`**: `{parcel: {"flax": FU, "pits": FU, "day": d}}`, what put it there tonight, for the signs.
- Deterministic and replay-safe: no draws; a world without the materials pack holds no fouling and is unchanged.

### 3.2 What each batch puts in

| Source | Where it goes | Load | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **flax retting in water** (a river or brook margin, a lake edge, a fen pool; U4's retting pond on a water parcel) | that water parcel | **1 FU a kg of straw** | each night the lot lies in (1 night) |
| flax retting in a **retting pit** (U7's work by water) | nothing to the river | | (the pit's own water; Gaps: a pit let out into the river) |
| flax retting **on grass** (dew, 3 nights) | nothing | | |
| **the lime pit emptied** (scrape and lay) | the pits' `below` | **3 FU a hide unit** | the night of the act |
| **the tan pits' seep** | `below` | **0.1 FU a hide unit** in the pits | each night hides lie in them |
| **strengthening** (old liquor drawn off) | `below` | **0.5 FU a hide unit** | the night of the act |
| **a lift** (the spent liquor let out) | `below` | **3 FU a hide unit** | the night of the act |

A light hide unit puts in 3 + 1 + 1.6 + 3 = **8.6 FU over its tanning**, a heavy one 10.2 [e: real tannery waste runs about 60 to
120 kg of oxygen demand a tonne of raw hide, 0.4 to 0.7 kg a 6 kg hide, with lime and tannin's toxicity on top; counted as twice the
oxygen demand]. A household's flax batch of 10 to 50 kg is 10 to 50 FU in one night; a supplier retting a whole plot's 400 kg in the
river, 400.

### 3.3 How it fades and how far it carries (the night step)

Run each night after U4's step has added tonight's loads and before U1's wild step reads the stocks (`engine/fouling.py`,
`night.py`):

1. **Fade:** F <- F x keep. **keep: 0.5 a night in flowing water** (a brook, a river, a large river: the flow flushes it), **0.7 in a fen
   or a lake** [e]. A retting pulse is a third of its first night's load by the third night: hunt_fish_materials §7.5's "about 3
   nights", and CO3's lore.
2. **Add** tonight's loads (§3.2).
3. **Carry downstream** in upstream-to-downstream order (`wild._upstream`, deterministic): each water parcel passes **d x F** to its
   `land.down` parcel (`landgen/write.py:86-105`), **d = 0.5 on a brook, 0.3 on a river, 0.15 on a large river** (dilution by the flow;
   the river's class, `land.river` and `wild.water_kind`), and from a fen or a lake parcel with an outflow 0.1. A lake mixes: each of
   its parcels passes 0.2 x F, split equally, to the body's other parcels ([[D2-229 One fish stock per water body|D2-229]]: one body). Carrying stops where the carried load is
   under 1 FU. At `out` (the water leaves the village), the load passes, at the same d, to **the next reach downstream**: the wild
   layer's areas' `down` (`wild/areas.py:112-129`) gives the next reach body (`reach:{node}` or `reach:{area}`, `wild/areas.py:156`),
   and its most upstream water parcel takes it.

**How far, in parcels** [m, the rule above, flowing water]:

| Load at the source | Brook | River | Large river |
|---|---|---|---|
| a household's 10 kg batch | the parcel and 3 below (10, 5, 2.5, 1.3) | the parcel and 1 below (10, 3) | the parcel and 1 below (10, 1.5) |
| a household's 50 kg | 5 below | 3 below (50, 15, 4.5, 1.4) | 2 below |
| a lift or a lime-pit flush of 15 hide units (45 FU) | 5 below | 3 below | 2 below |
| a supplier's whole plot, 400 kg | 8 below, past most villages' own water into the next reach | 4 below | 3 below |

### 3.4 What it harms, and by how much

Each water parcel's **fouling share s = min(0.8, F / M)**, where **M** is the load that fouls that parcel's margin through: **brook 30,
river 100, large river 300, fen or still water 20, lake 200 FU** [e: a household's batch should take a brook's edge, nick a river's and
barely touch a large river's; I do not know the real figures, and these calibrate]. U1's night reads s:

| What | Harm | Threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **the shallows** on that parcel (U1's stock) | lose the share s of their fish that night | any s |
| **fish held in set gear** on that parcel (traps, a keep box; U2) | die in the gear: the catch is dead fish from that night, spoiling as fresh fish; the tend says so | s >= 0.2 |
| **the resident fish** of the body ([[D2-229 One fish stock per water body|D2-229]]) | lose s x 0.5 x (1 / the body's water parcels) of its stock: the fish in that stretch suffocate | s >= 0.5 |
| **a run passing** (U1) | does not pass that parcel tonight: the night's passing is held over to the next night (as a frozen night's is held, [[D2-265 The river's signs|D2-265]]'s detail in U1); traps and weirs beyond it take nothing of the run that night | s >= 0.5 |
| herons and otters there | follow the stock as now (`waters.to_wild`) | |
| people and beasts | **nothing now**: no drinking mechanic exists; disease and sanitation wait with the middens ([[D2-281 Tanning comes in this build, with bark as a new good|D2-281]]; Gaps) | |

**Worked numbers** [m, scratch `u3/calc2.py`; the shallows left after the three nights of a pulse, before regrowth]:

| Batch | Brook | River | Large river | Fen | Lake |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 kg of flax (a household's strip) | a third killed the first night; 0.51 left | a tenth; 0.83 | 3%; 0.94 | half; 0.25 | 5%; 0.89 |
| 50 kg | 80%; 0.02 left, and the run held that night | half, the run held; 0.33 | a sixth; 0.73 | nearly all | a quarter; 0.54 |
| 200 kg | the stretch dead for nights | 80%; 0.02 | two thirds, the run held; 0.19 | | 80%; 0.03 |
| the pits' seep, 15 hide units steady | 10% a night (F settles at 3) | 3% a night | 1% a night | | |
| a lift of 15 hide units (45 FU) | 80% that night, the run held, fish dead in traps | 45% | 15% | | |

**What it means for people:** a household that rets its strip in the brook by its house takes half the brook's edge fish for a week
and kills what its neighbours' traps hold that night; retted in the river, a fishing village hardly notices one household and
notices a flax supplier for miles; retted on the grass or in a pit, it fouls nothing (the capital or the wait that removes the harm,
hunt_fish_materials §4.2). A tanner whose pits drain into a brook keeps it nearly fishless below; sited at the village's downstream
edge, or on the big river, the harm falls on the next village or almost nowhere: **siting is the tanner's choice and the fishers'
grievance** (hunt_fish_materials §7.5 "siting at the downstream edge"). The shallows come back at U1's regrowth once the fouling
stops.

### 3.5 Signs (guides/senses.md)

A bundle a source, each a change worth acting on, by the one noticing rule ([[D4-53 Every sign and every event is noticed by the one noticing rule|D4-53]], [[D4-75 Every perceived line about the world comes from the one noticing rule|D4-75]]). The `own` values are first values set by
analogy with the slaughter bundle's calibrated rows (the guide's table); **each is to be checked through `perceive.chance_of` for the
typical cases before the build, as the guide's checklist says** (not run for this spec).

| Sign | Sense; kind; scope | When (`when`) | Own, reach | Intended: on it / next door / two steps (clear) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "a sour, rotten stink off the water at {parcel}: something is retting in it" | smell; nearby; others | a retting lot in water there tonight (`fouling_from.flax`) | 2.0, around | 1.0 / 0.6 / 0.24 |
| "bundles of flax weighted down with stones in the shallows of {parcel}" | sight; nearby; others | the same | 1.0, near | certain on the parcel |
| "dead fish turning belly-up along the edge of {parcel}, the water dark and still" | sight; nearby, and so news downstream | s >= 0.1 at that parcel last night | 1.2, near | near certain on it; fog 0.3 |
| "dead fish rotting in the reeds along {parcel}" | sight; lingering (the next day, `x.age` 1) | as above, yesterday | 0.8, near | about 0.8 on it |
| "small fish gasping at the surface along {parcel}" | sight; the fisher's knack (an observation, [[D2-269 Experts' signs are observations of what is happening now, which may…|D2-269]]) | 0.05 <= s < 0.1 | 0.6, near; knack fishers | about 0.5 for a fisher there, rare for others |
| "a brown, frothing flush in the water below the tan pits at {parcel}, and a stink of lime and rot" | sight and smell; nearby | a lime-pit emptying or a lift of 5 FU or more tonight | 1.5, around | 1.0 / 0.5 / 0 |
| "the reek of the tan pits carries from {parcel}" | smell; far | the day a stage turns (not the standing pits: a standing stink is ambience, guide §2) | 2.0, around | 1.0 / 0.6 / 0.24 |
| (to the tender, U2's result) "Of the eels in T7, 4 are dead and soft: the water here stinks." | the result line, not a sign | fish died in the gear | | |
| "your hands are raw and stink of lime and the pits" | touch; to the doer | the day one limed, laid or lifted | 2.0, doer | certain for the doer |

The dead fish sign is what carries the harm to the people it falls on: a village below sees dead fish arrive with no cause in sight,
and must walk upstream or ask to learn why (news by presence, guide §1). None of these says who to blame or what to do ([[D2-269 Experts' signs are observations of what is happening now, which may…|D2-269]]).

### 3.6 What the book and the people can do about it

Nothing new in the engine: the flax grower may ret on grass (3 nights, no harm), dig a retting pit (U7), or ret in the river; the
tanner may site the pits where the water goes. The fishers' answer is social (an agreement, a demand, a payment, a custom) or a
counter-move (moving their traps up). The book's rules (U6) can say where a household rets ("ret on Brook Meadow"); a binding custom
or law against retting in the river is a later institution (hunt_fish_materials §8.3).

## 5. Tests

**Unit tests:**
- `peel bark` refused outside year days 4 to 9 with its line; inside, 1.5 bundles a unit with a crude tool; it draws 85 kg of young
  growth a bundle and leaves 7.5 bundles of dead wood; where timber was felled today it takes 1 bark a 4 timber first.
- Liming refused with no hearth of the household burning in the last 8 nights.
- A lot of 6 deer: limed 2 nights; laid with 6 bark; strengthened at nights 5 and 11; lifted at 16; curried with 0.6 tallow to 18
  leather. An overdue strengthening pauses the lot and rots 2% a night. A heavy hide refused before the capability point. A failure at
  capable loses a fifth, shown at the lift with a lesson.
- The bellows worn after 4 world years: the anvil works at half until re-leathered.
- `fouling.night` on a toy river of five brook parcels and one lake: a 50 kg batch gives 50, 25, 12.5, 6.3, 3.1 down the brook; fades
  by half a night; a lake mixes; carrying stops under 1 FU; `out` passes to the next reach's first parcel. Replay gives the same state.
- `share`: s = min(0.8, F / M) by water kind; the shallows' loss, fish dead in traps at 0.2, the body's loss and the run held at 0.5.
- The shoes term only with `shoes` on.
- Each sign row's `own` through `perceive.chance_of` for on the parcel, next door and two steps, clear, fog and night, against §3.5's
  intended column.

**Bot interface tests** ([[D2-298 Bot runs test interfaces and basic functioning only|D2-298]]: interfaces only): a bot peels bark in season and is refused out of it; limes, lays, strengthens,
lifts and curries a lot; makes a frame and harness; rets flax in a brook, on grass and in a pit; another bot tends traps below. Read:
the result lines, fouling present below the brook retting and absent for grass and pit, the dead-fish sign rows firing, no crash, the

**What live runs should read** (with the rest of the wave, [[D2-297 The catch stays as calibrated|D2-297]]; against the null arm and a qualitative end-state reading,

carriers; whether fishers buy bark for their nets; whether anyone rets in the water, where, and whether the households below notice,
complain, bargain or move; whether a tanner sites the pits with the water below in mind.

## 8. Open questions for Zach

**Whether shoes come in this build with an effect on the cold out of doors, since they are three quarters of leather's demand
(W2-7).** [[D2-10 Clothing is parked|D2-10]] parks clothing and lets shelter carry the cold; without shoes a village wears 2 to 4 kg of leather a year and the tanner
is a small by-trade; with them about 13 kg and a regional chokepoint. Designed (§2.7) behind a world setting, off by default: an
unshod person out of doors on a frost day eats 0.25 food more, shoes last 12 days worn and are resoled. Options: (a) shoes on, which
lifts [[D2-10 Clothing is parked|D2-10]] for footwear only; (b) shoes later with clothing, tanning living on frames, harness, bellows and barked nets; (c) shoes
with no effect (a demand that does nothing). **Recommend (a).** Blocks: the tanner's scale and the shoes setting's default.

**Whether a tanning lot that fails loses a share of its hides, drawn when laid and shown when lifted, rather than all at once (W2-8).**
Default taken: a half at aware, a third at rough, a fifth at capable, none at known. Options: (a) this; (b) all or nothing, as today's
make; (c) no failure, skill acting on the leather's yield only. **Recommend (a).** Blocks: the tanning rows and U4's failure for long
processes.

**Whether tanning's lime is wood ash from a household's hearth, folded in, until lime burning comes (W2-10).** Default taken: ash as a
condition of liming, no good. Options: (a) this; (b) an ash good from burning (it would bind as an artefact of the compressed year:
a hearth makes about a twelfth of its real year's ash per world year); (c) a lime good from a kiln now. **Recommend (a).** Blocks:
the lime row only.

**Whether the fouling numbers (§3.2 to §3.4) are accepted as first values to read in live runs, as the catch is (new).** They are my
estimates: a household's flax batch takes a third to most of a brook's edge fish and a tenth of a river's; a tanner's pits keep a brook
below them nearly fishless and nick a river; a supplier's whole plot retted in the river kills it for miles. Options: (a) accept as
defaults and calibrate in live runs with the rest of the wave ([[D2-297 The catch stays as calibrated|D2-297]]); (b) milder (the household batch barely felt, only a supplier's
retting harming); (c) harsher. **Recommend (a).** Blocks: the fouling rows and the signs' thresholds.

**Whether barking nets comes in this build (W2-16).** Designed in U3 §3.4: 0.5 bark and 0.25 firewood a net, a world year at the barked
rate; it halves a fishing household's making (U3 §6) and gives the fishers a reason to buy from the peeler. **Recommend now.** Blocks:
the net's rot and bark's second buyer.
